Overnight, in two lines

Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate, flipping seven Senate seats including Kansas, Texas, Iowa and Ohio, and five governorships, among them Texas, where the winner campaigned on a moratorium on new data centres. From January, Trump faces a Congress run by the opposition for his final two years.

Markets lurched and then recovered: Nasdaq futures fell more than 2% on fears of tougher AI and data-centre scrutiny before turning slightly positive, while the dollar and yields fell. AMD's strong forecast had already eased AI-spending worries, Anthropic open-sourced a system to prove which model produced an answer, and Iran called the latest US response "not convincing."

US politics

Democrats take the House and Senate, flipping seats deep in Republican territory

AP · NPR · NBC News · Nov 3–4
Senate: +7 D flipsHouse: ~258 D (NBC est.)5 governorships flippedTrump disapproval 62% (exit poll)

What happened. The AP called control of both chambers for Democrats overnight, unusually quickly; in recent cycles, control took days to settle. In the Senate, Democrats flipped seven Republican-held seats: Kansas (which the AP notes hasn't elected a Democratic senator in nearly a century), Iowa, Texas, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina and South Carolina, and held Georgia and Michigan. Republicans had started the year with a 53–47 majority that, NPR notes, few expected to be at risk. In the House, Democrats flipped more than two dozen districts, the AP reports, including some Trump won by double digits in 2024; NBC estimates they will end with at least 258 seats, a figure it says could move by a few. NPR highlights at least three Florida districts that the Republican legislature had redrawn, and all of Pennsylvania's competitive races.

Democrats also flipped governorships in Texas, Georgia, Nevada, Ohio and Iowa (NBC). In Texas, the AP projects Gina Hinojosa unseated Gov. Greg Abbott after campaigning on a moratorium on new data-centre permits until the legislature sets rules on water, power and tax incentives.

How it is being read. The AP calls the result "a widespread repudiation" of Trump and the Republican Party. NBC's exit poll found Trump's disapproval at 62%, more than three-quarters disapproving of Congress, and nearly half of voters saying they saw the election as a chance to rebuke the president. NPR says cost of living and perceived corruption defined the campaign. The war with Iran, which has pushed fuel prices to records, was also an issue: Trump said on Monday he hoped it would not affect voters. Detailed Republican responses were limited in the early coverage reviewed; Trump keeps his veto, so Democrats cannot pass legislation over his objection without Republican votes.

Why it matters (analysis)Divided government from January means congressional investigations in both chambers, a harder path for tax cuts and spending, and confirmation fights in the Senate. Markets reacted on two tracks: bonds rallied on expectations of less fiscal expansion, while tech fell first on the prospect of tougher oversight of AI and data centres. Analysts at Saxo, BNY and Morgan Stanley caution that a federal moratorium is unlikely with a presidential veto; the more concrete constraints are at state level, which is why the Texas result stands out. Tariff authority remains largely with the White House.

Chips · AI infrastructure

AMD's forecast pushes back on AI-spending fears, hours before the vote added a new kind of risk

CNBC · Reuters via StreetInsider · Reuters via Yahoo Finance · Nov 3–4
Q3 revenue $13.38B · +45%Data centre $8.55B · +97%2027 growth >80%Stock +8% after hours

What happened. AMD beat estimates with third-quarter revenue of $13.38 billion (vs. $13.09 billion, LSEG) and adjusted EPS of $2.09 (vs. $1.95). Data-centre revenue nearly doubled to a record $8.55 billion and is now 64% of sales, up from 47% a year ago. It guided fourth-quarter revenue to about $17 billion, well above the $16.2 billion consensus, and CEO Lisa Su told analysts she expects total revenue to rise more than 80% in 2027, with server revenue doubling and data-centre chips making up 80% of sales. Its Helios rack, pairing MI455X accelerators with "Venice" CPUs, is in production, with initial systems in testing at Meta (Reuters). The weak spots: PC and gaming revenue fell 8%, gaming alone 40%, and AMD expects PC shipments to drop by a double-digit percentage next year as memory shortages bite.

The results came after three straight down days for AMD in a sector-wide sell-off over AI overspending. Reuters described them as "a strong counterpoint" to fears the boom is losing steam; Nvidia, Broadcom and Intel rose after hours. Su said AMD has secured wafers, substrates, packaging and memory for "much higher growth next year."

Separately, the New York Times reported that Anthropic aims to double its compute to more than 7 GW next year from over 3.5 GW by end-2026, with AMD among the suppliers (up to 2 GW).

Why it matters (analysis)Demand signals from chipmakers and their customers remain very strong; the open question has shifted from whether buyers want capacity to whether it can be powered, permitted and financed. Tuesday's split screen shows both: a supplier raising its outlook, and voters in the largest data-centre state electing a governor who promised to pause new projects. How quickly state-level policy changes, rather than Congress, is likely to matter more for build-out timelines.

Anthropic open-sources FlashProve, a cryptographic proof of which model produced an answer

zkSNARK-based>100 tokens/s on one H100~19 MB proof per 1,024 tokensApache 2.0

Anthropic released FlashProve, a research prototype for "provable inference": a short zero-knowledge proof that a given response came from a specified model, checkable without access to the weights and without rerunning the model. On Llama 4 Maverick (400B parameters, 4-bit), one H100 generated proofs at more than 100 tokens per second, with each 1,024-token request yielding a proof of about 19 MB; GPU time was 70–95 times what vLLM needs to process the same prompt. The release includes code, a Lean formalisation and a protocol description. TechCrunch notes it is designed to hold even if the lab running the model is the one cheating, for example by quietly swapping in a cheaper model.

Anthropic calls this intermediate progress, not a product. If the overhead falls, verifiable inference could underpin model audits, regulatory evaluations and contracts between agents that need to trust which model is on the other side.

OpenAI says ChatGPT search equals a fifth of Google's; Google makes schools choose between AI and Search

1.2B weekly users~20% of Google volume (OpenAI est.)K-12 AI off by default

OpenAI consumer lead Adam Fry said ChatGPT's search-like queries now equal about 20% of Google's search volume, with 1.2 billion weekly users and 250 million weekly voice users; that is OpenAI's own estimate using its own definition of search, not an audited comparison. Google, meanwhile, said Gemini, Gemini Notebook and Google Search will be off by default for K-12 student accounts until administrators enable them. Because AI is now built into Search, schools that keep AI off also lose Search for students, Google's education VP told the New York Times. Critics framed that as forcing an AI-enabled experience on schools; Google pairs it with a "Guided Learning" mode that walks students through problems.

Both items point the same way: AI answers and web search are merging into one product, which changes how traffic and attention flow to publishers, merchants and schools.

South China Morning Post · Nov 4

Tencent releases Hy4, an open-weight model with vision and agent features for enterprise work

Tencent launched Hy4, its first flagship model with visual understanding (images, charts, visual maths), and used it to overhaul its workplace agent tool WorkBuddy. Tencent called it "one of the strongest open-weight models available today," while acknowledging it still trails top closed models on research-level maths, long-horizon coding and self-improvement benchmarks. It joins recent open-weight releases from DeepSeek, Z.ai and Alibaba.

Chinese labs keep competing mainly on open weights, which lowers costs for companies building agents on their own infrastructure; the gap to closed frontier models is narrower on everyday tasks than on the hardest ones.

CrowdStrike · The Neuron · Gizmodo via The Neuron · Nov 3–4

Agent security: CrowdStrike buys Dreadnode, Apple flags rogue-agent risk, OpenAI hires a contested red team

CrowdStrike agreed to acquire Dreadnode, which runs more than 100 automated attack strategies (jailbreaks, prompt injection, multi-turn and agent-specific attacks) against models, agent loops and MCP servers; terms weren't disclosed beyond "predominantly cash." Apple's annual filing warns that autonomous agents could exceed permissions, make unauthorised transactions or conceal actions, as it opens Siri's in-app actions to developers. And OpenAI announced a security partnership with Cathedral Systems, founded by former DOGE staff, which previously accessed parts of Anthropic's internal systems in a bug-bounty test; Anthropic disputes how the test was run and says weights and Claude conversations were unaffected (Gizmodo, via The Neuron).

As agents gain the ability to act and pay, testing them before deployment is becoming its own security category, and large vendors are now naming the risk in legal filings.

Shopify falls 8.7% on a slight GMV miss, as it bets on the first agent-driven holiday season

Revenue $3.74B · +32%GMV $119.15BFCF margin 22%

Shopify's revenue of $3.74 billion came in just under the $3.75 billion LSEG estimate, and gross merchandise volume of $119.15 billion missed the $120.03 billion StreetAccount figure; subscription revenue and gross profit also missed, while merchant revenue and adjusted EPS (49 cents vs. 46) beat. It guided fourth-quarter growth in the "low thirties." President Harley Finkelstein called it the 13th straight quarter of GMV growth above 20%. Shopify was an early partner for Meta's Muse agent, which can browse and buy from Shopify stores and check out with Shop Pay. Asked whether AI agents comparing prices are pushing them down, Finkelstein said "we're not seeing that prices are being lowered," and that agents are sending new traffic to smaller brands.

The sell-off reflects how fully priced the stock was rather than a weak quarter. Finkelstein's claim that agents are not compressing prices is the company's view; there is little independent data yet, and this holiday season will be the first real test.

A round trip on the election: tech sells off, then recovers, while bonds are the clear winner

Infobae · Pulso Económico · Río Negro · Nov 3–4

Country risk hits 831 as Brazil's currency slide spills into the peso

Dollar bonds fell as much as 1.5% and JP Morgan's country-risk index closed at 831 basis points, its highest since October 2025 and roughly double the 400 at the end of July (Infobae). Stocks rose: the Merval gained 1.6% in pesos. The new pressure is on the currency, which had been calm through the bond sell-off: the wholesale dollar topped ARS 1,550 for the first time, which Infobae links to the Brazilian real's renewed slide to 5.49 per dollar after Lula's re-election, plus usual start-of-month demand. The central bank did not buy dollars. Economy Minister Luis Caputo told the UIA industrial conference that "there will be more and more dollars" and rejected a devaluation as "impoverishing wage earners."

Monday's sell-off was partly attributed to fears that a Democratic Congress could weaken US financial support for Argentina; that scenario has now happened, so today's open is the first read on how much was already priced. January's roughly US$4.5 billion in bond payments remains the next hard test.
