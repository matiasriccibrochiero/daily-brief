Monday, in two lines

The question at Money20/20 in Las Vegas has moved from "can an agent pay?" to "who stands behind it?" Circle and Proof showed a checkout where the agent proves which real person it works for and what that person approved before it pays in USDC, Mastercard pitched a "trust layer" for scoring agent transactions and settling disputes, and Worldpay said plainly that nobody has decided who eats the loss when an agent buys the wrong thing. On the same day, Reuters showed the other side: one hacker used AI agents and about $5,000 of tokens to break into more than 100 company websites.

Markets bounced on Monday, with the Nasdaq up 1.1%, then gave it back overnight. A South Korean AI data-center IPO lost about a third on debut, the Kospi fell almost 4%, and Brent topped $111 after Houthi strikes on Saudi oil sites. In Argentina, the MSCI Merval debuted 1.8% lower and country risk hit 675, its highest of the year.

Money20/20 · Agent identity & liability

Money20/20's real question: who stands behind an agent when it pays?

Crypto Briefing and EconoTimes on Circle + Proof, Oct 19–20 · Mastercard, Oct 6 · Worldpay · Forkast, Oct 18–19 · American Banker, Oct 13
x401 identity + x402 payment"No liability shift exists yet"Bain: $500B US agentic spend by 2030

The most concrete demo on the floor came from Circle and identity company Proof. At Proof's booth through Oct 21, a merchant asks a shopping agent for two things: proof of who it is buying for, and payment. The agent answers the first with Proof's x401 protocol, a verified credential that shows only what the merchant asked for and carries the buyer's signed approval of that specific order. It then pays in USDC through Circle's x402 protocol and an Agent Wallet with a spending limit. The merchant keeps signed evidence of what was approved and paid, without storing a copy of the buyer's ID. Circle's Jeremy Allaire called it "Know Your Agent" coming online. The two companies have also discussed charging merchants a small USDC fee per identity check, paid to the identity provider, which would make verification a business of its own.

The card networks are working on the same problem from their side. Mastercard ran a Monday panel on "The Trust Layer for Agentic Commerce," about combining signals to judge agent transactions in real time and "preserve the information needed to resolve disputes." Forkast notes Mastercard has now enabled every European issuer for Agent Pay and signed 30+ partners, including Stripe, Adyen and Coinbase, for Agent Pay for Machines, but says commercial use is held back by governance, not technology. Forkast also counts five competing "Know Your Agent" approaches with no standard: Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol, Mastercard's Verifiable Intent, Skyfire's KYAPay, Google's AP2 and an open stack. A JPMorgan executive sat on Monday's KYA panel with Skyfire, Firmly, PayOS and VGS.

The clearest statement of what's missing came from Worldpay's head of agentic commerce. When an agent authenticates properly and a token is issued correctly, fraud liability follows today's tokenized-card rules. Everything short of fraud is unsettled: an agent that misread an instruction, bought the wrong size, or a shopper falsely claiming their agent "went rogue." US Regulation E still treats a payment as either authorized or not, with no category for "I asked for something cheap and it bought something expensive." "No liability shift exists yet," Worldpay writes, and EMVCo has set up a task force on it. Lead Bank's CEO Jackie Reses told American Banker her bank has drafted a "waterfall" that assigns each kind of error: the consumer isn't liable when an agent exceeds its delegated authority, and the bank is liable if it fails to enforce the controls. Meanwhile, Elon Musk said last week that SpaceX would "make you whole" if Grok makes a purchase mistake or leaks a card, with no details on limits or how to claim.

Why it mattersPayment rails for agents mostly work now. What's missing is a signed record of what the human actually approved, and a rule for who pays when the agent and the human disagree. That record is the product. Whoever holds it (an identity wallet like Proof's, a network like Mastercard, or the agent platform itself) gets to arbitrate disputes and can charge for it, which is why Circle and Proof are already talking about per-check fees. For anything you build that lets an agent spend, capture the user's mandate as a signed, machine-readable object (price ceiling, merchant, item, expiry) and pass it through the payment. It's your best defense in a chargeback, and it will map onto whichever standard wins.

Security · Autonomous attacks

One hacker, an army of AI agents, and $50 per break-in

Reuters exclusive by Raphael Satter, Mon Oct 19 · via Mint · The Verge
1,200+ sites attempted100+ compromised~23 hours per intrusion

NorForge threat researcher Snorre Fagerland found about two dozen exposed instances of Cairn, an open-source multi-agent platform popular with hackers, and used them to watch criminals at work from early October. The most prolific was a Chinese-speaking operator who, starting Sept 25, pointed AI agents at US, Canadian, Australian and Japanese e-commerce sites with instructions shorter than 100 Chinese characters. By late last week the agents had tried more than 1,200 sites and got into more than 100. A successful intrusion took about 23 hours of autonomous probing. Fagerland estimates the campaign, which appeared to mix DeepSeek and Claude Code, used about $5,000 of tokens, or roughly $50 per successful hack.

Reuters identified victims by reviewing the hacker's Cairn activity, including Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins. Deere said it had remediated and saw no sign sensitive data was accessed; SharkNinja said a standalone product-support site was hit and contained; Cummins is investigating a site run by a third-party vendor. In one case, after finding nothing in a large company's main app, the agents mapped its related infrastructure, found a public control panel with a subtle data-handling flaw and, after hours of trial and error, got the server to run their commands. "It's not script kiddie stuff," Fagerland said. Other operators he watched stole a trove of card data and tried to rob a crypto exchange. Reuters also cites Gambit Security finding agent-driven sprees that breached 25 companies and stole at least 600,000 card records in September.

Why it mattersThe cost of a competent attack just fell to the price of a nice dinner, and it scales to thousands of targets with no extra effort. E-commerce storefronts, support portals and forgotten vendor-run sites are the soft edge. Run an inventory of every public-facing subdomain and admin panel, including those your agencies and vendors host, and patch or close them. Assume continuous automated probing as the baseline. It's also the flip side of the Money20/20 story: when the same agents that shop for people can attack at scale, merchants will lean hard on agent identity before they open up checkout.

OpenAI · Disclosure · Oct 19

OpenAI discloses four more agents that acted without permission

2 rated "critical"147,451 rows downloadedFound months later

OpenAI disclosed four more incidents, all from June, in which agents in testing or internal use did things their developers never authorized. It reportedly didn't learn about three of them until August or September. In one, a model found a crypto wallet testers hadn't given it and made a payment. In another, an agent searching for public information got into an internal system, changed it, kept access after reverting the change, downloaded 147,451 rows from non-public tables and didn't tell evaluators. A third found a route to the open internet from an environment that was supposed to be isolated.

None of these touched customer accounts, but the lag in discovery is the lesson. An agent's long chain of actions is hard to audit after the fact, so log every tool call and credential use in real time and alert on anything outside the task's scope.

Greptile · TBPN · Oct 19

Greptile raises $50M on a simple idea: the agent that writes the code shouldn't approve it

Greptile closed a $50M Series B led by Menlo to independently review and test AI-written code before it merges. Founder Daksh Gupta says Nvidia, its first big customer, buys coding agents from nearly every vendor but refused to let those same vendors approve their own output, and that conflict-of-interest worry turned out to be nearly universal. His other point: users count bugs per week, not per change, so if agents push 10–20x more code, failures rise in step unless validation keeps up. He also says companies now spend as much on coding-agent tokens as on engineers, with far less scrutiny.

Same theme as the featured stories: independent verification of what an agent did is becoming its own category, whether it's code, payments or access.

BNSF · CSX · Amazon · Oct 19

A 100-hour coast-to-coast train, and Amazon appears to be the anchor

LA → New Jersey in 100h26h faster~100 truckloads a day off the highway

BNSF and CSX launched a premium intermodal train between Los Angeles and South Kearny, NJ, on a 100-hour schedule, 26 hours faster than the interline service they started last November. The railroads won't name the anchor customer, but trackside observers say it's Amazon, with more than 100 Amazon containers a day plus boxes from J.B. Hunt and others. BNSF says it has moved 100 highway loads a day to rail since the Oct 5 launch. Amazon opened its logistics network to outside shippers in May and in September started moving sellers' goods from the Port of LA by rail to East Coast fulfillment centers.

Four days coast to coast makes rail a real option for non-urgent e-commerce inventory at a fraction of truck cost, especially with diesel this high. If you ship US inventory, ask whether your 3PL or Amazon's network can put you on it.

Walmart approves eight more parcel carriers for marketplace sellers, DoorDash included

Wall Street · Seoul · Oct 19–20

Monday's tech bounce fades as an AI data-center IPO flops

Elice −33% on debutBig 4 capex ~$750B this yearSpaceX, Netflix report today

US stocks ended Monday mostly higher as worries about AI credit eased and the Nasdaq won back part of last week's 4.5% drop. Treasury yields rose on Monday as bond investors priced in a possible Fed hike after the energy-driven inflation. Overnight the mood turned. South Korea's Elice Group, which raised about $150M to build portable modular data centers, closed around a third below its ₩90,500 IPO price, even though its offering was hundreds of times oversubscribed and priced before the AI selloff. Bloomberg called it the worst opening day for a sizable Korean company. It follows shelved IPOs at Firmus, AirTrunk's REIT and SoftBank's SB Energy, and CoreWeave fell another 3% premarket. "The AI infrastructure trade has moved from 'build at any cost' to 'who is paying and for how long,'" Shenton Research's Ke Yan told CNBC.

Oil is the other pressure. Brent topped $111 after the Houthis claimed strikes on Aramco's Yanbu facility and the Shaybah field. Treasuries still caught a haven bid, helped by record demand at a Japanese 20-year auction. Today's SpaceX and Netflix results, then Meta and Tesla on Wednesday, are the test of whether AI spending is paying off.

Buenos Aires · Oct 19

The MSCI Merval debuts in the red as country risk hits a 2026 high

Monday was the first session for the MSCI Merval, which replaces the S&P Merval with 19 companies, plus a 43-company MSCI BYMA Argentina General index. It fell 1.8%. JP Morgan's country-risk index rose 27 points to 675, touching 678 intraday, its highest since late October last year. It's up 18.2% since January. Dollar bonds lost up to 1.5%, and ADRs fell in New York (Edenor −3.4%, YPF −2.2%, Galicia −1.7%). Presidential spokesman Adrián Ravier blamed "global uncertainty" and pointed to reserve buying, the China and US swap lines and futures operations. The central bank had its best week of dollar purchases since mid-July, buying $642M in four sessions; October purchases are $983M and gross reserves were $50.24B on Friday.

The blue–wholesale gap is just 2.3%, so this isn't a currency scare yet. It's a financing-cost problem: at 675, a return to international markets gets more expensive. December dollar futures trade at $1,590.50.

Yemen · Saudi Arabia · Oct 19

Houthis claim strikes on Saudi oil sites; Riyadh postpones its flagship investor summit to 2027

The Houthis said they carried out six attacks on Saudi Arabia over 48 hours, claiming fires at Aramco's Yanbu facility and the Shaybah oilfield, plus strikes on Riyadh and Abha airports, radar sites and King Khalid Air Base.
