Big Tech · Earnings

Microsoft and Alphabet both spend heavily on AI, but only one is still generating cash

CNBC · Reuters · FXStreet · Bloomberg · Oct 28–29
MSFT capex $45.2BGOOGL capex $51.36BGOOGL FCF −$9.07BPre-market moves

Two of the biggest buyers of AI infrastructure reported on Wednesday night, and the market treated them very differently. Microsoft reported $45.2 billion in capital spending and finance leases for its fiscal first quarter, 30% more than a year earlier but below the $50.67 billion analysts expected. Free cash flow fell 27% to $18.63 billion, which Reuters says was nearly $10 billion above Wall Street's estimate. Azure revenue grew 48% to $33.17 billion (FXStreet), and the company guided to 49–50% growth next quarter. CEO Satya Nadella said "the shiny object stuff is waning" as more customers sign long-term contracts.

Alphabet posted its first combined revenue and earnings miss in nearly four years, per CNBC and LSEG: adjusted EPS of $2.67 against $3.02 expected and revenue of $127.58 billion against $128.22 billion, still up 25% from a year earlier. Capex was $51.36 billion, below the $55.09 billion estimate, but free cash flow was negative $9.07 billion, far worse than the −$2.70 billion analysts expected and its second negative quarter in a row. Google Cloud revenue more than doubled to $30.71 billion. Search revenue grew 12%, short of the 14.7% expected, even though CEO Sundar Pichai said query growth was the fastest in five years. Alphabet kept its 2026 capex guidance of $195–205 billion, and CFO Anat Ashkenazi said spending will "increase significantly" in 2027; CNBC reports expectations of more than $300 billion.

The Oracle contrast. At its investor day Oracle said it would limit new capital-markets financing through fiscal 2030 to $20 billion ($10 billion of equity and $10 billion of gross debt, excluding refinancing), turn free cash flow positive in the second half of fiscal 2028 and exceed $30 billion of free cash flow in fiscal 2029, when capex is expected to fall. The stock ended regular trading nearly 11% higher (Reuters). That follows Meta's reaction last week, when its cash burn overshadowed better-than-expected revenue.

The two readings. Quilter Cheviot's Ben Barringer said Microsoft is "building out its architecture in a measured way," while Alphabet had "a fairly messy set of results and a poorly handled earnings call." Check Capital, an Alphabet shareholder, argued the other way: AI is "a sport of kings," and companies that can't spend $150 billion next year shouldn't "even bother showing up." Nasdaq-100 futures were down more than 1% early Thursday. Premarket prices can change by the close; CNBC notes a close below −9.77% would be Alphabet's worst post-earnings day since its 2004 listing.

Why it matters (analysis)For most of the AI build-out, investors rewarded companies for securing compute at almost any cost. This week's reactions, from Meta through Oracle to Alphabet, suggest the test has shifted to whether AI revenue can fund the next round of spending. That matters beyond the stocks: if self-funding becomes the bar, the heaviest spenders lean harder on debt, equity and supplier financing, and the pace of data-centre, chip and power orders becomes more sensitive to each quarter's cash flow. Amazon reports tonight.

Central banks · US

The Fed holds, but two dissenters wanted a hike and markets now expect one in December

Federal Reserve · CNBC · Reuters · Oct 28
Range 3.75%–4.00%Vote 10–2Dec hike odds 85% (CME)10Y ~5.04%

The FOMC kept the federal funds range at 3.75%–4.00%, where it has been since its September hike, the first since 2023. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favour of a quarter-point increase. The statement upgraded the Fed's view of the economy ("expanding at a strong pace," with "robust" capital investment) and kept the line that "inflation remains elevated," adding: "The Committee will deliver price stability."

What Warsh said. At his press conference, Chair Kevin Warsh, five months into the job, said first-half GDP growth was revised up to 2.4% and third-quarter growth was likely running above 3%, led by consumer spending and roughly double-digit growth in business investment. He noted that inflation has run above target "for close to 6 years" and that energy prices "have moved higher again in recent weeks." He described financial conditions as tighter but said he was "unwilling and unable to call them tight." He also announced that the Fed will hold fewer policy meetings from next year, with one 2027 meeting devoted only to strategy, with no rate vote (CNBC, Reuters).

Market reaction. Stocks finished slightly higher. The curve steepened: the two-year yield fell about 4 basis points to 4.7% while the 10-year rose to about 5.04% and the 30-year to about 5.37% (Reuters). CME FedWatch put the odds of a December hike at 85%, up from 78% on Tuesday. Reuters notes Cook is also the governor President Trump is trying to remove.

Why it matters (analysis)A hold with two hawkish dissents and an upgraded growth outlook reads as a pause within a tightening cycle, not the end of one. With oil above $100 because of the Iran war and the 10-year yield above 5%, borrowing costs for mortgages, corporate debt and the AI build-out are staying high, and the ECB and Bank of Japan meet over the next two days against the same energy backdrop.

Google opens Gemini and Search to more third-party apps, and Android Studio to 40-plus outside coding agents

Connected Apps: public preview50+ apps in GeminiUS developers, waitlist

Google opened its Connected Apps platform in public preview for US developers, so outside apps can be used inside Gemini and, over the next few weeks, Google Search. Gemini already has more than 50 such apps, including Adobe, Experian, Peloton and Localiza; Search's current set includes Instacart and Canva, and Calm is a new partner (TechCrunch). OpenAI · Help Net Security · The Decoder · Oct 27–28

OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Terra halves prices for computer-use work

$1 / $5 per M tokensTerminal-Bench 4.0: 50.3%"Critical" cyber rating

GPT-6.1 Terra is a cheaper model for operating apps on screen and routine work, rolling out to paid ChatGPT Work and Codex users and in the API as gpt-6.1-terra. OpenAI prices it at $1 per million input tokens and $5 per million output, half the price of GPT-6.1 Sol, and says it matches GPT-6 Astra on its agent benchmark at a tenth of the token price while finishing tasks more than three times faster. It still recommends Sol and Astra for the hardest coding. OpenAI rates Terra at the "Critical" cybersecurity threshold of its Preparedness Framework and says it carries Astra's safeguards. OpenAI's Ted Sanders wrote on Hacker News that for subscribers "it's not a bombshell release."

Benchmarks and pricing are OpenAI's own. The practical change is cost: agent workloads take many steps, so halving the per-token price makes long computer-use tasks cheaper to run at scale.

Reuters · TD Cowen/AFS Freight Index · Oct 26

US retailers face record holiday parcel prices as fuel surcharges climb past 29%

Ground +5.8% y/yExpress +13.8%Diesel $6.28/gal

The TD Cowen/AFS Freight Index, built from data on 1,800 shippers, expects fourth-quarter ground parcel prices to be 5.8% higher than a year ago and express prices 13.8% higher. US diesel averaged $6.28 a gallon on Oct. 19, up 65% since the Iran war began in February (EIA). At that price UPS's fuel surcharge is 29.5% of the base rate and FedEx's 29%. AFS says diesel explains just under 30% of the surcharge; the rest comes from how carriers changed their formulas, so that even at 2021's $2.91 diesel the surcharge would be 23.75%, against 7.75% then. UPS says its pricing reflects premium service; FedEx did not comment. Regional carriers such as OnTrac, GoFo, UniUni, Jitsu and Veho now reach about 70% of the US population (ShipMatrix).

For e-commerce sellers, shipping is one of the few costs rising faster than inflation this season. Switching carriers mid-peak is hard, so most of this lands in margins or delivery fees.

The Guardian · AP · UOL · Oct 28

Brazil: Flávio Bolsonaro lands in Miami, calls himself the "true president," then edits the letter

After crossing into Argentina and flying via Guayaquil, Flávio Bolsonaro arrived at Miami-Opa Locka airport on Wednesday with his wife and daughters. About seven hours later he published a letter calling himself Brazil's "true president" and repeating, without evidence, claims of electoral fraud. He then deleted it and posted a version calling himself "the leader of Brazil"; at least three versions circulated (Guardian, UOL). His own Liberal Party, Brazil's electoral and supreme courts, the heads of both houses of Congress and international observers recognise Lula's win. Folha, O Globo and Estadão all called the declaration "coup-mongering." Brazilian papers report the family received US humanitarian visas. Trump said, "we gave him a pass to come into the country… But I won't get involved," and called it "a very strange election." Lula called the flight "shameful." Bolsonaro is under investigation in at least two criminal cases, including an alleged $905 million money-laundering scheme involving betting companies; he denies wrongdoing.

A defeated candidate claiming the presidency from abroad, with US entry granted, raises the stakes for Brazil–US relations and for Brazilian police, who face criticism for letting him leave.

Iran: Vance writes to Tehran's security chief as the US reportedly prepares for strikes after the midterms

Vice President JD Vance sent a letter to Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, proposing a framework to restart talks and saying any negotiations "require discussions on the nuclear issue," according to a US official cited by Axios (first reported by the WSJ). Washington is pursuing talks and military preparation at once. Which one wins out over the next week will drive oil prices and, through them, inflation decisions at the Fed and ECB.
