Semiconductors · Governance · Policy

Jensen Huang resigns from Nvidia. Washington turns to chip exports and the officials who approved them

Nvidia · SEC 8-K · Reuters · CNBC · AP · The Guardian · AFP · Nov 12–13
Interim CEO: Colette KressFirst chair: Harvey JonesClose −4.3%Results Nov 17

What happened. Nvidia said Thursday morning that Huang, who co-founded the company 33 years ago, had resigned as president, CEO and director "effective immediately." Its 8-K filing dates the board's acceptance to November 11. CFO Colette Kress, 59, CFO since 2013, becomes interim CEO and keeps the finance job. Harvey Jones, 73, a director since 1993 and former Synopsys CEO, becomes Nvidia's first board chair. The board said it will consider internal and external candidates. A person familiar told CNBC that the board decided Wednesday night, after the report of a second affair, and made it official Thursday morning. Nvidia's statement thanked Huang for "immeasurable contributions" and did not mention the scandal. Huang was not quoted. Reuters reported, citing two people, that he had also had a relationship with another Nvidia employee, whom it did not name. Nvidia declined to comment.

The policy fallout. The resignation moved the story to Washington. Speaker Mike Johnson said there is "a developing consensus in Congress" on limiting chip exports to China. A Senate aide told Reuters that Republicans want curbs in the annual defense bill. At least four chip-export bills are being pushed, among them the Chip Security Act, which would require location tracking for advanced processors, and the Remote Access Security Act, aimed at Chinese firms renting overseas data centers. Reuters, citing two people, said senior White House officials are discussing whether to push out Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, while a White House official said Trump "maintains complete confidence" in him. Senate Banking chair Tim Scott asked Huang and Lutnick to testify. House Foreign Affairs chair Brian Mast said Huang has agreed to testify before Thanksgiving. Chuck Schumer asked what Trump knew when he invited Huang aboard Air Force One. Democrats Ron Wyden and Richard Neal opened a probe into the arrangement that gives the government a 15% cut of Nvidia's China sales. Trump posted that former Apple CEO Tim Cook should take the job.

The other side. Investors and analysts mostly treated this as execution risk, not a broken thesis. "The risk is not that Nvidia suddenly loses its technology roadmap… The risk is execution," said ClearBridge's Michael Feldman. Capital.com's Daniela Hathorn said key-man risk "shouldn't be confused with the company's entire investment case." Wealth Club's Susannah Streeter said Kress is a "safe pair of hands" but lacks Huang's global standing with customers and partners. Kress and engineer Ian Buck were named most often as internal candidates, and Arm's Rene Haas and AMD's Lisa Su were mentioned as outsiders (Reuters, Guardian). Kress told staff there will be a company meeting on November 18. Beijing denies that Alice Huang is a spy. Attorney General Todd Blanche said the Justice Department has not reached a conclusion.

Why it matters (analysis)Leadership comes first: Nvidia reports quarterly results on November 17, with an interim CEO who has also been the architect of its financing deals with customers such as CoreWeave and Anthropic. Over the longer run, policy probably matters more. Huang was the industry's most effective advocate for keeping China open to US chips. With him gone and both parties now hostile, tighter limits on sales and on remote access look more likely, and that would hit Nvidia's China revenue and Chinese AI labs together. Thursday's trading, with rivals up and the index nearly flat, suggests investors think AI spending can survive without Huang. Next week's results will be the first test.

Macro · Energy · Rates

"We don't need a deal with Iran": oil jumps, the 10-year passes 5.1%, and a December Fed hike is fully priced

CNBC · Bloomberg via Rigzone · AP · Anadolu · OilPrice · Nov 12
Brent +3.3%WTI $88.742-yr 4.88%EU gas +10%

What happened. "We don't need a deal with Iran. Those days have long since passed. We've got their money, Hundreds of Billions of Dollars, and we've got their Strait!" Trump wrote on Truth Social. On Monday he had called the eight-month war "militarily effectively over" (OilPrice). AP and AFP reported that he also told Vice President Vance to focus on domestic matters instead of the Iran negotiations. Brent settled up 3.3% at $95.82 and WTI rose 2.6% to $88.74. European gas rose about 10% to €95.6 per megawatt-hour (Anadolu). Iran said four tankers were hit by explosions over two days on "unauthorized" routes through Hormuz. Those claims have not been independently verified.

Bearish forces too. Supply keeps improving. Saudi Arabia told OPEC its October output rose 26%, Iraq reported a wartime high, and US crude stocks rose 4.7 million barrels, the most since early August (EIA via Rigzone). Brent is down about 25% from its late-October high of $118. Real Macro's Jeff Currie said Middle East volumes cap the near-term price while insurance and tanker rates keep longer-dated contracts high: "you can't get bearish here nor can you get really bullish." Again Capital's John Kilduff said the market "may have gotten ahead of itself" in pricing an end to fighting.

The rate path. Tuesday's hotter-than-expected CPI set this up. On Wednesday, Goldman Sachs added another quarter-point hike in March to its forecast. Housing is feeling it: NAR said October existing-home sales hit their lowest in more than 16 years. Fed Governor Michelle Bowman separately warned that AI-related debt and valuations could lead to a correction if returns disappoint (The Neuron). The dollar rose to around 160 yen, the first time since early September, which renewed talk of intervention by Japan (AFP). October PPI comes out today, with a 0.4% rise expected after September's 1.3%.

Why it matters (analysis)Two of this year's biggest market drivers are moving the same way. A longer Iran war keeps an oil premium in place, and the Fed is raising rates into a slowdown in housing. A 5%-plus 10-year raises the cost of everything financed with long-term debt, including the data centers behind the AI trade, which ties this story to Nvidia's. A counterpoint: physical oil supply is recovering, and Brent is still down sharply this month, so the oil move may not last.

AI infrastructure splits on earnings: Nebius trims its forecast and drops 17%, Cerebras raises its and jumps

NBIS −16.8%CBRS +17% after hoursCisco +3% AHCoreWeave −6.9%

Nebius beat third-quarter estimates with $981 million in revenue, against $907.5 million expected. But it cut the top of its 2026 revenue range to $3.0–3.2 billion from $3.4 billion because some third-party capacity, including a New Jersey site, slipped into the first half of 2027. It raised 2026 capex to about $27 billion and guided $40–45 billion for 2027, and said 75–85% of next year's spending is covered by customer prepayments and asset-backed financing tied to hyperscaler contracts such as Microsoft and Meta. "None of that capacity is canceled," CEO Arkady Volozh said. It targets 2027 revenue of $12–13 billion and pre-tax break-even by the end of next year. After the close, Cerebras raised its 2026 adjusted revenue forecast to $940–960 million from $880–890 million, posted an adjusted profit when analysts expected a loss, and said it has signed Microsoft as a cloud customer. Its $26.1 billion of remaining obligations rests largely on its OpenAI deal. CEO Andrew Feldman said it lacks the capacity for a broad rollout of OpenAI's faster tier. Cisco beat on revenue ($18.5 billion) and raised fiscal 2027 guidance on AI data-center switching. Applied Materials also beat and guided above consensus but slipped 1.6% after hours.

Analysis: Demand is not the constraint in these reports. Delivery is. Power, sites and third-party builds set the pace, and the market is punishing anyone who misses a date. Data-center availability was the shared complaint, from Nebius's delays to Cerebras's capacity limits.

Databricks · TechCrunch · The Neuron · Nov 11–12

Agents move into the background: Databricks makes Genie always-on, and Amazon ships an emulator its agents wrote

Databricks turned its Genie assistant into an always-on agent that can own long-running work, hand tasks to subagents, track progress, and ask for decisions through a priority inbox. It grounds its actions in a new "Genie Ontology," a continuously updated map of a company's metrics, people and processes. Existing Genie features are generally available. The always-on mode is entering preview. In a TechCrunch demo, CEO Ali Ghodsi used it to dig into poor employee-survey results and a lost deal. Amazon released AWS Emulator, a free local simulator of more than 200 AWS services. Amazon says AI agents wrote and maintain all of its code with no manual review by engineers, and that it does not yet fully simulate outages or service limits. n8n announced Nod, an open-source personal agent that operates apps on the user's own Mac or PC. Ramp's AI Index showed corporate AI spending at another record in October, but monthly growth slowed to 1.65%, and the top 1% of companies account for 80% of OpenAI and Anthropic revenue in Ramp's data.

Analysis: The design pattern is converging on long-lived agents with a structured model of the business, plus a human checkpoint for decisions. Amazon's claim of zero human review is unusual and worth watching as a test of how much trust agent-written infrastructure code can earn.

PPC Land · Amazon seller email · Nov 10–12

Amazon starts showing FBA sellers when stock will become Prime-eligible: five days in January, 12 at peak

US only5 → 12 daysBFCM Nov 19–30New fee rules Feb 1

An email from the Fulfillment by Amazon team says that starting in November, sellers will see an estimated lead time on the Manage Shipments page for when "most" units of a shipment are received and become Prime-eligible. Estimates are "personalized by destination and time of year," and Amazon's own example is a shipment that takes five days in January taking 12 in the holiday season. The feature covers direct sortable shipments and replenishment from Amazon's AWD and GWD storage services, rolls out over "the coming weeks," and Amazon calls the figures typical timelines, "not guarantees." Some sellers said a count of Prime-eligible units has been visible for a year. The new part is a forecast at shipment creation. PPC Land notes the email doesn't say when the clock starts, what share counts as "most," or whether the estimate is the benchmark Amazon uses to reimburse low-inventory fees caused by its own "excessive" receiving delays under fee rules that start February 1, 2027.

Analysis: For sellers, the gap between a carton arriving and selling with the Prime badge is working capital and stockout risk. Publishing Amazon's own benchmark makes delays measurable, which could matter in disputes over fees.

October inflation rises to 2.2%, the highest since April, on gas tariffs and vegetables; core falls to 1.5%

2.2% m/m33.1% y/y26.3% YTDRiesgo país 829

INDEC said consumer prices rose 2.2% in October, up from 1.9% in September and above the central bank's REM survey (1.8%). Per Fundación Libertad's Joaquín Aranguiz, none of the survey's 45 participants had forecast more than 2.1%. Annual inflation still eased to 33.1% from 33.4%, and the year-to-date total is 26.3%. Housing and utilities led at 4.2%, driven by new gas tariffs, a smaller subsidized consumption block and changes to the cold-zone subsidy regime. Seasonal prices rose 5.5% (vegetables +10.6%) and regulated prices 2.9%. Core inflation was 1.5%, the lowest since July 2025, which several analysts stressed. Adcap said the headline "overestimates underlying inflationary pressures." Puente warned the cold-zone changes may also show up in November. Markets: dollar bonds rose about 1.3% and JP Morgan's country risk fell 27 points to 829, while the Merval fell 1.1% to 82,526, its fifth straight drop. The wholesale dollar ended at 1,545.50 pesos, the central bank bought $103 million, and gross reserves fell $252 million to $49.08 billion as gold fell 2.8% (Infobae).

After meeting Putin, US envoys say Europeans will join the next talks. Moscow sets conditions, and Kyiv reports Starshield limits

Moscow → DublinLargest swap plannedKostiantynivka truce proposal

Witkoff and Kushner met Putin at the Kremlin for about two hours, then Zelensky in Dublin. Witkoff said a new trilateral round is agreed and "the Europeans will join at the next meeting." Kremlin aide Yury Ushakov called the talks "constructive" and said the parameters of what would likely be the largest prisoner exchange are being finalized. He also said Europeans must first choose between "peace" or "war," meaning their military aid to Kyiv, and the Kremlin has not confirmed who will attend. Putin repeated that Russia accepts only "the line of the state border" as a demarcation, called foreign troops in Ukraine "unacceptable," and proposed a five-day humanitarian truce in Kostiantynivka modeled on Oleshky. A Ukrainian adviser told the BBC that Russia used the Oleshky truce to move in equipment. After Dublin, Zelensky listed three outcomes: the biggest exchange of the war, Europeans in talks "on an equal footing," and more multilateral meetings. Earlier he said Ukraine faces "systematic" restrictions on SpaceX's military Starshield service, hurting drone interception, and Ukraine is seeking clarification from the US. EU chief Ursula von der Leyen said new Russia sanctions and a 2027 Ukraine funding proposal will come before the next European Council.

Analysis: Bringing Europe in answers Kyiv's main procedural demand, but the Kremlin is linking Europe's seat to an end of arms supplies, and the territorial gap has not narrowed.
