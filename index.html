Paraguay · Banking crisis

Paraguay's digital-bank collapse topples a president, and a judge jails him

Guardian · AFP · AP · Reuters · ABC Color · Ámbito · Nov 7–8
~3M accounts frozen$800–860M public moneyCharged Sat · custody orderedAlliana sworn in

What happened. Paraguay's central bank put Ueno Bank, a fast-growing digital lender, into resolution on Monday night, November 2, citing insolvency. Nearly 3 million customers — about 60% of the country's adults, the Guardian reports — found their accounts frozen. AFP describes the same scale as nearly half of Paraguay's 6.5 million people. Ueno also held an estimated $800 million to $860 million in state and pension funds. About 32,000 pensioners went unpaid, businesses reported sales falling by as much as 80%, and media outlets in the same group, including the newspaper La Tribuna, had operations suspended.

President Santiago Peña resigned on Thursday in an attempt to keep the lifetime Senate seat that former presidents receive, which would have given him special jurisdiction. Lawmakers impeached him on Friday anyway and stripped the seat, so he faces ordinary courts. Vice President Pedro Alliana was sworn in to finish the term, which ends in 2028.

On Saturday prosecutors charged Peña with breach of trust and administration for personal gain — AFP's wording is "mismanagement for personal gain" — and asked for preventive detention. Each charge carries up to 10 years, Reuters reported. After a hearing, Judge Hernán Otazú ordered Peña into pretrial custody at the Asunción facility where he had been held since Friday, and froze the assets of Peña and former central-bank chief Carlos Carvallo Spalding, AFP reported. Carvallo, who ran Ueno until 2023 and was later appointed to lead the central bank, faces the same two charges plus falsifying government documents.

The alleged chain. Peña, who took office in 2023 at 44 as Paraguay's youngest democratic-era president, appointed Carvallo — then Ueno's chief executive — to run the central bank. Less than three months later, the Guardian reports, Carvallo authorized Ueno to become a full digital bank. Peña had about $1 million in shares bought in 2019 in a company that became Ueno Holding; the Guardian says he sold them in April 2025 (Reuters has reported March 2025). Prosecutors say he used his influence to steer government deposits to Ueno and downplayed warnings about questionable loans and solvency. Eight former Ueno executives and directors were charged earlier in the week.

The defense, and depositors. Peña's lawyer, Ricardo Preda, denies the charges and says a minister, not the president, decides where state money is deposited (AFP). Peña has said the bank's problems were a regulatory matter. A deposit-guarantee fund covers up to $38,000, which the Guardian says would apply to more than 90% of individual account holders. On Friday the central bank began paying customers with up to $1,565 who have no debts and hold an account elsewhere. The central bank told AFP that more than 50% of customers have recovered their funds; that is the bank's own claim, and it is unclear whether it means half of customers or of balances.

In Buenos Aires, Ámbito reports that ITTI, the group's technology arm, operates locally as Kaitel SRL in Belgrano with about 943 staff. Some were suspended without pay for 30 days from November 5, and the company has said it is struggling to pay October salaries. Peña had been a close ally of both Javier Milei and Donald Trump; AFP notes his removal as another setback for Trump's network of South American allies.

Why it matters (analysis)A mobile-first lender grew large enough in about three years to hold most adults' money and a large share of public funds, then failed, and the failure removed a sitting president in five days. The case puts the supervisor-as-former-CEO relationship at the center of a national crisis. It is also a test of how quickly a deposit-guarantee scheme can restore confidence when the bank is the country's everyday payments rail, and it leaves Argentina with a direct labor-market spillover through the group's Buenos Aires tech operation.

Middle East · Shipping

Iran drops its missile-range cap as the US widens the blockade and debris hits Aramco in Riyadh

Iran International · CENTCOM · The National · Anadolu · Reuters · Nov 7–8
2,000 km cap liftedM/V Huda disabledTask Force Iron GateAramco tower damaged

What happened. IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebbi said Saturday that Iran's self-imposed 2,000 km limit on ballistic-missile range "has been lifted," citing Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei's directive to respond with "no geographical limits." He did not say Iran has already fielded longer-range missiles, only that capabilities would be shown "in practice." On the same day an IRGC aerospace adviser said the missiles have a range of about 2,000 km. In March, US and British officials said two missiles were launched toward Diego Garcia, about 4,000 km away; Iran denied responsibility (Iran International).

In the Gulf of Oman, US Central Command said a US aircraft fired precision munitions at the stern of the St. Kitts-flagged cargo ship M/V Huda on November 7, after the crew ignored warnings and tried more than once to breach the naval blockade. The ship had visited an Iranian port. CENTCOM says that since July it has redirected 147 commercial vessels that complied and disabled five merchant ships that did not, and that it had previously destroyed 10 Iranian commercial oil tankers it described as part of an IRGC shadow network. Industry coverage notes this is a cargo ship rather than a tanker, which extends enforcement beyond oil cargoes.

The same day, CENTCOM said Admiral Brad Cooper directed the creation of Task Force Iron Gate: about 20 civilian and military staff based in Tampa, tasked with stopping advanced weapons, dual-use technology, sanctioned materials and what Washington calls illegal revenue streams moving across Iran's land borders and the Caspian Sea (Anadolu, citing CENTCOM).

Riyadh. Early Sunday the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted two ballistic missiles and two cruise missiles fired by Yemen's Houthis toward Riyadh and the south. The Saudi energy ministry said falling shrapnel caused minor damage to an Aramco administrative building in the King Abdullah Financial District, with no casualties. A Reuters witness, cited by The National, saw blown-out windows across several floors of the Aramco tower. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said the group carried out six operations, naming King Khalid International Airport, Aramco sites in Riyadh and Khurais, and Pumping Station No. 8 in Dawadmi. The Houthi-run health ministry said Saudi strikes near Sanaa on Saturday killed at least 33 people; Saudi authorities said they were investigating.

The IRGC separately claimed two tankers hit mines in and near the Strait of Hormuz; no ships were named, and that claim is unconfirmed. Friday's US strike on an anti-aircraft launcher on Qeshm Island was covered in yesterday's brief. Diplomacy is still in motion: Qatari mediation continues, and no new ceasefire has been announced.

Why it matters (analysis)The weekend pulled in opposite directions. Enforcement widened — from tankers to general cargo, and from sea lanes to land and Caspian routes — while Iran publicly discarded a range limit that had been part of its stated doctrine. At the same time, a hit on an Aramco building in the Saudi capital, even by falling debris, puts energy infrastructure back in view just after Brent fell more than 16% last week on hopes the war might ease. That is the main geopolitical risk for Monday's open, and for shippers still trying to move goods through the Gulf.

Voters rejected data-center measures in 40 of 41 ballot races as Anthropic prepares to go public

40 of 41 opposed$895.4B compute commitmentsNBC: majority called it a vote factor

CNBC reports that local voters opposed data-center ballot measures in 40 of 41 cases in Tuesday's midterms, and that more than half of voters in an NBC News exit poll called AI data centers a "crucial" or "major factor" in their congressional choice. Anthropic, which is preparing a Nasdaq listing that CNBC says is likely aimed at mid-November, disclosed in an amended prospectus about $895.4 billion of purchase commitments and lease agreements, "substantially all" tied to compute and infrastructure. Its risk factors say opposition to data-center development has become more organized. A University of Southern California survey, cited by CNBC, found 71% of respondents do not trust large technology companies "at all" to act in society's interest on data-center development. Anthropic declined to comment to CNBC.

Analysis: the commitment figure shows how much physical plant a frontier lab has already promised to buy or lease, just as permitting politics turned against those sites in many local votes. Yesterday's brief covered the markdown in Anthropic's discussed IPO range; the new element is how clearly voters singled out the buildings themselves.

Seoul Economic Daily · CNBC · Nov 6–8

Korea's 11.7% weekly drop, and a margin-loan waiver starting Monday

The Kospi closed Friday at 5,059.69, down 6.02% on the day and 11.71% on the week, after a Level-1 circuit breaker, Seoul Economic Daily reports. Foreign investors sold more than ₩6 trillion (about $4.2 billion) over two sessions. SK hynix fell 10.44% and Samsung 6.35%; power-equipment names such as Hyosung Heavy (−12.11%) and LS Electric (−10.93%) were also hit. Starting November 9, the government will waive brokerages' 140% collateral-maintenance requirement on margin loans for three months, a step meant to limit forced selling.

That is the main Asia setup for Monday, alongside oil: Brent's more than 16% weekly decline priced in hopes of an Iran de-escalation that this weekend's events put back in question. There was no US cash session Saturday or Sunday.

Berkshire Hathaway release · 10-Q · Nov 7

Berkshire bought back $9.6 billion of its own stock and held about $377 billion in cash and T-bills

Buybacks $9.6BOperating earnings $12.71BCash + T-bills $377.4B

Berkshire Hathaway's third-quarter release shows net earnings of $22.11 billion, down 28% from $30.80 billion a year earlier, and operating earnings of $12.71 billion, down 6% from $13.49 billion. The company bought about $9.6 billion of treasury shares in the quarter, bringing the nine-month total to about $14.4 billion — the first meaningful repurchase stretch since the program was quiet in 2025. The 10-Q lists cash and cash equivalents of $36.0 billion in the insurance-and-other group, $4.7 billion in railroad, utilities and energy, and $336.7 billion of short-term US Treasury bills, or about $377.4 billion together as of September 30. Insurance underwriting profit fell, while BNSF, Berkshire Hathaway Energy and the manufacturing group posted higher operating earnings.

Analysis: after a week in which public markets marked down AI-linked stocks, the world's best-known value investor spent more on its own shares than on a single large outside purchase. That is a signal about relative prices, not a forecast.

Infobae · Ámbito · Nov 7–8

Pope Leo XIV lands at 16:30, meets Milei, and local markets stay shut Monday and Tuesday

Pope Leo XIV arrives at Aeroparque from Montevideo at 16:30 today, Infobae reports, for the first papal visit to Argentina in 39 years (John Paul II was last here in April 1987). He will visit the San Martín monument, meet President Javier Milei at Casa Rosada, and address officials at Palacio Libertad at 18:30. Monday is a national holiday under Decree 1103/2026; Tuesday is a holiday in CABA and Córdoba, and Wednesday in Buenos Aires province. The local financial market is fully closed Monday and Tuesday.

Friday's close, recapped by Infobae on Saturday rather than as a new shock: JP Morgan's country-risk index finished at 939 basis points, up more than 150 points or 19% on the week and its highest since October 24, 2025. Sovereign dollar bonds fell about 5%. The Merval, around 84,600, was down about 8% in pesos and 9% in CCL dollars on the week, with losses in 12 of the last 15 sessions. Oil's weekly drop hit energy ADRs: YPF fell 17.6% to $38.70 and Vista 18.2%. The Banco Nación retail dollar was $1,570 and the blue $1,555 (its first weekly decline in a month). The central bank bought only $51 million this week; gross reserves were $49.64 billion after an $880 million IMF payment on Thursday.

The Paraguay crisis has a local labor footprint: about 943 staff at ITTI/Kaitel in Belgrano, as noted above. Peña had been an ally of Milei's government.

BBC · Reuters · AP · Nov 7–8

Six killed in Kraków's main square; officials say the gunman was likely far-right inspired

6 killed · 16 injured3 more bodies at his homeNational mourning

A gunman drove onto Kraków's Main Market Square shortly before 15:00 local time on Saturday and fired at random for about three minutes, killing four women and two men. The BBC says three of the dead were Polish, one was British, and two have not been identified. Sixteen people were injured, including five foreign nationals — from Austria, Germany, Portugal, Russia and Britain — and two police officers, one of them critically. Police shot and arrested the suspect. Three more bodies, which Prime Minister Donald Tusk said were likely his parents and sister, were found at his home in Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski, about 200 km away.

The BBC identifies the suspect as a 25-year-old Polish man (Reuters has reported 24). Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said he appeared "fascinated by far-right ideologies" and held "ultra-nationalist" views. Tusk called the attack terrorism and said Poland had not seen "such drastic cases" of this kind. Kraków is in mourning until November 11; Reuters reported a two-day national mourning period. The suspect's name has not been officially confirmed.

Times of Israel · Al Jazeera · Nov 7–8

Herzog asks Gadi Eisenkot, not Netanyahu, to form Israel's next government

President Isaac Herzog announced Saturday evening that he would task Yashar leader Gadi Eisenkot with forming a government. Eisenkot received 67 party recommendations to Benjamin Netanyahu's 46; United Torah Judaism's seven seats made no recommendation. The formal mandate ceremony is Sunday at 17:00 Israel time (12:00 ART). Eisenkot then has 28 days, extendable by 14.

The path is narrow. Netanyahu called for a "national unity government" that would include all of his previous allies, and labeled the Arab-led parties "terror supporters." Yashar said Likud could join only without "the extremists" and did not invite Netanyahu himself. Avigdor Liberman said he is open to "painful compromises" on Haredi enlistment to avoid another election. Channel 12 reported that Shas would not oppose a UTJ-backed Eisenkot government; Shas called reports that it might join such a cabinet "fake news." A combination of the 48-seat change bloc, UTJ's 7 and a Shas non-opposition of 6 would be a 61–59 majority.

Also: New Zealand's one-seat lead, a Zaporizhzhia blackout, a Japan quake, and floods in Gaza

Updated preliminary results reported by Reuters give New Zealand's National–ACT–NZ First grouping 67 of 131 projected seats, one more than a majority. In Gaza, the Hamas-run government media office said rain flooded at least 70,000 tents in about 12 hours, with injuries but no deaths reported (Reuters).
