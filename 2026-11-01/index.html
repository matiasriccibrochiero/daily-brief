Binance reopens withdrawals after replacing 95% of the $6.3B stolen; FBI names North Korea's TraderTraitor

Reuters · AFP · Cointelegraph · TokenPost · Exchange.news · Oct 31–Nov 1
$6.3B stolen~$6B replaced~$3B frozen15 networks in phase one

The world's largest crypto exchange resumed bitcoin withdrawals shortly after 06:00 GMT on Sunday (03:00 ART), followed by ether, BNB, XRP and selected USDT and USDC routes on Ethereum, BNB Chain and Base (Reuters, TokenPost). Binance support says phase one covers 15 networks, opened in batches ranked by last week's user demand, and warns of congestion and delays. Deposits stayed open throughout.

Binance says it has covered about $6 billion, or 95%, of the stolen tokens by buying replacements with company money; the remaining 5% are less liquid assets that will take longer to source. Co-CEO Yi He said half of the roughly $1 billion SAFU user-protection fund was used, alongside part of the futures insurance fund and corporate treasury, and pledged to refill SAFU. About $3 billion of stolen assets has been located and frozen with partners such as Tether and Circle.

Attribution firmed up, mostly. The FBI said on Saturday that North Korea's state-sponsored TraderTraitor unit carried out the attack and that it has watched the group convert stolen assets into ether, with "further laundering" expected. President Trump said the same day that the US was "not certain" North Korea was involved. Pyongyang denies running state hacking units and did not respond to Reuters. Founder Changpeng Zhao credited US agencies with tracking and freezing funds. Binance says a full zero-knowledge proof-of-reserves report is due early this week.

Why it matters (analysis)Covering a theft this size from its own balance sheet within three days is unusual; customers of past exchange failures such as Mt. Gox and FTX waited years for partial recoveries. If the reopening holds, it suggests the largest exchanges can now absorb a multi-billion-dollar loss without a run. The open questions are how much the replacement buying and drained reserves weaken Binance's own buffers, what the proof-of-reserves shows, and whether regulators push for tighter controls on hot wallets and features such as Ethereum's EIP-7702 delegation, which Binance says the attackers abused. The record size also adds to TRM Labs and Chainalysis data showing state-linked groups account for a growing share of crypto theft.

Global · Energy

Iran says Hormuz could reopen in two days on its terms, as Doha talks run against the clock of Tuesday's midterms

Reuters via Business Standard · CGTN · Al Jazeera · Middle East Eye · AP via Newser · Oct 31–Nov 1
Indirect talks in Doha144 ships redirected (CENTCOM)Claims unverified

Iranian negotiators held talks with Qatari mediators in Doha on Saturday, after more than 12 hours of talks in Tehran on Friday, coordinated with Washington, a source told Reuters. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said a new proposal agreed with mediators would allow the Strait of Hormuz to be fully reopened within two days if the US accepts Iran's conditions: lifting sanctions, restoring oil-export waivers, releasing frozen assets, reducing US forces in the region, ending hostilities on all fronts including Lebanon, and recognising a new shipping route developed with Oman. He called the US overture "positive" but said there are no immediate plans for direct talks.

Security chief Mohsen Rezaei said the tone of Vice President Vance's letter "completely changed" Washington's approach, but also that Iran is debating whether to drop its ban on nuclear weapons if fighting resumes; no decision has been made. Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei ordered the armed forces to be ready and said any attack must meet a "geographically unrestricted response." Secretary of State Marco Rubio said President Trump has not decided whether to resume strikes and prefers diplomacy, adding that "talks have actually never stopped." Trump said Iran wants to "make a deal" but questioned its reliability.

Pressure continues at sea. CENTCOM says it has redirected 144 commercial vessels under its blockade of Iranian ports as of Oct. 31. Iranian state media said the US attacked two fishing boats off Qeshm Island, which the US had not confirmed. On Sunday Iran designated Britain's Royal Navy and Royal Air Force as "terrorist" organisations (Middle East Eye).

Why it matters (analysis)Both sides are now publicly describing a path out, but their sequencing still conflicts: Iran wants US concessions first and nuclear talks later, while the Vance letter reportedly puts the nuclear programme on the table from the start. A two-day reopening offer lowers the bar for a deal, while the Oman-route condition would hand Iran a lasting say over who uses the strait, which the US and Gulf states have resisted. With regional governments expecting a US decision after Tuesday's vote, the coming week is the most consequential since the ceasefire for oil, which ended October near six-month highs.

Paying for AI gets harder: OpenAI's smaller round confirmed, S&P warns on Meta, Anthropic faces a junk rating

OpenAI up to $20BMeta AA− · outlook negativeAnthropic ~BB (reported)

CNBC confirmed on Saturday that OpenAI is now seeking up to $20 billion, down from up to $30 billion, at a reported $1.25 trillion post-money valuation; the round is still open and terms could change. UAE investors and Coatue are leading, and the FT says some investors cut allocations before a Friday deadline. Separately, S&P Global kept Meta's AA− rating but moved its outlook to negative, projecting a $71 billion free-cash-flow deficit in 2027 from AI capital spending (Reuters). And the FT reported that Blackstone told investors it expects Anthropic to receive a high speculative-grade ("junk") credit rating after its IPO, reversing its earlier expectation of investment grade (TechCrunch).

Together these move the AI-spending debate from equity prices to the cost of debt: a weaker credit profile raises borrowing costs for data-centre build-outs and can limit access to pension and insurance money. Supporters note demand indicators, such as cloud growth, remain strong.

Apple Intelligence reaches China in December, with Alibaba's Qwen inside Siri

Apple confirmed that iOS 27.3, due in December after a developer beta from Nov. 4, brings Apple Intelligence to iPhones in mainland China, including Live Translation, Writing Tools, Visual Intelligence and notification summaries. Alibaba's Qwen will power parts of Siri, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence; Alibaba says users choose whether to enable it and that it will not train on their requests. Newer Siri features from WWDC 2026 are not included.

China is Apple's largest market outside the US and the one where it has lacked AI features for more than a year, a gap local rivals have used. The arrangement is a template for how US platforms may ship AI where foreign models are restricted: local model, local partner, user opt-in.

CNBC · Oct 31

Behind some personal AI agents, human contractors still make the calls

Personal-agent startups Dazzle AI (led by Marissa Mayer), Duckbill and Wajo AI told CNBC they route tasks models fumble, such as phone calls, bookings and CAPTCHAs, to background-checked human contractors. Duckbill has launched an API so other agent builders can hand off such tasks, breaking them into "atomic bite-sized pieces" in a model its CEO likens to Uber. Mayer called it a "transitionary period" and said the human in the loop is "a benefit." CNBC notes Meta employees had raised privacy concerns about an internal test using contractors for its Muse agent; Meta says it does not currently use humans for Muse requests.

It is a reminder that agent reliability on real-world actions lags demos, and that hybrid human-AI services raise disclosure and privacy questions users may not expect.

Cisco Talos · WIRED · Oct 31

New infostealer uses commercial LLMs to rank victims by what they are worth

Cisco Talos found Volta-Autodoxer, Windows malware that steals passwords, session tokens, payment cards and wallet seeds, then mines a victim's Gmail and delivery receipts and sends the dossier through OpenRouter to an LLM prompted to act as an "intelligence analyst." It defaults to Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6, with fallbacks to Moonshot's Kimi K2 and Google's Gemini 2.5 Flash in case of refusals. Talos has not confirmed use in the wild and says it may be built for sale to other criminals.

AI here is not inventing new attacks but speeding triage, helping low-skill attackers use stolen tokens before they expire. Built-in fallbacks across providers also show the limits of any single lab's guardrails.

Infobae · Clarín · Oct 31

Central bank ends October with its strongest dollar buying since July, before an $880M IMF payment

The BCRA bought US$1.395 billion in October, including $96 million on Friday, taking 2026 purchases to $15.96 billion, more than 50% above the implicit $10 billion target in the IMF programme (Infobae). It paused buying for three sessions last week amid volatility after the Brazilian real fell following Brazil's election. Gross reserves still fell $1.59 billion on Friday to $48.56 billion, which Infobae attributes to month-end bank reserve movements and a more than 3% fall in gold. The Treasury owes the IMF $880 million in interest on Monday and is expected to buy the dollars from the central bank. Separately, the government warned that beef, utility tariffs and fuel will add inflation pressure through year-end (Clarín).

Reserve accumulation is the main test of the IMF programme; Monday's payment and country risk at a one-year high will show how much of that buffer is usable ahead of the 2027 elections.

Spain's Urda rail crash: 69 dead, and a control-centre order under investigation

69 dead39 still hospitalised (Oct 30)2nd fatal crash on the line this year

A Renfe high-speed train ran into a stationary Iryo train near Urda (Toledo) on the Madrid–Seville line on Wednesday morning, killing 69 people (Castilla-La Mancha government; an earlier figure of 74 was retracted). Transport Minister Óscar Puente said a computer failure had halted six trains and that the control centre then gave an "inexplicable" order to resume at normal speed; radio recordings published by elDiario.es show the Iryo driver reporting she was still stopped. In January, a crash on the same line near Adamuz killed 46. Spain held a third day of mourning on Saturday.

Unions including Semaf, CSIF and UGT blame understaffing, fatigue and pressure on controllers to restore timetables, and warn against blaming one individual; La Razón reports that infrastructure manager Adif pressed controllers to recover traffic quickly to avoid delay compensation, which has not been independently confirmed. Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez has backed Puente and not sought his resignation, while the opposition is demanding accountability (CNN, Russpain).
