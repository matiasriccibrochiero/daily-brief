Nvidia's board investigates whether Jensen Huang was a Chinese intelligence target

Nvidia · NYT · FT · AP · CNBC · Reuters · Guardian · Bloomberg · Nov 8–9
−5.2% premarket~$4.5T companyIndependent directors leadHuang stays CEO

What happened. On Sunday Nvidia's board said it was "initiating an independent investigation in light of recent reporting" that Huang "has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort." A committee of independent directors, working with outside counsel, will run it. The board said it has "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised," that it has confidence in Huang, and that he will cooperate. The same day Huang emailed employees: "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did." He said he takes "full responsibility" and will remain chief executive "without interruption," according to a company spokesperson.

Who is involved. The woman is Alice Huang (no relation), a Chinese national and executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud company that is an Nvidia customer and rents computing power to Alibaba and other Chinese firms, the Guardian reports. The New York Times, which has reported on Megaspeed for about a year, says US intelligence concluded she was passing information to Beijing; the Financial Times says US officials believe she was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security. A Times reporter told NPR that her company bought nearly $5 billion of Nvidia chip technology. Megaspeed has been investigated over whether it helped restricted Chinese buyers get US-controlled chips; Nvidia has said an earlier internal review found no wrongdoing. Reuters says it could not independently verify the Times' claims.

Washington. The Times reported Sunday that Secretary of State Marco Rubio oversaw the drafting of a classified memo this spring warning senior officials about the relationship. President Trump said he had not spoken to Huang, called him "an honorable guy" and "a fine person," said he should stay on, and said he would "certainly" take him on his upcoming Asia trip. House Republicans had already sought briefings from Huang, Reuters reports, and CNBC describes the scrutiny in Congress as bipartisan. China's foreign ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun called the "so-called Chinese spy narrative" "entirely baseless vilification and smearing."

Markets and the other side. Nvidia was down 5.2% in premarket trading, CNBC reported, after falling about 3.5% on weekend-price platforms. Other US chipmakers including Intel, Marvell, Qualcomm and Arm were down more than 1%, while Asian suppliers such as TSMC rose about 4.7% on the broader tech rebound. Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives & Co. called it a "black eye moment" and said there is "clearly risk" Huang could be removed depending on the probe. Pepperstone's Chris Weston said the market does not expect him to step down; Sequoia's Shaun Maguire called Huang "irreplaceable for America's AI leadership," a post Elon Musk endorsed. Bloomberg notes Nvidia has no public succession plan. Nvidia reports quarterly results on November 17.

Why it matters (analysis)Nvidia supplies most of the chips behind the AI build-out and is the US company most exposed to the fight over chip exports to China. The probe raises three separate questions: whether any information or controlled hardware actually moved (the board says it has no reason to think so, and nothing has been proven publicly), whether the board can investigate a founder-CEO it has already said it backs, and whether Washington tightens export rules for Southeast Asian cloud resellers like Megaspeed. The third could matter more to Nvidia's revenue than the first two, and it comes as a new Congress takes office.

Middle East · Energy

Trump calls new Iran strikes "highly unlikely" as Rubio doubts a deal and a drone shuts Iraq's Khor Mor gas field

Iran International · Al Jazeera · Anadolu · Xinhua · Reuters · Rudaw · Nov 8–9
Iron Gate "in effect"Qatar PM at White House today~2,800 MW offline

Washington's signals. In a phone interview with CNN on Sunday, Trump said more strikes on Iran were "highly unlikely" to be needed "if they sign a very strong agreement" covering its nuclear program and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. He also said US forces had identified targets including 38 bridges, and that Task Force Iron Gate, the new effort against Iran's land and Caspian supply routes, is operating at full strength. The Jerusalem Post, citing two sources, reported that US and Israeli forces prepared for renewed strikes over the weekend but no order was given. Secretary of State Rubio was more pessimistic, saying a diplomatic agreement may not be possible and calling Iran's demands unrealistic (Anadolu, Al Jazeera). Trump meets Qatar's prime minister in the Oval Office on Monday, and said he expects to discuss Iran and "denuclearisation" with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin at next week's APEC summit.

Tehran's position. A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Iran sent a nuclear proposal through Doha, but that further talks depend on sanctions relief, access to frozen assets and war reparations; the US wants nuclear issues addressed first. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said mediators had been inactive for several days since the US midterms and repeated an offer to reopen Hormuz within two days if Iran's conditions are met.

On the ground. Iranian media reported four explosions on Qeshm Island and blasts in Bandar Abbas on Sunday night; the IRGC-affiliated Tasnim agency said a US projectile hit Qeshm, which has not been independently verified, and a provincial official said no vital infrastructure was damaged. UKMTO said a tanker was struck by an unidentified projectile leaving Hormuz on Saturday; IRGC claims that two or three tankers hit mines remain unverified. In Iraq's Kurdistan Region, an explosive-laden drone struck the Khor Mor gas field on Sunday night, starting a fire and halting output. Operator Dana Gas said no staff were hurt; the regional electricity ministry said about 2,800 MW of generation was lost. No one has claimed the attack. The Houthis claimed strikes on Riyadh and on Aramco's Abqaiq facility (Xinhua); Saudi officials have confirmed intercepts and debris damage in Riyadh, reported yesterday, but not the Abqaiq claim.

Why it matters (analysis)Public statements now point in two directions at once: the president is lowering the odds of new strikes while his top diplomat is lowering the odds of a deal, and the two sides still disagree on what gets negotiated first. Oil traders chose the softer reading, with Brent down about 2% on Monday. Khor Mor shows how the conflict keeps reaching infrastructure outside Iran: the field feeds much of Iraqi Kurdistan's power supply, so the immediate effect is blackouts, not lost exports.

Apple's new CEO is pushing for an agentic Siri as soon as next year

ReportediOS 28 cycle changedComputer-use gap

Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports that Apple chief executive John Ternus held a closed-door meeting with software chief Craig Federighi in recent weeks, pressing him to move beyond incremental Siri fixes toward an assistant that can manage tasks on its own. The goal, per the report, is an agent-style Siri as early as next year to compete with OpenAI's ChatGPT Dots, Meta's Muse and xAI's Grok. Apple is also changing how it develops iOS 28 and macOS 28, giving engineers more time to explore features before locking them in.

Gurman adds that Mike Rockwell, who has run Siri since its AI reset, is still seriously considering leaving next year, and that Apple lacks a "computer use" capability, meaning an agent that operates apps by clicking and typing, which OpenAI and Anthropic already ship. AppleInsider separately reports deeper Siri control of Apple's own apps won't arrive until spring 2027. Apple has not commented. Analysis: Apple controls the operating systems on well over a billion devices, so if it ships an agent with system-level permissions, it would set the default for how most consumers first hand tasks to AI.

Trump says he "doesn't mind guardrails" on AI and will work with states that want data centers

First post-midterm interview"Deep second position"

In a 60 Minutes interview aired Sunday, Trump said AI risks can be managed and that he doesn't "mind guardrails," but added, "I don't wanna put us in a deep second position" and argued that "China has no guardrails." He framed the issue as a race: "If we don't have it, China's going to have it." Reactions split along familiar lines. Analysis: the comments do not change policy, but they are the first signal from the White House on AI since the election shifted control of Congress.

Onodrim raises $300 million to buy and modernize European defence suppliers

Amsterdam-based Onodrim Industries, founded by former Palantir staff Aistis Šimaitis and Alexander Blessing, raised a $300 million Series A led by Lux Capital, with General Catalyst, Lakestar, Altimeter and others, at a $1.3 billion valuation. It follows a $45 million seed earlier this year. China's factory prices rise at the fastest pace in four years as consumer inflation cools

PPI 5.7%CPI 0.7%

China's producer price index rose 5.7% in October, its highest in more than four years, while consumer inflation slowed to 0.7%, below expectations, CNBC reports. Reuters attributes the factory-gate rise to higher oil costs and the consumer slowdown to weak domestic demand. Analysis: rising input costs that factories cannot pass on to Chinese shoppers squeeze margins at home and can push export prices up, which matters for anyone sourcing goods from China going into 2027. Pope Leo XIV tells Argentina's leaders "the economy must serve the person"; Milei declines to debate it

First papal visit in 39 yearsMass in Palermo today

Pope Leo XIV landed at Aeroparque on Sunday afternoon, where President Milei greeted him, then held a private meeting at the Casa Rosada and gave his first speech in the country at the Palacio Libertad. He urged leaders to put the common good above immediate interests, to strengthen democratic institutions through dialogue, and to back policies that "stimulate private initiative and ensure that economic growth also benefits those who are disadvantaged" (Vatican News). He said, "The economy must serve the person, not the person the economy."

AP described the speech as a diplomatic but pointed critique of Milei's austerity and libertarian agenda, and said it exposed differences on social spending and AI that the government had played down. Milei called the visit "verdaderamente estremecedor" (Infobae) and said he did not want "to put the Holy Father into the base debate of local politics" (Clarín). On Monday the pope visits the Pequeño Cottolengo Don Orione in Claypole at 9:15 and celebrates Mass at the Monumento a los Españoles in Palermo at 11:30, then meets workers and religious leaders (Perfil). Argentine markets are closed Monday and Tuesday for the holiday; INDEC publishes October inflation on Thursday.

Eisenkot formally receives the mandate to form Israel's government, with 28 days to do it

President Isaac Herzog formally tasked Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the centrist Yashar party and a former military chief, with forming a government on Sunday. Recommendations do not guarantee a coalition: the 67 span parties with sharply different positions.
