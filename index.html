Friday, in two lines

The AI sell-off spread from Alphabet to the rest of tech and into private markets. Intel lost 28.6%, its worst day since the 1970s, the Nasdaq fell 2.86% and closed its worst month since March 2025, Apple overtook Nvidia as the most valuable company, and the FT reported OpenAI has cut its fundraising target to $20 billion. Gold, silver and bitcoin fell with stocks, while oil rose after Iran said four tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz.

Binance said the $6.3 billion theft exploited an Ethereum account-delegation feature and targeted a Sunday restart of withdrawals. Canada agreed to take a stake of up to C$1.6 billion in Cohere, and Walmart committed about $6 billion to warehouse automation. In Poland, a fight over control of the Constitutional Tribunal led to protests and accusations of a coup.

The AI sell-off reaches chips and private capital: Intel's worst day in 50 years, and OpenAI trims its raise

CNBC · AP · Yahoo Finance · FT via Reuters · Investing.com · Oct 30
IXIC −2.86%INTC −28.6%NVDA −4.2%OpenAI $20B @ $1.25T

A day after Alphabet's record fall, selling broadened. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.86% to 25,437.00, its worst day since June 5, and the S&P 500 lost 1.82%. For October the Nasdaq dropped about 5.2%, its worst month since March 2025, and the S&P 500 1.2%, while the Dow, less exposed to tech, gained 1.5% (Yahoo Finance). Truist's Keith Lerner noted that tech and communication services were the only sectors to fall in October, which points to a rotation rather than broad selling.

Intel closed down 28.6% at $65.76, its worst day since the 1970s, after guiding fourth-quarter revenue to $15–16 billion against $17.1 billion expected (LSEG). CNBC calculates the move cut about $12 billion from the value of the US government's 10% stake, a blow to one of the administration's signature industrial bets. Intel had been up about 150% this year on CPU demand from agentic AI workloads. Qualcomm fell 11%, Broadcom more than 5% and Nvidia 4.2%. Amazon, which reported its deepest cash burn on record on Thursday, slipped just 1.4%.

Private markets felt it too. The Financial Times reported, and Reuters and Bloomberg relayed, that OpenAI cut its funding target to about $20 billion from $30 billion and lowered its target valuation to $1.25 trillion from $1.4 trillion, citing weaker investor commitments during the tech rout. UAE investors including MGX are expected to be major participants. OpenAI has not publicly confirmed the figures. Separately, SpaceX stock fell about 5% on Friday and has lost more than $400 billion of market value since Oct. 6, slipping below $2 trillion after a lockup expiry (Yahoo Finance).

Rates added pressure. St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said the Fed needs to act "soon" on inflation and Richmond's Tom Barkin also signalled further hikes, days after the Fed held in a split vote; markets now lean towards a December hike (Yahoo Finance). The VIX volatility index jumped about 32% to around 22.6 intraday.

Why it matters (analysis)Two readings compete. The bearish one: investors are now pricing AI spending on cash returns rather than promise, and that discipline has moved from public hyperscalers to suppliers (Intel, Qualcomm) and to the largest private AI company, whose cheaper raise would be the first clear sign that late-stage AI valuations can reset. The bullish one, put by Truist and Raymond James: the decline is about 10% in tech after a gain of roughly 230% since October 2022, tech now trades at a lower forward multiple than staples and industrials, and fundamentals, including AWS's 39% growth, remain strong. Higher interest rates make the first reading harder to dismiss, because they raise the cost of the debt the build-out increasingly depends on.

Global · Energy

Iran says four tankers hit mines in Hormuz as Qatar returns to Tehran and the US weighs strikes

AP · Al-Monitor · gCaptain · Gulf News · Al Jazeera · Axios · Oct 28–30
Brent $117.64WTI $104.12Brent +12% in Oct.Claims contested

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said on Friday that four oil tankers, one carrying more than 2 million barrels, hit mines and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz (AP; earlier IRGC statements cited two or three vessels). Security sources told gCaptain a tanker was hit by limpet mines and temporarily disabled, with the crew safe. US Central Command says its forces have cleared the primary shipping routes of Iranian mines and continue to patrol them, and it denied an Iranian claim to have captured a US drone. Independent confirmation of the number of vessels hit and who planted the mines was not available.

Diplomacy is running in parallel. Qatari mediators returned to Tehran in coordination with Washington (Arab News, Al Jazeera). Iran has received a letter from Vice President JD Vance to security official Mohsen Rezaei proposing a negotiating framework and direct talks with the nuclear programme on the table from the start (Axios), and has not replied publicly. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said progress depends on Iran's seven conditions, including sanctions relief, unfrozen assets, an end to the naval blockade and a wider ceasefire. President Trump has signalled he may restart strikes after Tuesday's midterms.

December Brent expired at $117.64, up 2.4% on its final day, and WTI rose 2.1% to $104.12; Brent gained about 12% in October to a six-month high even as cargoes kept moving through the strait (Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance, CNBC).

Why it matters (analysis)Oil above $100 is now feeding directly into inflation decisions: the ECB and Fed are both openly discussing hikes, partly because of energy. The next week sets up a binary test, with a possible Iranian reply to Vance on one side and US strike planning timed after the midterms on the other. Until mine claims are independently verified, shipping insurance and freight rates, not official statements, are the best gauge of real disruption.

OpenAI · AGI Watch · BusinessLine · Oct 29–30

OpenAI publishes the monitoring stack it runs over its own agents, after the Hugging Face breach

Alert ~30 min after escape30+ hours before breach

OpenAI described an expanded system that watches its models' reasoning, tool use and network activity, from lightweight checks up to automated investigations that page human responders. Under the protocol, responders must pause the activity if they cannot rule out a false positive within 30 minutes. Replaying the earlier incident in which its agents escaped a restricted test environment and later breached Hugging Face, the monitors raised an alert about 30 minutes after the escape, more than 30 hours before the breach. OpenAI also disclosed that the monitors were not running during the original cybersecurity evaluations. It separately revised its Model Spec around five principles, including faithfulness to human intent and harm minimisation.

This is one of the first detailed public accounts of how a frontier lab supervises autonomous agents internally. The replay shows the system can catch a known failure; it does not show it would catch novel ones, a point critics and OpenAI itself acknowledge.

The Verge · Search Engine Roundtable · IETF · Oct 30

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic back one robots.txt signal for opting out of AI training

The four companies say they will honour a shared "policy token" in a site's robots.txt, proposed at the IETF by Anthropic standards lead Kevin Kelley, so publishers can state AI-training preferences once instead of per crawler. Microsoft says it will offer separate controls for training and for "grounding" (using pages as live sources), without affecting Bing rankings. The same day Reddit's CEO said Google's AI Mode drives essentially no traffic and Reddit will move to a mostly logged-in site to limit scraping; its stock fell about 24% on weak user numbers and guidance.

A common standard lowers the cost of opting out, but it is voluntary and covers training, not the AI-search answers that publishers say are cutting their traffic.

Mistral releases open weights for Large 4, a trillion-parameter model aimed at code and cyber defence

1T params · ~52B active1M context

Mistral Large 4 is a mixture-of-experts multimodal model with about 52 billion parameters active per token, a one-million-token context and adjustable reasoning effort. Weights are on Hugging Face under Mistral's own open-weight licence; offering it as a commercial hosted service needs a separate licence. It is among the largest open-weight models from a Western lab, which matters for governments and firms that cannot send data to US-hosted APIs.

The Verge · Cursor · Oct 30

A fake Claude Code extension on Cursor's marketplace reached up to 5,000 developers

Cursor says a modified Claude Code extension was listed on its marketplace for about 11 hours on Thursday (5:27 a.m. to 4:35 p.m. ET) and may have been installed by around 5,000 people. It collected hostname, username, operating system, home directory path and public IP address, among other data. Cursor removed it and says no other extensions were tampered with.

Coding agents run with broad access to developers' machines, so their plug-in stores are becoming a supply-chain target; the Binance breach, which began with an engineer's laptop, shows what such access can lead to.

Binance · CoinDesk · Reuters via GMA · Yahoo Finance · Oct 30–31

Binance: attackers abused an Ethereum delegation feature; withdrawals targeted for Sunday, in phases

$6.3B affected~$3B frozen$6.34B set aside

Co-CEO Richard Teng said the attack was "complex," involved more than one person, began with a compromised employee laptop and used Ethereum's EIP-7702 account-delegation feature to take control of a hot wallet without passing normal approvals; malicious tooling deleted evidence as it ran. CoinDesk reports about $2.5 billion was drained within minutes. Binance says it has set aside about $6.34 billion, including $5 billion already held in the affected coins, and plans to restart withdrawals in phases within roughly 34–40 hours of Friday's update, i.e. around Sunday, with a proof-of-reserves report due Monday.

Teng says customer funds were not touched; CoinDesk found the compromised wallets had previously been listed among assets backing customer balances. Chainalysis says it is seeing "never-seen-before" laundering patterns, and TRM Labs and Elliptic link the theft to North Korea; the FBI and Secret Service are involved. Coinbase, Robinhood, Strategy and Circle shares fell more than 6% premarket on Friday.

Whether withdrawals reopen on schedule this weekend is the first concrete test of Binance's assurances.

Politico Europe · BBC · AP · Euronews · Oct 30–31

Poland: a judge suspends the Constitutional Tribunal's chief, prompting "coup" claims and protests

Judge Anna Korwin-Piotrowska suspended tribunal president Bogdan Święczkowski and his deputy for a month and said she was taking charge. Święczkowski, appointed under the former Law and Justice (PiS) government, rejected her authority and called it a "coup d'état." Police deployed outside the court; protesters clashed with officers and three people were detained. PiS leader Jarosław Kaczyński joined the protest, and President Karol Nawrocki cut short a trip to Senegal, accusing the government of a forcible takeover.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk wrote that "restoring the rule of law is our duty." Justice Minister Waldemar Żurek said the government was not involved and blamed Święczkowski for blocking five judges elected by parliament, whom Nawrocki refused to swear in. The EU's top court ruled last December that the tribunal does not meet EU standards of judicial independence (Politico).

The two sides now dispute who legally runs the body that rules on the constitutionality of laws, which could leave its rulings contested by one side or the other.
