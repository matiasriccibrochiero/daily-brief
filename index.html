US economy · Fed · Bonds

Hotter October inflation and a hawkish Waller put a December rate hike all but priced in

BLS · Reuters · CNBC · Washington Post · Sharecast · Nov 10
CPI +0.4% m/mCore +0.3%2-yr 4.74%10-yr 4.99%

What happened. The Bureau of Labor Statistics said consumer prices rose 0.4% in October after 0.6% in September, against a 0.3% consensus. Core prices, excluding food and energy, rose 0.3% for a third straight month, versus 0.2% expected. Reuters notes it is the third consecutive month of headline gains of 0.4% or more. Shelter rose 0.3% and accounted for more than 30% of the increase; food rose 0.5%, with beef up 0.9% and snacks up 1%; energy rose 0.7% even though pump prices have been falling in recent weeks, and transportation services jumped 0.8%. Used cars fell 1.2%. The BLS again published no year-over-year headline rate because October 2025 prices were never collected during last year's shutdown; economists polled by Reuters estimate annual core inflation rose to about 2.8% from 2.6%.

The Fed. Hours earlier in London, Governor Christopher Waller said he is "leaning toward" a December increase even if October data came in soft, and that if readings ran higher than forecast "a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered," adding that "hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk" (CNBC). Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said she sees two more quarter-point hikes before a pause. Chicago's Austan Goolsbee warned on Monday against letting the economy overheat. The Fed raised rates in September and held in October, with Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack dissenting.

Markets. The 2-year yield jumped more than 18 basis points to 4.736% and the 10-year rose 13 basis points to 4.992% after trading above 5%; a soft $42 billion 10-year auction, with dealers left holding 10%, the most since May, added to the selling (CNBC). The S&P 500 fell 0.38% to 7,520.93 and the Nasdaq 0.74%, after Monday's best day since April. US bond markets are closed today for Veterans Day; producer prices follow on Friday.

Why it matters (analysis)The debate has moved from whether the Fed hikes again to how much and how often. Falling oil is not yet showing up in the inflation numbers, and the strength is in food, shelter and services, which tend to be stickier than fuel. Mortgage, auto and credit-card rates key off the 10-year, which is back at levels last seen before the 2008 crisis, and the higher cost of debt is also the main reason investors cite for growing caution on capital-heavy AI infrastructure. Some economists, including Goldman Sachs Asset Management's Lindsay Rosner, say hikes could extend into 2027; others expect energy relief to cool prices by spring.

AI · Capital markets

Anthropic sets an IPO range that values it at up to $1.4 trillion, with trading planned for November 19

CNBC · TechCrunch · Anthropic prospectus · Nov 10
825M shares$70–78Up to $64.35B raisedNasdaq: ANT

What happened. Anthropic's amended filing offers 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78. At the top, it would net up to $64.35 billion and be worth up to about $1.405 trillion on a fully diluted basis, up from the $965 billion valuation of its May private round (CNBC). On shares outstanding after the offering, the implied market value is closer to $0.9 to $1.0 trillion. People familiar told CNBC it plans to price on November 18 and trade on the 19th, with about 20% of shares earmarked for retail investors, versus a typical 5% to 10%, and that the book is already covered at the range. In a separate $26 billion private placement at the IPO price, AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC will buy Class N shares not subject to lock-up.

The numbers. Revenue for the nine months to September 30 was $33.24 billion, up about 1,200% year on year, and the company says run-rate revenue reached roughly $81 billion at the end of October, $10 billion more than a month earlier. It reported a $104 billion net loss for the period, mostly from changes in the fair value of convertible notes and derivatives, against a $25.1 billion loss a year earlier. The same day, Anthropic launched Claude Meetings, Whiteboards and Sheets in beta (see AI & agents).

Context and caveats. CNBC reported last week that some investors had been bracing for less than the $2 trillion once discussed, with one calling $1.5 trillion "a stretch." The listing comes after a Democratic sweep in Congress, state ballot measures that could slow data-center construction, 10-year yields near 5%, and a week of turmoil at Nvidia, one of the placement buyers.

Why it matters (analysis)This would be the first public listing of a frontier AI lab, giving markets a daily price on the economics of building large models rather than relying on private rounds. The size of the chipmaker and cloud-provider placement shows how tightly Anthropic's suppliers and its investors overlap, which critics describe as circular and supporters describe as strategic alignment. Demand in the book over the next week will be a direct read on whether the recent selloff in AI infrastructure stocks reflects a change in long-term conviction or a reaction to interest rates.

Reuters · OpenAI · Nov 10

OpenAI lets businesses claim ChatGPT profiles and deploy their own customer-facing agents

BetaUS e-commerce firstShopify integration

OpenAI launched "Businesses" in ChatGPT. Through a Business Hub, a verified company can claim its listing, update details, upload product feeds with prices and availability, and see aggregated mentions, views and website clicks. A second tool, Business Agents, lets users chat with a company's own agent about its products or services; it starts with eligible US e-commerce and online-service businesses. Companies can sponsor their agent and use it as the landing point for ChatGPT Ads, with sponsored placements labeled. OpenAI says it does not share individual conversations with businesses and only shows insights above minimum volume thresholds. Shopify merchants can connect through Business Hub or the Shopify admin (Reuters).

Analysis: it is a two-sided listing product, similar in structure to Google Business Profile, inside a chatbot. It moves some product discovery from search pages into conversations and gives OpenAI an ad unit tied to an agent rather than a link. Questions remain about how sponsored answers will be separated from organic ones in practice.

TechCrunch · NY DFS · Nov 10

New York's AI safety law takes effect, with frontier labs required to register by January 1

>$500M revenue threshold72-hour incident reports

New York opened the registration portal for the RAISE Act. Frontier developers with more than $500 million in prior-year revenue, which includes OpenAI and Anthropic, must register by January 1, 2027, publish safety frameworks and report critical safety incidents within 72 hours. Trade reports put penalties at up to $1 million for a first violation and $3 million after. Supporters see it as the first concrete state reporting regime for large labs; industry groups have argued state-by-state rules risk a patchwork, and federal preemption remains debated.

Singles' Day peaks today with flat sales and no headline totals from Alibaba or JD

~1T yuan to Oct 31Roughly flat y/yChina retail +1.1% Jan–Sep

China's biggest shopping festival peaks on November 11 after a campaign that began October 20. Retail data platform Syntun put cumulative sales at just over 1 trillion yuan ($154 billion) through October 31, broadly in line with a year ago; the full 2025 festival hit a record of almost 1.7 trillion yuan, largely because platforms keep stretching it (AP). Alibaba and JD again did not publish totals. Alibaba built its Qwen assistant into Taobao and Tmall for orders and recommendations, and AI "token plans" were sold as products for the first time. Analysts quoted by AP describe "discount fatigue" as year-round promotions erode the event's draw, while health and supplement categories grow. The backdrop is a split economy: exports rose 35% in October while domestic car sales fell almost 25% in the first nine months.

Analysis: a flat Singles' Day says more about China's household demand than about e-commerce itself, and continued export strength keeps pressure on prices and competition in overseas markets.

Mercor doubles its valuation to $20 billion to train and test AI agents on professional work

$750M Series D$20B valuation$10B a year ago

Mercor, which began as an AI hiring platform and now supplies subject-matter experts whose work is used to train and evaluate AI models, raised $750 million at a $20 billion valuation, double the roughly $10 billion of its October 2025 round. Benchmark, Felicis, Nvidia and Apollo-affiliated funds participated (Reuters). Analysis: evaluation and expert-data companies are a direct bet that enterprises will customize agents on their own processes rather than use generic ones.

Path Robotics raises $300 million at $2.45 billion to take its welding AI to other factory tasks

Columbus, Ohio-based Path Robotics raised a $300 million Series E led by Georgian at a $2.45 billion post-money valuation, with D. E. Shaw Ventures and NAVER Ventures joining. Nvidia slides a second day as a shareholder suit and a new report add to the Huang probe

Class action filed$7.5B in shipments, per The InformationOvernight +3%

Nvidia fell 5.47% to $168.89 on Tuesday, briefly dipping below a $4 trillion market value. The Rosen Law Firm filed a proposed class action in San Jose alleging the company and Jensen Huang misled investors about his relationship with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang and Nvidia's ties to Megaspeed (CNBC). The Information reported that her relationships may have helped facilitate at least $7.5 billion of Nvidia chip shipments through Chinese companies; Nvidia and Megaspeed have denied wrongdoing, and China calls the allegations groundless. Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives & Co. wrote that investor chatter puts about a 15% chance on Huang's removal and expects the board review to take four to six weeks. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said there are "real concerns that need to be addressed." Shares were about 3% higher in overnight trading.

House Republicans send the Hegseth impeachment articles to committee, ending the effort this session

211–204Massie the lone GOP no11 Democrats absent

The House voted 211–204 to refer Rep. Thomas Massie's eight impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Republican-led Judiciary Committee, which NPR and BBC say effectively kills them before this Congress ends. Republicans voted 210–1 for the motion, joined by the chamber's one independent; all 203 Democrats voting opposed it, and 11 Democrats did not vote. The articles accused Hegseth of waging war on Iran without authorization, extrajudicial killings in strikes on alleged drug boats, the seizure of Venezuela's Nicolás Maduro, and targeting Sen. Mark Kelly. At his first Pentagon briefing in six months, Hegseth called the effort a "farce" and "sabotage." Democrats, who take both chambers in January, have promised investigations; Hakeem Jeffries, expected to be Speaker, told NPR "He's got to go." Senate Republicans re-elected John Thune as their leader for the minority.

First Russia–Ukraine talks in nine months are "very productive," per Rubio, but core gaps remain

Delegations met for about four hours in Abu Dhabi with US envoy Jared Kushner, mainly on security guarantees for Ukraine, humanitarian issues, prisoner exchanges and the return of children; a second session is today. Zelensky called the day "constructive and quite substantive," Russia's Vladimir Medinsky voiced hope for progress, and Rubio, speaking at the G7 in France, said it is "not like something that's on the verge of being solved in the next 24 hours" and that Europeans should eventually join. Foreign Policy and ABC report the two main sticking points are unchanged: Kyiv and European allies want an immediate ceasefire before a settlement, which Moscow rejects, and Russia wants Ukraine to withdraw from four regions it claims, which Kyiv refuses. Industry sources told Reuters Russia's refining capacity is down about 30% since late October after Ukrainian drone strikes.
