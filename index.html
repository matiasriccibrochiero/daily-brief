Middle East · Energy · Markets

Trump calls the Iran war "militarily effectively over," keeps the blockade, and oil keeps falling

AP · Al Jazeera · Foreign Policy · UPI · Xinhua · CNBC · Nov 9–10
No deal announcedBlockade staysBrent −12.5% in a weekS&P +2.35%

What happened. Trump met Qatar's prime minister and foreign minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, at the White House on Monday, with Vice President JD Vance present. No agreement was announced. Afterward Trump made a surprise appearance in the briefing room: "Iran militarily is effectively over. We're just keeping it the status quo" (AP). He said military action "doesn't seem to be a very necessary thing, but we're locked and loaded," and that he expects the conflict to end "fairly soon" (Al Jazeera). The status quo he described means keeping the US naval blockade of Iranian ports and the new Task Force Iron Gate against land and Caspian supply routes, which he said Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain and Kuwait prefer. Al Thani posted that he was "grateful for the positive discussions on reaching a deal."

What's on the table. Foreign Policy reports Al Thani carried Iran's two-day proposal: on day one the US lifts the blockade and releases some frozen assets while Iran invites IAEA inspectors to its bombed nuclear sites; on day two Iran reopens the Strait of Hormuz and nuclear talks resume. Tehran attaches seven conditions, including canceling new sanctions, restoring oil waivers, recognizing a Hormuz shipping route worked out with Oman, and ending fighting on all fronts including Lebanon. Rubio called those terms "outrageous and ridiculous and really not realistic" on Sunday and said before Monday's meeting, "I wouldn't say we're at the verge of a breakthrough."

Tehran's response. Iran's messages were mixed too. President Masoud Pezeshkian said Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei wants an end to the "neither war nor peace" situation and that Iran is open to talks based on mutual respect but "will not surrender to force and bullying" (Al Jazeera, Anadolu). Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, wrote: "FOLD YOUR HAND. STOP manufacturing FAKE NEWS about imaginary talks." Shipping data cited by Forwarder News put Hormuz traffic at about 20% of prewar levels, with roughly 162 ships waiting off Fujairah and Khor Fakkan on Sunday.

Markets. Brent settled down 5.85% at $92.57 on Monday and WTI fell 4.38% to $86.85 (CNBC); Brent traded near $91.7 in Asia on Tuesday, down about 12.5% over a week and at its lowest since late August (Zee Business). Cheaper oil helped the S&P 500 to a 2.35% gain, its best day since April 8, and the Dow back above its Election Day close.

Why it matters (analysis)"Effectively over" is a statement about fighting, not a settlement: the blockade, the Hormuz restrictions and the sanctions are all still in place, and those are what move energy prices and shipping costs. Markets are pricing a lower chance of renewed strikes, which is a real change from a week ago. A durable fall in oil would require ships to move through Hormuz again, and neither side has yet given ground on the order of steps that would allow it.

Chips · National security · Washington

Nvidia falls 4.3% as the Huang probe reaches the FBI and Congress

Reuters · CNBC · AP · Anadolu · Nov 9–10
$178.67 closeFBI looking into it, per TrumpHouse letter, Dec 1 deadline

What happened. On the first trading day after Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation into whether chief executive Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, the stock closed down 4.27% at $178.67 while most chipmakers rallied: AMD rose 13.3%, Marvell 9.5%, Broadcom 8.8% and Intel 5.5% (CNBC). CNBC and Investing.com note Nvidia has now given back its gains for the year.

Washington. Trump said the FBI is looking into Huang's affair with Alice Huang, the former Megaspeed executive US officials believe passed information to Beijing, and that he spoke with the CEO, who told him he had no idea she was a spy; Trump said he believed him (Reuters). The New York Times reported that Trump had been briefed on the relationship and the intelligence and chose not to act; the White House declined to comment further. Sen. Chris Coons said he is pushing to investigate "whether our national security was compromised." Three House Foreign Affairs Democrats, Gabe Amo, Gregory Meeks and Sydney Kamlager-Dove, wrote to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick asking for a halt to advanced AI chip exports to China and for answers by December 1 on whether Commerce knew of the intelligence before approving exports (Reuters).

Other views. China's foreign ministry again called the allegations "groundless." Megaspeed has denied wrongdoing. Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives & Co. told CNBC that replacing Huang would be "comedy" but that the probe creates "a huge uncertainty." Huang told staff he is "committed to protecting NVIDIA and supporting America's national security." Reuters said it could not locate Alice Huang for comment.

Why it matters (analysis)The story has moved from a governance question inside one company to a policy question in Washington. The part with the largest practical effect is export control: Democrats take both chambers in January and are already asking Commerce to explain past licenses. Tighter rules on Southeast Asian cloud resellers would affect Nvidia's sales more directly than the outcome of the board's inquiry. Nvidia reports results on November 17.

Anthropic releases Claude Fable 5.2 to all users and Mythos 5.2 behind verification

Two tiers, one base modelExternal pre-release testing

Anthropic released two models built on the same underlying system. Fable 5.2 is available to all users, with what the company calls strong cybersecurity and biology safeguards. Mythos 5.2 offers fuller capabilities in vulnerability discovery and sensitive scientific research, and access requires applying through Anthropic's Life Sciences and Cyber Verification Programs. Anthropic says that on its FrontierCode 2.0 test, Fable 5.2 at High effort scored about level with Opus 5.5 at Max effort for roughly half the cost per task, and at its highest setting scored above any released model it tested. Pre-release testing involved the UK AI Security Institute and METR, among others. These are the company's own figures; independent benchmarks were not yet available.

Analysis: splitting one model into a public version and a gated version for dual-use work is a concrete answer to the question of how labs release systems that are useful for both defense and attack. It also makes access policy, not just raw capability, a point of competition between labs.

Meta · OpenAI · Google · Nov 9

Meta ships a $0.10 agent model; OpenAI lets one agent drive several computers

$0.10 / $0.50 per M tokens1M-token context

Meta introduced Muse Ember, its smallest model, aimed at the repetitive work agents do all day: tool calls in sequence, routing, extraction and small code fixes. It has a 1-million-token context, handles text, images, audio and video, and costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output on Meta's API; Meta claims better value than Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna, which has not been independently checked. Meta's Muse agent also arrived on Apple Watch and Wear OS. OpenAI now lets a single ChatGPT "dot" connect to several of a user's computers and pick one per task, for example testing a build on a second Mac. Google added Maps Platform app-building to AI Studio.

Analysis: the price floor for agent workloads keeps dropping, and assistants are being given access to more of a user's devices. Both trends shift attention from model quality toward permissions, reliability and cost per completed task.

US trucking spot rates run about 35% above last year as capacity shrinks and diesel stays above $6

Van linehaul +35% y/yTruck posts −33.9%Diesel $6.29

DAT's Dean Croke put spot linehaul rates 35% above a year ago for dry van, 34% for reefer and 31% for flatbed, with all-in van rates at $3.11 a mile. Load posts are 26.5% higher than last year while truck posts are 33.9% lower. Part of the lift is holiday restocking; part is structural, including FMCSA's non-domiciled CDL rule, which the agency estimates could eventually affect about 194,000 drivers. Diesel averaged $6.29 a gallon, $2.54 more than a year ago. Fleets are not adding equipment: FTR counts about 15,600 Class 8 truck orders in October, down 36%, with EPA's 2027 rules unsettled. Cold-storage operator Lineage reported a 14.9% fall in quarterly AFFO and blamed oil, trucking capacity and tariffs.

Analysis: shippers are paying more per load, carriers are only partly keeping it because of fuel, and the driver pool is flat. If oil keeps falling, carrier margins improve before shipper rates do.

5,000 glasses this year20,000+ by end-2027Dronamics ~$500M

PR Newswire · Nov 9

Coframe raises $25 million for websites and ads that test and rewrite themselves

Coframe raised a $25 million Series A co-led by Khosla Ventures and Hanabi Capital, bringing total funding to $35 million. Its system plugs into a company's site or ad campaigns and uses a team of AI agents plus a model trained on user behavior to generate, test and ship variations continuously. The company claims more than $300 million in incremental customer revenue and a 39% experiment win rate; those figures are its own. It says it is also optimizing pages for AI agents that shop on people's behalf, including through a collaboration on ChatGPT ads. Analysis: it is a bet that A/B testing becomes a continuous automated process, and that a growing share of site visitors will be software rather than people.

US October inflation is due today, without an annual rate

Headline est. +0.3% m/mCore est. +0.2%8:30 ET / 10:30 ART

Economists expect consumer prices rose 0.3% in October and core prices 0.2%, after 0.6% and 0.3% in September, mostly because gasoline eased. The Bureau of Labor Statistics will not publish official year-over-year rates this month because October 2025 prices were not collected during last year's government shutdown. It is the last CPI report before the Fed's December meeting. Analysis: with oil down sharply this week, markets will look past energy to services and food for signs of whether the war-driven price spike is spreading.

Massie forces a House vote on impeaching Defense Secretary Hegseth by Wednesday

8 articles3 GOP votes neededSenate needs two-thirds

Rep. Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican leaving Congress, introduced eight articles of impeachment on Monday, mainly accusing Hegseth of waging war on Iran without congressional authorization, with references to Yemen and Venezuela. Because it was filed as privileged, House leaders must act within two legislative days, either with a vote or a motion to table it. Democrats are broadly in favor; House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries is a "hard yes," and at least three Republicans would need to join. Congressional sources told Reuters it may have the votes to pass, though conviction in the Senate, which requires two-thirds, looks unlikely. Trump defended Hegseth: "He won two wars. I don't know how you can impeach him." Speaker Mike Johnson said impeachment "in the middle of hostilities abroad" would send a bad signal. Only two Cabinet secretaries have been impeached, in 1876 and 2024.

Russia–Ukraine talks resume in Abu Dhabi as a US official cites private "flexibility" on Donetsk
