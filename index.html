Anthropic files to go public, and the prospectus is mostly a story about compute

Anthropic S-1 (SEC) · TechCrunch · CNBC · Fortune · Reuters · Oct 26
$16.29B H1 revenue~85% implied gross margin$542–567B compute commitmentsFounders hold 50.1% of votes

After confidentially filing in June, Anthropic published its 318-page S-1 on Monday. It plans to list Class A shares on Nasdaq under ANT, with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading a syndicate of more than 20 banks. It has not said how much it will raise or at what price. CNBC notes it would follow SpaceX's June listing, which raised $85.7 billion and is the largest IPO on record.

The numbers. Revenue was $16.29 billion in the six months to June 30, up 1,304% from $1.16 billion. Q2 alone was $11.6 billion (Fortune). Cost of revenue was $2.38 billion, which implies a gross margin of about 85%, against roughly 12% a year earlier (CNBC's calculation). The operating loss narrowed to $693 million from $2.1 billion. The net loss was $80.7 billion, but about $72 billion of that is a non-cash charge from revaluing convertible notes as the company's valuation rose. About 80% of revenue is usage-based. Net revenue retention was around 900% as of Sept. 30; the median public cloud software company was 108% in January, per Altimeter's Jamin Ball. More than 11,900 customers spend over $100,000 a year, about 1,900 spend over $1 million, and roughly 250 spend over $10 million. No customer is above 10% of revenue, though the top five are about a quarter. International revenue was $4.5 billion, 27.8% of the total. Headcount is 5,300, and the company says Claude wrote more than 90% of its code contributions in September.

The obligations. Anthropic has committed to between $542 billion and $567 billion of future compute and infrastructure, much of it non-cancelable and payable whether or not it is used (Fortune, Reuters). The filing lists $110 billion with Amazon through 2036, $114.5 billion on Google TPUs through 2033, at least $44.9 billion with Microsoft through 2033, and up to $120.5 billion with SpaceX through 2029. At full delivery, the SpaceX deal is about $3.1 billion a month. It is also starting "Anthropic Direct," leased data centers run by partners, with $125.2 billion of rent over five years. Compute was its largest cost at $5.6 billion in Q2, so it earned a little over $2 for each $1 of compute that quarter.

Governance and risks. The seven co-founders will hold 50.1% of voting power through a "Founder LLC," and the Long-Term Benefit Trust will appoint most of the board. Anthropic says this protects its safety mission. TechCrunch notes the controversy: public shareholders would have little practical control over a company that, in its own words, is building technology with "catastrophic or existential risks." Anthropic itself says the structure "has no direct public company precedent" and makes takeover bids less likely. Insider lock-ups open in stages at 27, 48, 62 and 76 days, instead of the usual 180. The risk factors run to more than 90 pages. They include the Pentagon's "supply chain risk" designation, which a California court found illegal but the D.C. Circuit upheld; the government appealed the California ruling on Monday. They also cover dependence on TSMC, state data-center moratoriums, and safeguards against weapons misuse that the filing calls "incomplete." CNBC also confirmed that the FTC has asked Anthropic executives to testify in its consumer-safety probe of AI companies.

Reaction. PrimerIQ and AP linked Monday's chip-led rally to the filing, after two losing weeks for the S&P 500. Supporters such as Redpoint's Brescia called it a "blockbuster business," while Axios's Dan Primack read the filing as a pivot to using public markets to fund compute. MIT's Sinan Aral said valuations of $1.5–2 trillion being discussed would mean 22–30 times revenue (that range is market talk, not a figure from the filing). The timing is tense: CNBC notes that neocloud stocks such as CoreWeave and Nebius lost over 30% in October, and Firmus pulled its IPO.

Why it matters (analysis)This is the first full public look at a frontier AI lab's finances, and it settles some questions while sharpening others. Usage-based revenue and very high gross margin suggest that paying customers are real and growing. But the business has committed to spend more than 30 times its first-half revenue on compute, much of it non-cancelable, so its results are now tied to whether demand keeps up with capacity it has already booked. The same few companies (Amazon, Google, Microsoft) are its investors, suppliers, distributors and competitors, which is unusual for a public company. How investors price the deal will also signal how much appetite public markets have for funding AI infrastructure at a time when neocloud and data-center listings are struggling.

Brazil · LatAm markets

Brazil's market has its worst day since 2020 as investors unwind the bet on Flávio

Reuters via Yahoo Finance · Valor International · G1 · CNN Brasil · O Globo · Axios · Oct 26–27
Ibovespa −13.88%Dollar +8.2% to R$5.405y CDS 120 → 144 bpAnnulment bid dismissed by justices

The first trading day after Lula's re-election was a broad sell-off. B3 halted trading for 30 minutes when the Ibovespa fell 10% at the open, the first circuit breaker since 2020. At its low the index was down more than 17%, and it closed 13.88% lower at 176,608. The dollar opened up as much as 12.5% at R$5.60 (CNN Brasil) and closed 8.2% higher at about R$5.40. Valor calls that the biggest one-day gain against the real since 1999, and Reuters since at least 2002. The January 2035 DI rate briefly topped 16% and closed at 15.19%, with interest-rate futures up more than 260 bp. Five-year CDS rose to 144 bp from 120 bp, and the Treasury cancelled bond auctions planned for Oct. 27 and 29.

Domestic and rate-sensitive stocks fell hardest. Per G1, Magazine Luiza fell 58%, Vamos 57% and Hapvida 55%, and PrimerIQ shows homebuilder Cyrela down 42%. Banco do Brasil fell 27% and Bradesco as much as 22%. Exporters gained from the weaker real: Suzano rose 10%, Embraer 9% and Vale 4%. Raw sugar fell 5.6% because the currency move made Brazilian supply cheaper.

Why the move was so large. The market had positioned for the other result. After Flávio Bolsonaro led the first round, the Ibovespa rose about 7% and the real nearly 5% on expectations of fiscal tightening. Oxford Economics' Mauricio Monge says Lula's main task is to convince markets he can deliver "a credible, sustainable fiscal adjustment" while prioritising wages and jobs. PrimerIQ puts the budget gap above 9% of GDP. Valor reports Lula will need to bring a centre-right bloc into the cabinet, and O Globo says the market is now pressing him on fiscal adjustment. Axios quotes the view that Brazil will "muddle through," with markets rather than policy imposing discipline.

The political side. Flávio still has not conceded and wants the election annulled over the two-hour extension of voting. O Globo reports that STF and TSE justices dismissed the request, noting the extension did not materially affect a 4.4-million-vote margin and recognising Lula's win.

Why it matters (analysis)A one-day 8% currency move and a 14% equity drop in a G20 economy are rare, and they raise borrowing costs for the government, companies and households at once. The cancelled bond auctions and record rate swings show the strain is in funding markets, not just stocks. What happens next depends mainly on the fiscal plan Lula presents and the make-up of his cabinet, then on the central bank's Copom meeting on Nov. 3–4. Spillover to neighbours came through bonds first, with Argentine country risk up 8% on the day.

ChatGPT Work · Codex · APIResponses API for tools

Fortune · Mastercard · Stripe · Oct 21–26

Card networks say consumer agents are already shopping at scale, and payments infrastructure is adjusting

Mastercard Agent Pay for Machinesx402Stripe Treasury for agents

Mastercard's head of product Jorn Lambert told Fortune that agentic commerce is at "a bit of a turning point." A year ago, he said, the industry expected developer protocols such as MCP and ACP to drive it. Instead, mass-market personal agents like Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots brought consumers in faster. Mastercard's Agent Pay now covers retailers and some government services such as parking tickets. Its Agent Pay for Machines runs on x402, the open payment protocol Coinbase revived, which can settle by card, debit or stablecoin and makes micropayments practical. Separately, Stripe launched Treasury for agents on Oct. 21. It lets agents such as ChatGPT and Claude check balances, pay invoices, move funds and convert currencies, with businesses choosing which actions need human approval; spending limits arrive "in the coming weeks."

The adoption claim comes from Mastercard and is not backed by published volume figures in the article. Still, payment networks and processors are building controls for agents as distinct payers, with limits and human approval, rather than treating agent traffic as ordinary card-not-present traffic.

IBM · HashiCorp · Kodem · Dark Reading · Oct 26

Agent identity becomes a product category as IBM ships Agent Identity and runtime policy tools spread

IBM Agent Identity GATask-scoped tokensPer-agent runtime policy

At TechXchange, IBM made Agent Identity generally available. It registers agents in a central inventory with owners and risk ratings, carries on-behalf-of delegation across identity providers so an action traces back to a person or approval, and issues short-lived, task-scoped OAuth tokens instead of standing access. It ties into HashiCorp Vault for credentials. Security firm Kodem added runtime enforcement that blocks an individual agent or tool call that steps outside its policy. Dark Reading's write-up of the in-the-wild AGENTS.md attack, covered here yesterday, shows the threat these tools target: instruction files that redirect coding agents.

Large vendors now sell agents as their own kind of identity, separate from users and service accounts. Delegation records and per-task permissions are the controls auditors and regulators are likely to ask for as agents take actions in production systems.

Fed meeting starts today: a hold is expected, the debate is about December

The FOMC begins its two-day meeting on Tuesday and decides on Wednesday afternoon (ET). Markets broadly expect Chair Kevin Warsh to hold the target range at 3.75%–4.00% after September's 25 bp hike; futures put the chance of another hike this week under 10%. The focus is on whether guidance points to another increase in December and how the vote splits. The ECB (deposit rate 2.50%) decides Thursday and the Bank of Japan (1.25%) Friday, both expected to hold. Microsoft and Alphabet report Wednesday after the close and Amazon Thursday. On Monday, Verizon beat on earnings but missed on revenue and fell, and Cadence raised its 2026 growth outlook to 21% with an $8.2 billion backlog.

Oil swung on diplomacy, not supply: prices dipped on reports of talks in Tehran, then recovered because Houthi attacks on Saudi facilities continued. Energy-driven inflation is why a hiking bias is still on the table even as growth slows.

Infobae · La Nación · Rava · Oct 26

The Brazil shock reaches Argentina through bonds and ADRs while the peso moves modestly

Country risk rose 8.2% to 765 bp, above the 720–750 bp range some analysts had floated on Sunday. Argentine shares listed in New York fell as much as 12% and the Merval lost about 3%. The official dollar rose only $10 to $1,560, with the blue at $1,575 and the CCL at $1,682.65. La Nación notes Mercado Libre, which earns a large share of revenue in Brazil, was among the stocks under pressure.

The pattern from last week holds: the BCRA's dollar purchases keep the currency steady, while external financing costs rise. A higher country risk makes it harder for Argentina to refinance next year's debt in markets, and the Brazilian real's slide makes Brazilian goods cheaper for Argentine buyers.

Iran: regional envoys meet in Tehran as Trump weighs "a deal" or more strikes

Israel votes today in its first election since October 7, with polls pointing to deadlock

Polls are open from 7am to 10pm local time (2am to 5pm ART), with about 7.3 million eligible voters. A Haaretz average of the main TV channel polls put Netanyahu's bloc at 50 seats and Gadi Eisenkot's opposition at 54, both short of the 61 needed. Exit polls come right after polls close. If no bloc can form a government, Netanyahu's cabinet stays on as caretaker.

A hung result would mean weeks of coalition talks during a live regional war, which matters for Israel's posture toward Iran and for the oil risk premium.
