Semiconductors · Governance

Nvidia's board weighs removing Jensen Huang, its founder of 33 years, as a second affair is reported

WSJ · New York Times · Reuters · Semafor · Bloomberg · TechCrunch · Nov 11–12
~−8% after hours11 directorsHuang owns ~3.6%Results Nov 17

What happened. On Wednesday evening, The New York Times, citing five people who had worked at the company, reported that Huang had a relationship with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of health care, who reports directly to him. The Wall Street Journal then reported that the board was preparing to oust him and would meet as soon as that night; Reuters confirmed a late meeting took place, but no decision had been announced by Thursday morning. Semafor reported that some directors favor CFO Colette Kress as interim CEO and that the board briefed the White House; Bloomberg reported Nvidia has hired an outside search firm, and Semafor said Apple hardware chief Johny Srouji is among people being considered. Nvidia has not commented.

How it got here. The board opened an independent investigation on Sunday after reports that US intelligence believes Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive with whom Huang acknowledged an affair, is a Chinese operative who helped arrange chip shipments to Chinese companies; Nvidia, Megaspeed and Beijing deny wrongdoing. Huang told employees he takes full responsibility. Senators Chris Coons and Elizabeth Warren asked the Commerce and State departments and the White House to preserve documents on Nvidia's exports and on Huang's contacts, writing that officials may have known of concerns "yet did not move to block the continued flow of advanced AI chips."

The mechanics. Nvidia's bylaws let a majority of its 11 directors remove the CEO with or without cause, and all directors except Huang are classed as independent. In practice it is harder: Huang, 63, owns about 3.6% of the company, has said he has around 60 direct reports, and has never named a successor. If the after-hours drop holds, Nvidia will have lost roughly $600 billion in market value since the investigation began (The Next Web). Taiwan's Taiex fell 2.6% this morning and European chip stocks including ASML and ASM International fell 2.5% to 2.9%, while AMD and Broadcom rose after hours.

Why it matters (analysis)Nvidia supplies most of the chips used to train frontier AI models and has signed billions of dollars of financing deals that underpin the data-center buildout, so a leadership change reaches well beyond one stock. Analysts quoted by Euronews expect little short-term effect on the business if the order book holds, and Quilter Cheviot's Ben Barringer said Huang could stay on in a reduced role. The open questions are who would run product strategy and customer relationships that have centered on one person, and whether the export-control inquiry widens. The company reports quarterly results on November 17, leaving the board little time.

Russia · Ukraine · Diplomacy

Abu Dhabi talks end with a planned prisoner swap and no breakthrough; US envoys meet Putin today

AP · BBC · ABC · RFE/RL · Kyiv Independent · Nov 11
First trilateral round since Feb>1,000 each side, per TASSMoscow, then Dublin

What happened. The second day of US-mediated talks lasted under two hours. Both delegations confirmed an agreement "in principle," in Russian negotiator Vladimir Medinsky's words, on a large prisoner exchange; Ukraine's Rustem Umerov said details and implementation are still being worked out. Russian state agency TASS said it would be the largest ever, with more than 1,000 people on each side; Zelensky had floated 1,000-for-1,000 or 1,500-for-1,500 a day earlier. Neither side has confirmed numbers or timing. Jared Kushner called the round a "positive step" and said he and Steve Witkoff travel to Moscow on Thursday to meet Vladimir Putin, then to Dublin to brief Zelensky.

The positions. The talks were framed around Europe's post-war "security architecture." For Kyiv that means guarantees allies would come to its aid if attacked again; for Moscow, limits on NATO in eastern Europe (BBC). Ukraine's Davyd Arakhamia called it "one of the hardest" rounds and said the Russians came "with very strict directives." Medinsky said "in some areas we found common ground, while in others we did not," and dismissed a role for European governments, which consulted with the US separately in Abu Dhabi: "We have not received a single peace initiative or a single concrete peace proposal from European countries." Zelensky said Europe "must be represented at every stage of real diplomacy." The two core gaps reported earlier in the week, an immediate ceasefire and Russia's demand that Ukraine leave four regions, were not publicly narrowed.

On the ground. The sides traded large drone attacks during the talks. Kyiv Independent reported Russian strikes on six major power substations causing outages, and a strike on a market in Odesa Oblast that killed at least four; Ukrainian drones reportedly targeted Russia's largest oil refinery and a Baltic Sea port.

Why it matters (analysis)Prisoner exchanges have been the one area where the two sides repeatedly deliver, so the swap is a measurable result but not a signal on territory or a ceasefire. Today's Putin meeting is the next test of whether Washington can move Moscow on sequencing. Energy markets watch the refinery strikes: industry sources told Reuters this week that Russian refining capacity is down about 30% since late October.

CoreWeave jumps 24.7% after promising profit in 2028 and saying it won't borrow unsecured at today's yields

Q3 revenue $3.66BBacklog $132.5BDebt $46.9B2026 capex $37–39B

The AI cloud provider, which had fallen more than 40% in a month as it became the focus of worries about debt-funded data centers, reported third-quarter revenue up nearly 170% to $3.66 billion, above the $3.55 billion consensus, and a smaller-than-expected loss of 95 cents a share (LSEG via Reuters). It raised 2026 revenue guidance to $13.0–13.3 billion and said it expects positive adjusted net income in the first half of 2028. Its backlog rose to $132.5 billion from $104 billion in June, while quarterly capital spending rose to $14.5 billion from $9.4 billion. CEO Mike Intrator said it is largely sold out through next year, with $9 billion of customer prepayments due over three months, and that it expects to raise less than $5 billion of additional capital through 2027. He told Reuters the Democratic midterm win means more scrutiny and slower permitting but not a material change in costs.

Analysis: the company addressed the specific fear, refinancing at 5% Treasury yields, by tying new borrowing to signed contracts. Skeptics note the backlog is concentrated in a few large AI customers, and the stock gave back about 6% after hours on the Nvidia news.

Also in AI: Google's cheap "decisions" API, an AI-checked proof of Signal's encryption, and a $740,000 bill from stolen keys

Google launched a Gemini Decisions API whose first model, Gemini Decision 1, returns predefined answers with confidence scores for up to 1,000 closed-ended questions at once across text, images, audio and video, priced at $0.10 per million input tokens with 150–250 ms responses, per the company. DeepMind said its AlphaProof Code verified specified security properties of Signal's encryption code in four weeks; it covers defined properties, not every possible attack. DeepMind also released AlphaGenome 2 for non-commercial research. Railway's breach report says attackers obtained customer secrets potentially affecting up to 100,000 workspaces, a maximum-exposure estimate, and that stolen credentials ran up about $740,000 in unauthorized AI usage. United Airlines said it will block personal AI agents from booking on its site, citing privacy and possible FAA and DOT rule issues (as summarized by trckr.ai).

Analysis: the Railway figure illustrates a newer risk: leaked AI API keys convert directly into spending, which makes key management a cost-control issue as well as a security one.

Singles' Day sales grow 4.8% to 1.78 trillion yuan, a third of last year's pace, as chatbots take a bigger role

¥1.78T ($267B)+4.8% vs +14.2%Oct 7–Nov 11AI token packs >10x

Data provider Syntun said sales across major platforms during the October 7 to November 11 promotion rose 4.8% to 1.78 trillion yuan, against 14.2% growth last year, amid a property downturn, weak income expectations and job insecurity (Reuters). Alibaba and JD again published no headline totals. JD said it sold more than 50 million AI-related products, transaction value for AI PCs, glasses and recording devices more than doubled, and sales of large-language-model token packages rose more than tenfold. Reuters said it was unclear how much the platforms' shopping chatbots, which recommend and complete purchases, actually added to sales. On the logistics side, JD reported robot-processed orders up 45% year on year, and Guangzhou customs cleared more than 42 million cross-border shipments during the period. Analyst Liu Xingliang said that when a sale runs for weeks, "the sense that 'I have to grab deals tonight' fades."

Analysis: growth in a stretched-out event is a weak signal of household demand; the AI angle is so far more visible in what is sold (devices, compute credits) than in measurable conversion gains.

Britain's Fractile raises $720 million to ship inference chips with memory stacked on top

$720M Series C"Over £5B" valuationShips 2027

Fractile's round was led by Thrive Capital, Founders Fund, Lightspeed and Redpoint and backed by the UK's Sovereign AI Fund, which the government said would give Britain "a greater stake in the hardware that will underpin its future economy." The government put the valuation at over £5 billion; the FT and a Reuters source reported about $7 billion. After a pivot, Fractile's design stacks ordinary DRAM directly on each chip to ease the trade-off between memory capacity and speed that long agent workloads strain, and it pairs the hardware with its own compiler. CEO Walter Goodwin said the money will let it ship its first chips and systems next year. Analysis: inference-specific chip startups such as Etched, Axelera and Fractile are now raising at sizes once reserved for model labs, on the bet that running models, not training them, becomes the larger compute market. None has yet shipped at scale.

Merval falls a fourth session as dollar bonds rebound; October inflation due at 16:00, seen near 2%

Merval −0.6%Riesgo país 852Mayorista $1,544REM 1.8%

On the first session back from the holidays, the MSCI Merval fell 0.6% to 83,576, its fourth straight decline and a new low in dollar terms since October 24, while Globales and Bonares rose about 1% on average and up to 3.2% in longer foreign-law bonds as they caught up with Monday and Tuesday's gains abroad (Infobae). Democrat Scott Colom wins Mississippi's Senate seat, widening the incoming majority to 55, as a December 11 funding deadline nears

55 Democratic seatsAlaska undecidedFunding runs out Dec 11

Colom narrowly defeated Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith and becomes Mississippi's first Black US senator since Reconstruction (AP), giving Democrats 55 seats in the next Senate with Alaska still uncalled. Separately, a watchdog filed an FEC complaint alleging a Democratic-aligned nonprofit that spent nearly $20 million in Mississippi, Kansas and Alaska hid its donors (Washington Post).
