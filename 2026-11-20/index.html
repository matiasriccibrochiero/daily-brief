Markets · AI trade

Anthropic's 17% debut doesn't stop a global selloff in AI stocks

CNBC · AP · BBC · NPR · Yahoo Finance · Aju Press · Nov 19–20
ANT close $97.17846M shares tradedVIX ~28.4Dow −6.2% this week

The debut. Anthropic opened on the Nasdaq late Thursday morning at $111.50, 34% above its $83 IPO price, briefly touched about $115 and closed at $97.17, up 17.1% (CNBC, Newsworks). Over 846 million shares changed hands, compared with 522 million on SpaceX's first day. CNBC put the fully diluted market value at about $1.8 trillion at the close, making it the seventh most valuable US company. Other outlets cite different share counts: the BBC says about $1.76 trillion, and Yahoo Finance gives a lower basic-share figure. Shares were indicated about 6% lower in Friday premarket trading (CNBC).

The market around it. Indexes were up early on the IPO pricing, with the Nasdaq up as much as 2.1%, then fell to session lows almost as soon as ANT started trading. The Nasdaq ended down 2.25% at 23,879.20, the S&P 500 down 2.02% at 7,175.83 (its sixth straight loss and lowest close since April 29, about 8.2% below its October 6 record), and the Dow down 1,101 points to 49,097.06 (CNBC, Portfolio Terminal). Nvidia fell 3.3% to a 52-week low, AMD lost 7.7% after a short-seller report (see AI), and the VIX volatility index hit nearly 29, its highest since March. Week to date the Dow is down 6.2% and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq about 5% each, on course for their worst weeks in more than a year.

Asia on Friday. The Nikkei fell 5.06% to 56,436.65, with SoftBank Group down 14.5%, its worst day since August 2024. Korea's Kospi fell 5.21% to 4,869.96, and Seoul paused program selling on both its main and junior markets. Aju Press reports Taiwan's Taiex down 6.63% to 35,877.63; CNBC says it is heading for its steepest weekly fall since October 1997. China's CSI 300 lost only 0.76%, and Alibaba rose about 2.4% after strong earnings. European tech stocks opened about 3% lower, with ASML down 5.8%.

Why markets sold off: the competing explanations. Strategists quoted by CNBC called it "sell the news" profit-taking once the event was over. Interactive Brokers' Steve Sosnick said selling picked up after the S&P 500 broke the 7,300 support level, and that "sell the rally" has replaced "buy the dip." Others point to a run of specific worries: a likely Fed rate hike in December (CME FedWatch about 95%, per CNBC), Nvidia's CEO turmoil and a new California subpoena, export-control questions about chipmakers, and Anthropic's own disclosure of about $901 billion in compute purchase commitments. Deutsche Bank's Binky Chadha says the market is now "very oversold" while growth and earnings stay strong, and ARK's Brett Winton argues Anthropic's margins show AI spending is paying off.

Why it matters (analysis)Investors bought Anthropic, but not the wider AI trade. Money went into the newest, fastest-growing lab while suppliers and backers of its rivals were sold: Nvidia, AMD, SoftBank (a major OpenAI investor) and Asian chipmakers. A 17% pop is solid but well below the opening spike, which tells you buyers paid a premium for scarce access, not that they are paying any price for AI. Next week matters: US markets close for Thanksgiving on Thursday and have a half day Friday, so liquidity will be thin while worries about rates and chips are unresolved.

Geopolitics · Diplomacy

The Shenzhen summit ends with a four-point declaration; Ukraine's security guarantees move to Berlin

White House · Axios · Reuters · BBC · AP · RFE/RL · Al Arabiya · Nov 19–20
First US–China–Russia summitNo arms-control dealUkraine, Iran not mentioned

What was signed. The first-ever three-way meeting of the US, Chinese and Russian leaders produced a short "Declaration toward Peace" (White House). It covers four areas: support for UN reform; higher production and supply of refined petroleum products; "respect for immunities of State officials," which Axios reads as a rebuke of the International Criminal Court, which issued a 2023 arrest warrant for Putin; and a restated principle that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought," with talks on nuclear risk and proliferation. It sets no weapons limits and no timetable for talks, and it does not mention the wars in Ukraine or Iran (Axios, Reuters).

How each side framed it. Trump's stated goal was denuclearization. Asked what headline he wanted, he told Fox News he'd "already gotten the headline" and praised a "friendship relationship" with China. He also said Putin "would like to see an end to that war," though no progress on Ukraine was reported (Axios). Axios reported that US allies were uneasy about the meeting beforehand. Analysts had not expected an arms-control breakthrough.

Berlin. The substantive Ukraine talks took place in Berlin, where US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Ukrainian and European leaders. Kushner said he believed Russia was "comfortable" with Article 5-style guarantees, under which an attack on Ukraine would trigger a collective defense response, and that Trump was open to them as part of a broader package (AP). Germany, France, the UK and Ukraine issued a joint statement calling for legally binding guarantees, including US-supported monitoring and a multinational force (German Chancellery, RFE/RL). On Friday, Russia's deputy foreign minister rejected the idea, calling any "quasi-membership" of NATO for Ukraine unacceptable (Al Arabiya).

Why it matters (analysis)Shenzhen was mostly symbolic, and the one practical item, more refined fuel supply, targets the oil shock from the Iran conflict. On Ukraine, the main open question is now explicit: Washington and Europe are discussing guarantees close to NATO's, and Moscow publicly rejects them. Whether Kushner's reading of Russia's position or the Russian foreign ministry's statement holds will decide whether planned US–Russia–Ukraine talks go anywhere. Supporters of the summit say getting the three leaders at one table is progress in itself. Critics say it gave Putin a platform without anything in return.

Short seller Hunterbrook targets AMD over firmware found in blacklisted Chinese servers; AMD falls 7.7%

AMD −7.72%IEIT blacklisted Mar 2025

Hunterbrook Media reported that IEIT Systems, a Chinese server maker placed on the US trade blacklist in March 2025, had shipped server updates containing at least six newer versions of AMD firmware. Its affiliated fund, Hunterbrook Capital, disclosed a short position in AMD. AMD said the report misrepresents its practices and that it complies with US export rules. Several outlets note that the findings don't show AMD supplied the firmware or broke the law; the key question is whether licenses applied (Stock Analysis, Aju Press). Separately, California's attorney general sent Nvidia an investigative subpoena on whether its advanced chips or confidential information reached China and whether investors were misled (CNBC).

Why it matters: export compliance has become a market risk for the whole chip sector, not just Nvidia. The report helped push down Asian chipmakers on Friday.

The Information (reported) · Seeking Alpha · Nov 19

Nvidia is reportedly close to buying diffusion-model startup Inception for at least $500 million

Per The Information, Nvidia is in advanced talks to acquire Inception, maker of the Mercury "diffusion" language models, for at least $500 million plus performance-based earnouts. Nothing has been signed or announced. Most language models write text one token at a time. Diffusion models refine a whole passage in parallel, and Inception says this makes Mercury up to 10 times faster and cheaper to run. The talks reportedly began before Jensen Huang's resignation; Nvidia's venture arm had backed Inception's $50 million seed round.

Why it matters: it would be a sign that Nvidia is buying model research to make inference cheaper on its hardware, while its leadership and China compliance are under scrutiny.

PMA · FreightWaves · Seatrade Maritime · APL Logistics · Nov 18–20

LA–Long Beach port talks resume today, hours before the watchmen's contract expires

~250 watchmenExpires 05:01 ART Sat85% of Long Beach cargo

ILWU Local 26 and the Pacific Maritime Association go back to the table today, Friday. The contract extension ends at 12:01 a.m. Pacific on Saturday (05:01 ART), and the union has a strike vote and has signaled it won't extend again. No strike had started as of the latest updates (APL Logistics). The PMA says its offer would raise average full-time pay from more than $170,000 to $198,000 and include a $3,200 one-time bonus. Local 26 president Luisa Gratz told Seatrade the union had made a counterproposal the employers "refused to respond" to, and called the PMA's pay figures inaccurate. The watchmen are about 2% of the ports' ILWU workforce, but they work at terminals handling about 85% of Long Beach and 33% of Los Angeles cargo, and the PMA expects longshore workers to honor any picket line.

Why it matters: a picket could stop most of Long Beach's cargo during the last stretch of holiday shipping, while US truckload spot rates are already up more than 50% from a year ago (FreightWaves). The outcome is not yet known; check tomorrow's brief.

Revenue $188.8B, +5.2%US e-com +23%Ads +37%

TechCrunch · Nov 19

Everful raises $48.5M to manage factory-direct sourcing from China for smaller retailers

Everful, based in San Francisco and Singapore, raised a Series A led by Vertex Ventures SEA & India and January Capital, with Square Peg and Lightspeed participating. It handles the whole process for retailers without sourcing teams: finding suppliers, consolidating orders, quality control and logistics. It uses AI agents for trend tracking, finding suppliers and coordinating factories. Co-founder Sisi Song, formerly of Alibaba, says it is on track for $100 million in annualized revenue by year-end, ships over 5 million items a month to more than 60 countries and works with over 30,000 factories (company figures). She argues that the end of the US de minimis exemption favors "cross-border B2B sourcing plus local selling" over direct-to-consumer cross-border shipping.

Intuit sells Mailchimp to Bending Spoons for $3.68B, less than a third of the $12B it paid in 2021

Bending Spoons, the Milan-based company that buys mature software businesses and is backed by Berkshire Hathaway, will pay $3.31 billion in cash plus $368 million in newly issued Bending Spoons shares, with closing expected in early 2027. Mailchimp's revenue fell 1% to $313 million last quarter, and Intuit expects it to be flat to down 1% for the year. Bending Spoons bought Airtable in September at about 10% of its former valuation and also owns Evernote, Vimeo, WeTransfer, Eventbrite and AOL. Separately, Intuit beat first-quarter estimates ($2.91 adjusted EPS on $4.35 billion revenue), but its full-year guidance was below forecasts, and the stock fell 15% after hours (CNBC).

Why it matters: 2021-era software acquisitions are now being sold at large losses, and companies like Bending Spoons are buying these slow-growth products to cut costs and run them for cash.
