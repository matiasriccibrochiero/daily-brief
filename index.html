Space · Launch infrastructure

SpaceX catches a Starship upper stage with its tower for the first time, after a full day in orbit

SpaceX · Space.com · Aviation Week · The Verge · Aerospace America · Nov 14
Flight 15 · Ship 4214 orbits26 Starlink V3 deployedBooster lost in Gulf

What happened. Starship lifted off from Starbase, Texas, at 4:47 pm Central on Friday. The plan was to bring both stages back and catch them with the launch tower's "chopstick" arms, which would have been the first full recovery of an orbital rocket. The Super Heavy booster relit 32 of 33 engines for its return burn, but the burn ended early on low fuel. SpaceX says its guidance system triggered an automatic abort and diverted it offshore, where its flight termination system fired after the landing burn; the root cause is under investigation. The upper stage reached orbit, deployed 26 next-generation Starlink V3 satellites, and coasted for about 20 hours. On Saturday it relit one engine to deorbit, crossed Baja California and northern Mexico, and lowered itself into the tower's arms at 4:20 pm Eastern (18:20 ART), 22.5 hours after launch. "That was the missing link," SpaceX commentator Dan Huot said on the webcast.

Why the ship catch is different. SpaceX had caught the booster on three earlier flights, but never the ship. The upper stage comes back from orbit, much faster and hotter, and has to survive reentry on about 18,000 heat-shield tiles before it can be caught (Aerospace America). This was only the second Starship flight to reach orbit, after Flight 14 on September 28.

Why it matters (analysis)Recovering both stages intact is the premise behind Starship's projected cost per launch, which in turn underpins plans such as the 100,000-satellite Starlink constellation SpaceX has described and NASA's lunar lander contract. Saturday shows the hardest half of that is possible; it does not yet show it is repeatable, or how much refurbishment a caught ship needs before flying again. The booster loss is a reminder that the earlier booster catches are not yet routine. Huot's "holy grail" framing is the company's; independent observers will judge by turnaround times on the next flights.

One router, 1,000 connection attempts every five seconds: officials trace the US ground stop to an L3Harris upgrade

US DOT · Reuters · CNBC · AP · USA Today · Nov 14
20,000+ flights disrupted6,400+ canceled Sat875 Sun alreadyNormal by late Sun (FAA)

The cause, as officials describe it. At a Saturday briefing, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said an L3Harris contractor was doing routine work at "at least one" remote FAA site, moving telecom lines from copper wire that was being discontinued to fiber, which also meant moving from older TDM circuits to IP networking. L3Harris owns the network the FAA uses to control US airspace and has run it for more than 20 years. A newly installed router "started flooding other routers" with more than 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds, Duffy said, which disrupted communication with aircraft and some radar feeds, so the FAA shut the airspace. Officials told Reuters there may have been problems with routers in both Arkansas and Texas. Duffy said investigators have not determined whether it was installation error or a faulty router. FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford said there was "zero evidence" of anything "nefarious" or AI-related. L3Harris said it is "supporting the FAA in its investigation."

Recovery. FlightAware counted more than 6,400 US cancellations and 8,000 delays on Saturday as of 9:35 pm ET, after about 9,200 cancellations on Friday; some of Friday's disruption was weather (CNBC). Reuters tallies more than 20,000 flights disrupted since Friday. Bedford apologized, said "the worst is behind us," and expects airline schedules to be largely back to normal by late Sunday. House Speaker Mike Johnson backed Duffy's request for more air traffic control funding, saying "we need a lot more."

Why it matters (analysis)The explanation moves the story from "aging system fails" to "upgrade of an aging system fails," which cuts two ways. Supporters of the $30–50 billion modernization requests can point to how much of the network still runs on discontinued copper. Critics will ask why a single site's change could cascade nationwide, which is a network-design and change-management question as much as a funding one. The findings are preliminary and come from the government, which is also a party to the contract.

Four nonprofits sue five AI labs in California, asking a court to freeze development of more capable models

SF Superior CourtUnfair Competition Law5 defendants

Public Citizen, the Irreplaceable Education Fund, Heat Initiative and The Alliance for Secure AI sued Anthropic, Google, Meta, OpenAI and SpaceX Super Intelligence. They ask for an injunction barring the companies from building or deploying systems more capable than today's most advanced models, and from letting AI systems autonomously direct AI research. The complaint cites risks including AI-enabled cyberattacks, bioweapons and loss of control, and rests on a single claim under California's Unfair Competition Law (AP, Public Citizen). The companies had not filed responses in the coverage reviewed; industry defenders argue such claims lack a legal basis. Analysis: a court ordering a halt is widely seen as unlikely, but the case could force discovery into safety practices and adds a state-court front to a debate that has so far played out in legislatures.

Argentina

Policy push
TN · Infobae · Boletín Oficial · Nov 13–14

With inflation at 2.2% and activity weak, the government speeds up road concessions and approves two Vaca Muerta projects over $3 billion

27 road sections16 provincesRIGI: US$3B+AySA: US$340M Dec 17

What happened. A resolution in Friday's Boletín Oficial authorized tendering 27 more sections of national highways in 16 provinces as toll concessions, adding to the 9,000 km already concessioned this year; private operators would take on maintenance and expansion in exchange for tolls (TN). The government also approved two large projects under the RIGI investment regime tied to Vaca Muerta: a natural-gas-liquids processing plant and pipelines from the Neuquén basin to Bahía Blanca, together worth more than US$3 billion, which it says will create 19,000 jobs from 2030. The handover of the state's AySA water-utility shares to the winning consortium was set for December 17, pushing back US$340 million in proceeds by a few weeks.

Context and views. October inflation of 2.2% was the highest monthly reading since April, though Economy Minister Luis Caputo highlighted core inflation at a 15-month low. TN reports private analysts and business leaders are worried about falling purchasing power and weak domestic demand; consultancy LCG projects inflation stabilizing slightly below 2% and says a 1% target will be hard to reach, especially with exchange-rate pressure likely before the October 2027 election. The government maintains that fiscal and monetary order must come before a sustained recovery. Bonds gained about 3% on the week and country risk fell more than 100 points to about 830 (Infobae).

Zelensky says the US floated NATO membership if Ukraine leaves the Donbas. Kushner calls for "confidentiality"

Unconfirmed by USReferendum pledgeMoscow softens on EU seat

"The Americans raised a question: 'What if Ukraine joins Nato? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?' This was the first time this had been raised," Zelensky said in remarks released Saturday (BBC). He said Ukraine will not cede territory, but that any concrete package with security guarantees, no limits on its military and reconstruction money would go to a referendum. The US did not confirm it; an official told the BBC "many ideas continue to be discussed." Envoy Jared Kushner later urged "confidentiality" and criticized "maximalist" proposals and "strong political rhetoric" being aired, which the BBC read as a reproach to Kyiv. Russia's stated position has not changed: it wants the rest of the Donbas before any ceasefire, and Moscow's talk of a "new European security architecture" is, per the BBC, long-standing code for a weaker NATO. Other NATO members are wary of Ukrainian membership. Moscow said it will not dictate the EU delegation's participation in talks, softening its earlier stance (Euronews). On the ground, a Russian attack on Zaporizhzhia injured 22 people on Saturday, Ukraine's emergency service said.

A Trump–Xi–Putin meeting in Shenzhen is expected next week, but there is no agenda yet and Beijing is said to be wary
