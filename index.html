Thursday, in two lines

A day after Walmart invited shopping agents in, America's biggest banks moved the other way. Axios reports Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi are blocking or restricting outside agents because they can't tell whether an agent is following its user or acting on its own. The industry answer is taking shape at the same time: Google launched agent computers with built-in identity and governance, and Anthropic handed its Agent Skills standard to the Linux Foundation.

Markets had their worst AI day in months. The Nasdaq fell 2.6% as Meta, AMD and Nvidia sold off on doubts about funding the buildout, and Jensen Huang himself said capital is becoming "scarce." Treasuries rallied hard, Brent hit a five-month high on Iran threats against Gulf data centers, and Argentina's country risk crossed 700. Legora, Warp Sciences and Tractian still raised big rounds.

Agent identity · Banking

Banks lock agents out because they can't tell who is acting

Axios scoop, Oct 22 · The Verge · Google Cloud blog · Walmart terms · Microsoft Security blog, Oct 21
Morgan Stanley blockingJPM, BofA, Citi restrictingRead-only is the middle ground

Axios reports that major US banks are finding unauthorized third-party AI agents inside customer accounts, and some of them appear to be taking actions independent of what the user asked. In most cases the customer did give permission: they handed an assistant their username and password. That still breaks the rule at most banks that a human logs in to their own account. Bank officials told Axios they also see agents calling call centers and acting outside instructions. One executive described the situation as "horrifying."

The core problem is attribution. "Many banks are broadly not able to parse whether agent account activity is user-directed," Axios writes, and one executive said they struggle even to tell agents apart, or to tell an authorized agent from a rogue one. So the banks are defaulting to no. Morgan Stanley has started blocking third-party agents, and JPMorgan, Bank of America and Citi are restricting them. (Jamie Dimon already called outside agents "too risky" on Oct 13.) Most banks are moving toward read-only access, the same arrangement Quicken-style budgeting apps already use, so agents can see balances but not move money. Some are running "day after" drills in case an agent incident locks customers out.

It isn't only banks. The Verge's Mia Sato describes agents being walled off across the web: Amazon's blocks on Perplexity and Muse, users unable to book Delta or Amtrak trips with agents, and generic bot firewalls that make agents unreliable even where nobody banned them. Meanwhile attackers are already using agents. Microsoft says the China-aligned group Storm-0940 used the open-source ARTEX agent framework in the ConnectWise backup breach, attempting sign-ins at more than 2,250 tenants at up to 2,900 attempts a minute. Banks see the same kind of bot traffic every day, which makes them slow to trust any agent.

The fix that keeps showing up is the same: give each agent its own identity and scoped permissions instead of the user's password. Walmart's new Agent Profiles do it for shopping. Google's new governance stack, launched Thursday, gives each agent a cryptographic ID based on the SPIFFE standard, a central registry of approved agents and tools, and credentials injected outside the agent's reach so it never holds them. Stripe's agent treasury lets founders pick which accounts an agent can touch. What doesn't exist yet is a shared way for a bank to check that a specific agent was authorized by a specific customer for a specific action.

Why it mattersThis is the clearest sign yet of where the agent-to-agent opportunity sits. Agents already have users, and now they have open merchants. The missing piece is a trust layer that a cautious counterparty like a bank can rely on: proof of which agent this is, who authorized it, for what, and up to what limit, plus logs that hold up in a dispute. Banks have the cash to build it but, as one source told Axios, not the speed. That leaves room for a startup doing agent identity, delegated authorization or "agent KYC" that sits between consumer agents and regulated institutions. It also makes the spending caps and approvals Walmart and Stripe shipped look less like features and more like what every regulated counterparty will require.

AI capital · Markets

The AI trade's worst day since June, and Huang says capital is scarce

AP · CNBC live blog · Infobae · Bloomberg markets wrap via Swissinfo, Oct 22–23
Tech sector −3%Credit spreads widest since May 2025Solidigm IPO shelved

Thursday's selloff picked up where Meta's and Tesla's earnings left off. The S&P 500 fell 1.21% to 7,663.43, its worst day since July, and the Nasdaq dropped 2.60% to 26,008.86, its worst since June. The Dow closed flat at a one-month high as money rotated into Procter & Gamble (+5.6%), Home Depot and J&J. The damage was in AI: AMD −8.1%, Intel −8%, Meta −7%, Nvidia −4.4%, Alphabet −3.2%, and SpaceX −9.1% (per Infobae) after its Wednesday jump. Western Digital and Bloom Energy beat estimates after the bell and still fell 5%.

The most quoted line came from Jensen Huang, asked on CNBC about the neocloud companies that rent out GPUs: "There's a scarcity of capital. The demand [for] the capital is much greater than the availability of the capital." Ed Yardeni called AI a "bubble" but said "taking some air out" is healthy. Bloomberg adds that corporate bond risk premiums have widened to their highest since May 2025 just as the big spenders are borrowing more. SK Hynix dropped plans for a possible $10B US listing of its Solidigm memory unit, citing market volatility. "The bar has certainly risen, with credit markets showing increasing signs of stress and a decreasing willingness to fund additional capex," said Bank J Safra Sarasin's Wolf von Rotberg.

Money went to bonds. The US 10-year yield fell about 19bp to 4.95%, its biggest one-day drop since March 2023, which lifted homebuilders (+3.8%) and regional banks (+2.5%). The calm returned overnight: Nasdaq 100 futures are up about 0.3%, Korea's Kospi recovered from a 4.1% intraday loss to close higher, and chip ETFs are up 2% premarket. Julius Baer's Mark Matthews: "Demand still massively exceeds supply."

Why it mattersFor founders, the public mood feeds into private rounds with a lag. The pressure is landing on the most capital-hungry layer (neoclouds, chips, data centers), not on application companies; Legora, Warp and Tractian all closed big rounds the same day. If credit stays tight, expect investors to favor companies that sell outcomes and show revenue per agent over ones that need GPUs to grow. It's also a reason to raise when a round is available rather than wait for Microsoft and Alphabet to report on Oct 28.

Google Cloud · AGNTCon + MCPCon · Oct 22

Google launches Agent Compute Engine, a computer for every agent

Preview in coming monthsHardware-isolated microVMsOpen-source Agent Substrate

Agent Compute Engine is the service that already runs Gemini's work agent, now coming to customers. Each agent gets its own fast-booting microVM with its own disk, and Google pauses the machine whenever the agent is "thinking" or waiting on a human, then resumes it from a snapshot. Many agents share one OS template to keep costs down. For Kubernetes users, the open-source Agent Substrate does the same on any cluster: Google says it ran 200,000 agents with under 500 ms suspend and resume, and it works with Claude Code, OpenClaw and Hermes. On top sits governance: an Agent Registry of approved agents, tools, MCP servers and skills; SPIFFE-based Agent Identity; OpenTelemetry tracing for every tool call; and, this quarter, per-agent token budgets and model routing. It will extend to coding agents running on employee laptops.

"One computer per agent" is becoming standard infrastructure, competing with Daytona-style sandboxes. Per-agent identity and cost budgets are exactly what banks say they're missing today.

Anthropic · Oct 22

Anthropic donates Agent Skills to the Linux Foundation's agent group

Agent Skills, the format where a folder with a SKILL.md file teaches an agent a task, now belongs to the Agentic AI Foundation, the Linux Foundation body Anthropic co-founded with Block and OpenAI and where MCP already lives. More than 70 platforms support it, including ChatGPT, Cursor, GitHub Copilot and VS Code. Anthropic says 1 million skills have been published and nearly 280 million installed. Engineers from Anthropic, Google and OpenAI will maintain it, led by David Soria Parra and Mahesh Murag. Anthropic is handing over the name, logo, GitHub org and the agentskills.io domain.

MCP (how agents reach tools) and Skills (how agents learn procedures) now sit under neutral governance. A merchant or supplier can publish both once and reach every major agent.

DeepSeek and MiniMax get Beijing's approval for global use

A social account affiliated with state broadcaster CCTV said DeepSeek and MiniMax won state approval for global use of their models, a week after China proposed export controls on 23 AI technologies including models, agents and computing clusters. Cheap Chinese open models stay available to builders outside China, but now with a government licence attached, which is a factor for anyone choosing a model for regulated customers.

FourKites · Built In Chicago · Oct 22

FourKites moves from tracking freight to selling fixed outcomes

Supply chain visibility company FourKites launched Outcome-as-a-Service, where customers pay for measured results such as on-time-in-full delivery, lower expedited freight spend or cost to serve, and AI agents resolve problems instead of just flagging them. It set a goal of having agents complete 1 billion hours of operational work for customers over the next decade. "A VP of supply chain isn't measured on whether they've adopted AI," said CEO Mathew Elenjickal. "They're measured on whether product arrives on time and in full."

It's the second company this week, after Whop, to price on outcomes rather than seats. For ops software that's where pricing is heading, and it requires agents you trust to act without someone approving every step.

Returns robots go from pilots to hundreds of live cells in Europe

Sereact and Teradyne's Universal Robots formalized a partnership after shipping hundreds of systems to live European sites. Iran threatens Gulf data centers and AI sites if the US and Israel strike again

Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's supreme national security council, said a new US–Israeli attack would bring strikes on oil facilities, undersea cables, data centers and AI facilities in up to seven Gulf countries. An adviser to Iran's chief negotiator called the strategy "zero red lines." Foreign ministers from three European countries, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Egypt meet in Istanbul today.

The Gulf has been a growing AI hub for US companies. A threat against its data centers, on top of the oil risk, matters for any AI company planning compute there.
