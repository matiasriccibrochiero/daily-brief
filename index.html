Anthropic's amended S-1: a $17B quarter, a $23B loss, $895B of commitments, and a warning about self-improving AI

CNBC · TechCrunch · SEC filing · The Decoder · WSJ via investingLive · Yahoo Finance · Nov 2
9-mo revenue $33.24BQ3 growth +47% q/q$895.4B commitmentsTrading as soon as Nov 16

What happened. Anthropic filed an amended prospectus (S-1/A) with the SEC on Monday, adding third-quarter numbers to the filing it unveiled last week. Revenue for the nine months to Sept. 30 was $33.24 billion, up roughly 1,200% year on year; the third quarter alone was about $17 billion, up about 1,127%. Growth is slowing quarter on quarter, though: Q3 revenue rose about 47% from Q2, after 144% in Q2 and 132% in Q1 (CNBC). The Q3 net loss was $23.29 billion, and $104 billion for nine months, most of it from non-cash changes in the fair value of convertible notes and derivative liabilities. On an operating basis, The Decoder and PPC Land report a swing back to a loss of about $567 million, driven by roughly $5 billion of short-term compute bought at high prices and $2 billion of employee donation matching.

The biggest number is the forward bill. Anthropic says it has purchase commitments and leases totalling about $895.4 billion, "substantially all of which is related to compute and infrastructure." It gets most of its capacity from Amazon, Google and SpaceX, plus some neoclouds. Compute and infrastructure expenses rose $14.4 billion, or 329%, over the nine months.

The risk factor that drew attention. New language in the filing says Anthropic's growing use of AI in its own R&D "may cause the capabilities of our models to advance more rapidly than our ability to develop, validate, and implement corresponding safety" measures, that it has "in the past not identified" accelerations until after they occurred, and that internal estimates for future models "indicate that we may imminently meet" its Responsible Scaling Policy definition of "dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI." It adds that no current model has been designated as crossing that threshold, and that it does not expect to safely let Claude build successive frontier models "without meaningful human supervision" (TechCrunch, quoting the S-1/A). TechCrunch also notes the sceptics' view: a prospectus is both disclosure and marketing.

Pricing pressure. The Wall Street Journal reported that prospective IPO investors are pushing for a valuation around $1.5 trillion, below earlier talk near $2 trillion, after last week's tech sell-off (via investingLive and Yahoo Finance). The roadshow is expected to start around Nov. 9–10, with trading possible from Nov. 16; no price has been set.

Why it matters (analysis)This is the first time investors can see a frontier lab's full economics in audited, legally binding form: revenue scaling faster than almost any company in history, alongside commitments that dwarf it and slowing sequential growth. How the market prices that will set a benchmark for OpenAI's planned listing and for the whole AI buildout trade, which is already under strain. The RSI disclosure matters for a different reason: it puts a lab's own claim that AI is accelerating AI research into a securities document, where it carries legal weight, which regulators and safety researchers are likely to cite whichever way they read it.

Election Day: polls open after a record early vote, with mixed final signals on the Senate

AP · Fox News · NPR · New York Times/Siena · Washington Examiner · Nov 2–3
54M+ early ballotsSenate now 53–47 RFirst closings 20:00 ARTLast 03:00 ART

What is happening. Voters are choosing all 435 House members, 35 senators and dozens of governors. More than 54 million people voted early, nearly 9 million more than in 2022, according to the AP count cited by Fox News. Republicans hold the Senate 53–47, so Democrats need a net gain of four seats; with Vice President JD Vance breaking ties, Republicans keep control with 50. The AP reports a heavy presence of observers: Justice Department monitors on one side, and tens of thousands of volunteers from Democratic and watchdog groups on the other, watching for intimidation or federal interference. Disputes over voter-roll lists, certification and monitoring could run past tonight.

The final polls point in two directions. The last New York Times/Siena surveys, of 5,708 likely voters in seven Senate battlegrounds (Oct. 23–Nov. 1), found Democrats ahead in six, including double-digit leads in Michigan, with Iowa essentially tied. The same polls found that voters who decided in the last month favoured Republicans by nine points, a figure Republicans have highlighted. CNN's Harry Enten has pointed out that Republicans outperformed polls by about four points on average in Trump-won states in recent cycles. An NBC poll (Oct. 28–30) gave Republicans the edge on immigration and the economy and Democrats on inflation and the Iran war.

Marvell buys Lumentum for $115B, one of the largest tech deals ever, to own the optics inside AI data centres

4.5 MRVL per LITE share~$80B equity valueClose H1 2028

Custom-chip designer Marvell agreed to acquire laser and optical-networking maker Lumentum in an all-stock deal valued at $115 billion including debt, about $80 billion in equity. Marvell holders will own about 67% of the combined company. Marvell projects more than $1 billion of annual cost synergies. TechCrunch notes the deal is larger than Microsoft–Activision ($68.7 billion) and that Nvidia invested $2 billion in each company in March. Closing requires shareholder and regulatory approval and is expected in the first half of 2028.

As AI clusters grow, moving data between chips with light rather than copper has become a bottleneck, and this deal bets that one company should control both the electrical and optical sides. The main risk is a long regulatory review: closing is not expected until the first half of 2028.

The New York Times and three papers sue Brave over AI scraping and a paywall blocker

The Times sued Brave Software in federal court in Manhattan, alleging it scraped articles without permission, sold access to its search index to AI companies (the complaint names Anthropic, Perplexity, Mistral, xAI and Cohere as buyers), and offered a "Paywall blocker" that shows subscriber content without a login. Trajectory raises $45M from Sequoia to let domain experts retrain models from feedback

The 18-person startup, founded about a year ago by former Google DeepMind and Apple researchers, raised a $45 million Series A at a $300 million valuation led by Sequoia, with Nvidia, Bessemer and Bain Capital Ventures. The EU's new €2 handling fee on every imported item is now live, on top of July's €3 duty

€2 per item+ €3 duty ≤ €150Import parcels −60% since July

From Nov. 1, the EU charges a €2 Union Handling Fee for each distinct item in a consumer parcel shipped from outside the bloc, regardless of value. It is separate from the temporary €3-per-item customs duty on parcels up to €150 that began in July, so a low-value parcel with two different items can carry €10 in duty and fees before VAT (Irish and Latvian tax authorities). Platforms, sellers or carriers pay it and may pass it into prices; consumers should not be charged separately at the door. CEP Research reports carriers are adding data requirements and changing services, with some temporary suspensions, and says the July duty already led to a roughly 60% drop in import parcel volumes.

The rules aim at the flood of cheap direct-from-China orders from platforms like Temu and Shein; consumer group BEUC says the fee should fund product-safety checks. For cross-border sellers, the per-item design favours single-item orders and EU-based warehousing.

FreightWaves · ShipMatrix · Nov 2

US holiday parcels forecast up 4% to 2.4B, while shipping rates are up 16%

ShipMatrix expects carriers to deliver about 2.4 billion packages this peak season, up 4% and the most since 2022, helped by an extra shopping day. Retailers are paying about 16% more per parcel than last year, mostly from fuel surcharges (now about 30% of the rate, largely due to carriers changing the formula, per the TD Cowen/AFS index) and 5.9% general increases that FedEx and UPS plan to repeat in 2027. Amazon is hiring 300,000 seasonal workers, up 20%; UPS 100,000, down from 109,000. In the first half, Amazon's US parcel volume rose 9.7% and FedEx's 4.6%, while UPS fell 5.7% and USPS 3.1%, with about 145 million parcels moving to retailers' own networks and regional carriers.

Some retailers are raising free-shipping thresholds in response. The shift of volume from legacy carriers to Amazon and regional networks is now the main structural story in US parcel delivery.

Infobae · Confidencial · Nov 2–3

Bonds slide as markets price the US vote into Argentine risk

Country risk rose 18 points to 805 basis points as long bonds fell up to 1.7%. Infobae attributes the move mainly to the US election, along with Brazil's political signals and Wall Street's weakness: investors fear that a strong Democratic result could weaken Washington's financial backing for Argentina. The central bank bought no dollars in the official market. Farm exporters brought in a record US$2.88 billion for an October (Aldazabal y Cía.). The roughly US$800 million IMF payment due Monday was made by buying Special Drawing Rights, Confidencial reports.

Analysis cited by Infobae estimates that local electoral risk ahead of 2027 explains almost 60% of the spread. Adcap recommends caution on fixed-rate peso bonds. The next big test remains roughly US$4.5 billion of bond payments in January.

US strikes Iran's Qeshm Island on the eve of the vote; both sides still say a deal is possible

The US carried out "limited strikes" on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, three US officials told CNN, calling them defensive after Iran fired surface-to-air missiles at US aircraft. The Wall Street Journal, which first reported it, said similar strikes had been carried out in secret in recent weeks. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they shot down two US reconnaissance drones over the island. Parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, who leads Iran's negotiators, said any "miscalculation" would get an "unpredictable" response, and a senior official told Reuters the sides were far from a deal. Vice President Vance said the US had "certainly made some progress"; Trump told Fox News "there's a very good chance we make a deal" but said the US "might have to go after" the Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site.

Oil swung from a 7.6% drop on Monday to a rebound overnight, showing how much is priced on headlines. Goldman Sachs raised its December Brent forecast to $105 and sees up to $140 in 2027 if Gulf output stays depressed (Reuters via Moneycontrol). US gasoline and diesel are on track to be the most expensive of any Election Day (CNN).
