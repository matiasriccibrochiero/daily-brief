Agent-to-agent payments · Money20/20 day 3

Agents start paying agents: Mastercard, Visa and Evervault ship the plumbing

Mastercard newsroom and PYMNTS, Oct 20 · Visa/BILL/Billtrust release, Oct 20 · Evervault via Business Wire · Mural via PR Newswire · Payments Dive
"402 Payment Required"Signed vouchers, settled laterPayPal: not meaningful until 2028

Yesterday's brief was about who carries the loss when an agent pays. Tuesday was about how the payment actually happens. Mastercard opened early access to Agent Pay for Machines, built on x402, the open protocol where a paid service answers a request with HTTP "402 Payment Required." The model: a business gives an agent a task and a spending limit. When the agent hits a paid service, it signs a payment voucher, "a secure promise to pay within its approved limit." The provider verifies it and delivers, and vouchers can be bundled and redeemed later. That runs off-chain at machine speed, with no account, API key or invoice per vendor. Mastercard's line: "Payments aren't the hard part. Authority is."

The demo at Cloudflare Connect: marketing agency Hype asks an agent, running on a platform like Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, to plan a protein-bar campaign. The agent finds registered providers through a discovery service, proposes a plan (social research from AIsa, images through Cloudflare AI Gateway), and Hype approves the budget. Result: nine payments, two independent merchants, one budget. Coinbase supplies the wallet, Base settles on-chain, and BVNK acts as the agent wallet using Mastercard's Global Money Platform, so sellers can be paid in fiat or stablecoins. Pilots ran with Cloudflare, Ant International, AIsa, Base, BVNK, Coinbase and Hype. Developers now get buyer- and seller-side APIs, a sandbox and docs.

Visa showed the B2B version, and it's live. With BILL and Billtrust, a BILL Payment Agent registered in Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol and Agentic Directory pays supplier invoices through Billtrust portals using tokenized cards, with controls tied to the buyer's approved payment instruction. Visa says agent authentication "can satisfy the site protections historically provided by CAPTCHA," with no separate agent login. For sites that haven't adopted any protocol, Evervault launched Browser Tokens: short-lived stand-ins for card numbers and passwords that pass Luhn and BIN checks, and are swapped for the real value only at a hostname the developer approved. Ramp and Natural are early customers. Natural's Walt Leung pitched it as a way to scale agentic commerce "without waiting for every merchant to adopt new protocols." On the treasury side, Mural raised a $15M Series A led by Tether for accounts that "people and agents" hold, with limits, approved counterparties and approvals on larger payments. It has processed $2.1B across 128 countries.

The counterweight came from PayPal CEO Enrique Lores, who said agentic commerce won't drive meaningful sales until 2028: "the amount of commerce that is done through agents in '26, or that will be done in '27, is going to be relatively small." PayPal is betting that trust, its original e-commerce edge, matters even more when agents shop.

Why it mattersThe machine-to-machine side is arriving before the consumer side. Agents buying data, models, compute and B2B services within a budget is happening now; agents buying sneakers for people is a 2028 story by PayPal's own estimate. That points to where a new company can get real volume first. The building blocks are taking shape: a mandate (task + budget + allowed counterparties), discovery of who sells what, a voucher that promises payment, and settlement in whatever currency the seller wants. Mastercard's discovery service and directory are the first open marketplace for agent services. Building there early (as a seller agents can find, or as the layer that writes and checks mandates) is cheaper than waiting for consumer checkout to settle on a standard.

AI capital · IPO race

Anthropic's draft S-1 shows the bill for AI: $450B committed, up to $750B more

WSJ exclusive via Bloomberg/Business Standard, Oct 20–21 · Reuters via Investing.com · SpaceX Q3, WSJ and Investing.com
H1 net loss $80.7B ($72B accounting)H1 compute ~$9BOpenAI: ~$30B at $1.4T+

The Wall Street Journal saw Anthropic's confidential draft IPO prospectus. Key numbers: about $450B of compute agreements over the next seven to ten years, mostly non-cancelable, including $111B with Google Cloud, $110B with AWS and $31B with Microsoft, plus up to $750B of further compute spending including deals still in negotiation. It spent close to $9B on compute and infrastructure in the first half, against $7.3B in all of 2025. The first-half net loss was $80.7B, but $72B of that is accounting charges on convertible notes and similar instruments, not cash. Bloomberg separately reported that Anthropic told investors it was profitable on an adjusted basis in Q3, with an 85% gross margin and positive free cash flow. Marketing could start as soon as the week of Nov 9. Risk factors include models showing "self-preserving" behavior and political resistance to US data centers.

Reuters reports that OpenAI is trying to lock in about $30B, at a valuation that could top $1.4T, before Anthropic's numbers invite comparisons. SoftBank and MGX are in talks. OpenAI is pitching $50B of annualized revenue, rising to $70B by year-end. And SpaceX gave the market the proof it wanted: Q3 revenue of $14.58B versus ~$13B expected, net income of $4.4B, and $7.6B from its AI ("Super Intelligence") division. Musk said SpaceX and Tesla have formed committees to study combining, with no agreement yet. SpaceX traded 9.9% higher premarket; ING called it "evidence that AI-related demand remains very real."

Why it mattersLast week's worry was who pays for AI infrastructure. Anthropic's filing answers part of it: the labs themselves, through long contracts the clouds can borrow against, which is why Nscale-style deals keep coming. For builders, it means model and compute prices are set by firms with hundred-billion-dollar fixed costs that need volume. Expect aggressive pricing and bundling for agent workloads, and treat any single model vendor as a commodity input you can switch. The IPO also produces the first audited view of what frontier AI costs and earns, which every investor in your next round will use as a benchmark.

Microsoft Security · SecurityWeek · Oct 20

Microsoft says treat agents as insiders, and gives them zero permissions by default

Dynamic PermissionsAgent risk score60 agent types detected

Microsoft extended Project Perception with controls aimed at the agents companies run themselves. Dynamic Permissions start an agent with no or minimal access, grant it per task, and let it expire as the task changes. High-impact actions need human review. Dynamic Agent Risk keeps a live risk score from identity, data, endpoint, network and runtime signals, including whether the agent is drifting from what the user intended. Conditional Access can then cut it to read-only, narrow its data, require approval, or halt it. Guardian Agents investigate and contain. Defender now detects 60 kinds of local agents, including Claude Code, Codex and Copilot. "Frontier models, regardless of their origin, cannot be trusted," said security chief Hayete Gallot, meaning they're unpredictable rather than malicious.

This is the enterprise version of the payments mandate: task-scoped, expiring authority with an approval step. If your agent will run inside corporate customers, design it to request narrow, per-task permissions. It'll pass security review faster.

DHS · DeepSeek · CNBC · Oct 20

US bars DeepSeek, and anything built on it, from civilian federal systems

A Homeland Security order issued in July and made public Monday requires civilian federal agencies to remove DeepSeek technology and stop buying it, including third-party products built on its models. DHS cited possible transfers of government data to China and security weaknesses in the models. China's foreign ministry said it would protect its companies' rights. On the same day, DeepSeek open-sourced DeepInfer, the inference stack it uses in production.

The "built on" clause matters for startups. If you sell to US government or regulated buyers, a cheap open-weight Chinese model deep in your stack can disqualify the whole product. Keep model choice swappable and documented.

Daytona becomes a unicorn selling agents "a safe place to fail"

$60M Series B$1B valuation25x run rate since Jan

Daytona, founded in 2023 by a Croatian team, provides isolated sandboxes (Linux, Windows, macOS) where agents run code and tools through one API, and can pause, resume or branch without losing progress. Isolation is becoming a default line item in the agent stack, alongside the security and payment controls above.

Salesforce buys Braze for $5.66B to bring real-time B2C engagement to Agentforce

$47.50 / share cashCloses in FY2028

Salesforce will pay $47.50 a share in cash for Braze, the customer-engagement platform behind app push, in-app, email, SMS and WhatsApp journeys for big consumer brands. Brand-to-customer messaging is consolidating into the same platforms building agents. When a shopper's agent talks to a brand's agent, this is the layer the brand side will run on. For DTC operators, expect bundling pressure on Braze pricing after close.

Shippo · e-commerce.news · Oct 20

Shippo: 85% of merchants were overpaying for shipping, and agents are now buying labels

Ahead of peak, Shippo says 85% of merchants shipping 100+ labels a month had a cheaper option available, with average potential savings of 22%. In 44% of those cases, the cheaper service would also have arrived sooner (July platform data). It's pushing its Estimate API (delivery dates at checkout, which it says are 90%+ accurate), AI cost dashboards with weekly alerts, and scan-to-print packing. Its MCP connections, which let merchants compare rates, buy labels and track parcels from inside Claude or ChatGPT, are growing 138% month over month. Shippo moved about 440M packages in the last 12 months, up 39%.

Shipping is one of the first operational jobs being handed to agents through MCP. A 22% cost gap is the kind of number an agent can close automatically.

Buenos Aires · Oct 20

Country risk breaks 700, then buyers step in

Dollar bonds opened down about 1.5% and JP Morgan's index hit 710 at 10:15, its highest since Oct 24, 2025. Bargain hunters drawn by high yields after seven losing sessions reversed the move, and it closed at 672, ending a seven-day climb. Hard-dollar bonds are still down about 5% over the month, more than 6% for the long end, against a 2026 low of 403 in July. The central bank bought $103M, and gross reserves are $50.20B. The Finance Secretariat swapped $3.90B of the D30O6 dollar-linked bond early (73% of the outstanding amount) to reduce FX volatility before its Oct 27 fixing. Adcap notes higher US Treasury yields are now flowing through to Argentine corporate credit, up 74bp on average in a month.

The wholesale rate is still 27% below the band ceiling of $1,940.30, but the blue and CCL both set nominal records. Year-end dollar futures are at $1,590.50.

UK · Saudi Arabia · Iran · Oct 20

UK sends Typhoons to defend Saudi Arabia; Iran's mediation offer goes unanswered

Britain will fly defensive counter-air missions with RAF Typhoons to protect Saudi cities and infrastructure from Houthi drones and missiles. It won't strike the Houthis, and it's extending air-to-air refueling for Saudi operations. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting called the attacks, including on Riyadh's airport, "absolutely despicable." This adds up to a wider war around the world's two main oil chokepoints, the Red Sea and Hormuz. It's why freight and fuel costs, not just crude, are the line to watch for anyone landing goods from Asia.
