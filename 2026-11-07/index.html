Overnight, in two lines

The US added 165,000 jobs in October, nearly three times what economists expected, and unemployment slipped to 4.1%. Treasury yields rose and several forecasters now expect the Federal Reserve to raise rates in December. Stocks steadied after Thursday's rout: the Nasdaq gained 1.1% and the S&P 500 0.3%, though the S&P still logged its worst week since June.

In private markets the AI repricing kept going: Bloomberg reports OpenAI has raised $11 billion of a round now expected near $20 billion (below a reported $30 billion target), and prospective Anthropic IPO buyers are discussing $1.1–1.3 trillion. Zipline raised $1.5 billion as Walmart adds 300 drone-delivery stores, Washington is pursuing indirect Iran talks led by JD Vance, and New Zealand's election is too close to call.

Macro · Labor market

US hiring jumps to 165,000 in October, and rate-hike odds rise with it

BLS · CNBC · Reuters · AP · Haver Analytics · Nov 6
Payrolls +165KUnemployment 4.1%Wages +0.4% m/m · +3.1% y/yRevisions +84K

What happened. The Bureau of Labor Statistics said nonfarm payrolls rose by 165,000 in October, against a Dow Jones consensus of 59,000 (Reuters' poll had 65,000). August and September were revised up by a combined 84,000, putting September at 60,000. The unemployment rate edged down to 4.1%, and the household survey showed 463,000 more people at work while the labor force grew by 421,000, so the drop came from hiring rather than people leaving the workforce. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% on the month and 3.1% on the year, both above forecasts.

Gains were concentrated in professional and business services (+70,000), health care (+42,000), transportation and warehousing (+25,000, mostly couriers and messengers) and manufacturing (+19,000). Haver Analytics notes the six-month average is now about 82,000, compared with roughly 10,000 a month across 2025.

The weaker parts. Not everything was strong. The broad U-6 rate, which counts involuntary part-timers and discouraged workers, rose to 7.9%. CNBC reports the average spell of unemployment rose to 27.1 weeks, the longest since September 2021, though the BLS describes the count of long-term unemployed (about 2.0 million) as little changed. AP's coverage stresses that inflation is still eroding paychecks: 3.1% wage growth is the figure to weigh against prices.

Market and policy reaction. The two-year Treasury yield rose to about 4.52% and the 10-year to 4.86% (MarketWatch). Oxford Economics said the report "leaves the Fed free to focus on inflation" and expects rate increases in December and March, citing energy prices and AI spillovers as the main inflation risks. That is a forecast, not a Fed signal.

Why it matters (analysis)A labor market adding jobs this fast removes the main argument for the Fed to hold still while oil-driven inflation runs. Higher short-term rates would weigh most on borrowers who depend on cheap credit, including leveraged data-center builders and late-stage startups. That is part of why a strong jobs number did not translate into a strong day for stocks. The U-6 rise and longer unemployment spells are the counterpoint economists will watch for signs that the gains are narrowing.

The AI valuation reset reaches the two biggest labs

Bloomberg (via TechCrunch, Bloomberg Tax) · Yahoo Finance · Nov 6
OpenAI $11B @ $1.25TRound ~$20B vs $30B targetAnthropic talk $1.1–1.3TReported · not final

What happened. Bloomberg reported Friday that OpenAI has raised $11 billion so far in a new round at a $1.25 trillion valuation. The round has not closed; a source told Bloomberg it could total about $20 billion in the coming weeks. In late September, Bloomberg had reported OpenAI was aiming for $30 billion. The Information separately reported that OpenAI is expected to brief investors on third-quarter results next week. OpenAI had not commented to TechCrunch at publication.

Hours earlier, Bloomberg reported that prospective buyers in Anthropic's IPO are now discussing a valuation of about $1.2–1.3 trillion, with some weighing as low as $1.1 trillion. Those buyers had told bankers earlier that $1.4–1.5 trillion looked fair, and TechCrunch notes talk of roughly $2 trillion about a month ago. Anthropic is expected to start formal IPO marketing next week; its amended S-1 (covered in Friday's brief) shows about $895 billion of compute and infrastructure commitments.

Context. These reports follow Thursday's sell-off, which several outlets tied to worries that a Democratic Congress and local opposition could slow data-center construction, plus higher borrowing costs. Friday's jobs report added to the rate side of that worry. Both labs still command valuations above $1 trillion, so this is a markdown from very high expectations, not a collapse.

Why it matters (analysis)Public-market sentiment is now setting private prices for frontier labs in real time. A smaller OpenAI round and a lower Anthropic IPO range mean less fresh equity per dollar of future compute spending, which matters because both companies have contracted for very large, multi-year infrastructure. Where the Anthropic book prices next week will be the first hard market test of that spending. All figures here come from people familiar with the talks and could change.

Independent assessment says Anthropic's AI research is moving about twice as fast, with much of the evidence redacted

~2× pace vs 2024–25Aug 12 – Oct 29 data~100-page report

METR, an independent AI evaluation group, published an assessment estimating that Anthropic is improving model capability on well-defined tasks at roughly twice the rate it did in 2024–2025, based on internal development data from August 12 to October 29. METR attributes much of the speed-up to AI tools helping Anthropic staff with coding and experiment analysis. It found most of the gain still comes from AI assisting people rather than running research projects on its own, but saw no clear bottleneck that would stop further acceleration over the next year.

METR says Anthropic redacted most of the material it considered most informative; Anthropic says the redactions protect confidential information from competitors. That limits outside verification. The finding matters because faster AI-assisted research is the mechanism behind concerns about rapid, self-reinforcing capability gains, and because Anthropic is about to market its IPO.

The Neuron · AI of the Day · company posts · Nov 6

Agent products keep shipping: Perplexity Teammates, Mistral managed agents, Meta's Muse avatars

Perplexity launched Teammates, always-on work agents that can take several assignments, watch for changes and collaborate, reusing existing Perplexity Computer projects and permissions; users can inspect or redirect their work. Mistral expanded its Studio platform with managed agents, workflows and apps aimed at enterprises that want control over models and data; Apps and Managed Agents are in public preview. Meta rolled out real-time animated avatars for its Muse assistant in the US, with interruption support and hundreds of voices. Google released Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash for 15-second video generation and editing at about 30% lower prices, by its own account.

The common thread is a shift from chat to persistent agents that run in the background with set permissions, which raises the same questions each time about oversight and access controls. Feature claims are the companies' own.

The Morning Build · TLDR AI · Nov 6

Open-weight releases: Baidu's ERNIE 5.5 Flash base and Inkling 2 Small

ERNIE: 280B total · 14B activeInkling: 594B · 17B active

Baidu published ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Base, a mixture-of-experts model with 280 billion parameters (14 billion active per input), pre-trained on 33.5 trillion tokens, with weights on Hugging Face in FP8 and BF16. It is a base model released before instruction tuning so developers can adapt it; a post-trained version is promised. Separately, Inkling 2 Small, a 594-billion-parameter MoE model with image input, launched through the Tinker platform with closed weights, and its developers say full Apache 2.0 weights follow next week after external red-teaming. Large open releases from Chinese and Western labs continue to narrow the gap with closed models for teams willing to host their own.

Zipline raises $1.5 billion at $21.5 billion as Walmart adds 300 drone-delivery stores

$1.5B @ $21.5B postWalmart +300 sitesWas $7.6B in JanUber: 1M/day goal by 2029

Zipline raised $1.5 billion at a $21.5 billion post-money valuation, led by Paradigm, WCM Investment Management and Layer Global, with Uber, Tiger Global, J.P. Morgan, Fidelity, ARK and others participating. It plans to expand US manufacturing and hiring. Under a new multiyear deal, Walmart will add Zipline drone delivery at 300 more stores, up from 21 today; Walmart gave no timeline or list of markets. Walmart says it has completed more than 2 million drone deliveries since 2021, and it also works with Alphabet's Wing, which is targeting 270+ Walmart locations.

Zipline says it completed more than a million deliveries in Dallas–Fort Worth this year with a median order arriving 19 minutes after checkout; Uber is targeting one million Zipline deliveries a day by 2029. These are company figures and goals, not audited volumes. Amazon says Prime Air will reach nearly 500 US communities by year-end.

Analysis: the valuation nearly tripled since January, a bet that drone delivery becomes everyday last-mile logistics rather than a niche. The open questions are the usual ones for physical networks: delivery density, aircraft utilization, maintenance cost and local regulation as it scales.

Axios · AP · Reuters · Nov 6

Vance leads indirect push for a new Iran ceasefire as a strike and a tanker attack keep tension high

Axios reports, citing two sources, that Vice President JD Vance has spent two weeks in near-daily contact with Qatari and Pakistani mediators on a new ceasefire and a restart of nuclear talks, after writing to Iran's Supreme National Security Council secretary. Vance said Wednesday the war was the main reason Republicans lost the midterms. Trump said "we're talking to them" and spoke with Qatar's emir on Friday; Qatar's prime minister said a deal is possible but progress is slow. AP reports Iran has proposed reopening the Strait of Hormuz within two days if Washington meets conditions including an end to hostilities and release of frozen assets.

At the same time, Trump confirmed a US strike on an anti-aircraft launcher on Qeshm Island after it fired toward a US jet, and Reuters reports one seafarer was killed in a suspected missile attack on the tanker Velos Sapphire. Iranian reaction to Vance's remarks is divided, Iran International reports. Talks are indirect and no agreement has been announced.

New Zealand election too close to call, with the Opportunity Party as possible kingmaker

Preliminary results from Saturday's general election put National at about 29.4% of the party vote and Labour at 29.1%.
