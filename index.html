Anthropic prices the biggest IPO ever at $83 a share and debuts on the Nasdaq today

Anthropic · CNBC · AP · TechCrunch · Bloomberg Law · Nov 18–19
980M shares at $83$81.3B raised~$1.5T fully dilutedTicker ANT

What happened. Anthropic priced 980 million Class A shares at $83 on Wednesday evening, above the $70–78 range it set last week. It had planned to sell 825 million. About 956.4 million shares come from the company and about 23.6 million from its seven co-founders, who each sold 3.38 million shares (Anthropic, CNBC). Underwriters have a 30-day option on another 147 million shares. Trading starts today on the Nasdaq Global Select Market, and the offering is expected to close Friday, November 20 (Anthropic).

The record. The $81.3 billion raised tops SpaceX's June IPO, which raised $75 billion initially and $85.7 billion after underwriters exercised their extra-share option (CNBC). Bloomberg reports a combined $107.3 billion when a private placement alongside the IPO is included. A group of tech companies including Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and AMD agreed to invest $26 billion together at the IPO price, and Amazon's and Google's existing stakes are each worth more than $100 billion (CNBC). The roughly $1.5 trillion valuation compares with $965 billion in Anthropic's May private round and is below the $2 trillion it was reportedly aiming for months ago (TechCrunch).

The business behind it. Revenue for the nine months to September 30 was $33.24 billion, up about 1,200%, and the company says run-rate revenue reached about $81 billion at the end of October. It reported a $104 billion net loss for those nine months, mostly from fair-value changes on convertible notes and derivatives, and its prospectus lists more than 90 pages of risk factors (CNBC). OpenAI has confidentially filed for an IPO and has said a debut is likely in 2027.

Why it matters (analysis)This is the first frontier AI lab to list, and it comes in a rough week for AI stocks: Nvidia's CEO departure, a software selloff and rising rates. Pricing above the range shows strong institutional demand; today's trading will show whether that extends to the broader market. The circularity is worth noting too: some of the biggest buyers, like Google, Amazon, Nvidia and AMD, are also Anthropic's suppliers of compute, so its results now flow directly into theirs and into index funds once it becomes eligible.

Monetary policy · AI financing

Fed minutes flag opaque AI debt as Nvidia narrows what its data-center guarantees cover

Federal Reserve · CNBC · Yahoo Finance · TechCrunch · Axios · Nov 17–18
Funds rate 3.75–4.00%Oct vote 10–2 to holdNvidia Q3 net income $64.9B

The minutes. At the October 27–28 meeting, "almost all participants" judged that further rate increases "would likely be appropriate" to bring inflation back to 2%. The committee held at 3.75–4.00% by a 10–2 vote, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissenting in favor of a quarter-point hike, and "a couple" of participants raised the possibility of a larger move in December (Federal Reserve, CNBC). Governor Christopher Waller told CNBC on Wednesday the Fed should consider a half-point hike in December. Officials still expect inflation to fall sharply in 2027 and beyond.

The AI angle. Officials called AI-related demand "a material source of inflation risk." Staff warned that "off-balance-sheet leasing commitments and circular financing arrangements among AI-related firms could amplify losses if returns on AI investment were to disappoint," and "most participants" noted that AI-related debt had grown rapidly through "complex and opaque financing structures," with exposures concentrating in a few firms (minutes via CNBC).

Nvidia's clarification. In its CFO commentary and Tuesday's call, interim CEO Colette Kress said Nvidia's loan guarantees for neoclouds and data-center projects only take effect after the customer has raised its own financing, built the facility and installed Nvidia equipment; if a project is delayed or fails before that, "our guarantee doesn't exist anymore." She cited OpenAI's Ohio campus, backed by a $105 billion guarantee, as an example, said this is a clarification rather than a policy change, and said Nvidia will be "far more selective" in future while prioritizing projects with land, power and buildings in place (TechCrunch, Axios). TechCrunch notes these terms had not been disclosed publicly before.

Why it matters (analysis)The two items speak to the same question: who carries the risk if AI data-center spending outruns returns. The Fed is signaling higher rates and naming AI financing as a stability concern; Nvidia, the biggest single backstop, is signaling that its support shifts construction risk back to builders and lenders. For heavily leveraged data-center developers, that combination means costlier and more conditional money. Supporters of the spending point to Nvidia's 103% revenue growth and Anthropic's demand as evidence that the returns are real.

Google releases Gemini 4 Neon, a mid-tier model aimed at fast agent work at lower cost

$3 / $15 per M tokens1M-token context

Neon sits between Gemini 4 Carbon, Google's largest model, and the fast Hydrogen. Google prices it at $3 per million input tokens and $15 per million output, with cached input at $0.15, and says it is built for long, tool-heavy agent sessions, including acting as an orchestrator directing parallel Hydrogen subagents in its Antigravity coding environment. Google cites third-party rankings: third overall on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its highest thinking level, first on that firm's coding-agent index, and second on the Vals Index. Those placements come from Google's announcement, and some benchmarks quoted are run in Google's own harness. It is rolling out in the Gemini API, Gemini for business, and to paid Pro and Ultra subscribers; a cybersecurity variant goes to vetted defenders in the coming days. Google also opened its Genie 4 world model in Project Genie.

Palo Alto Networks · CNBC · Ctech · Nov 18

Palo Alto Networks will buy Applied Compute for $4.5 billion to secure companies' own open-weight models

The all-stock deal brings in a platform for post-training and serving open-weight AI models, which companies tune on their own data and run in public or sovereign clouds. Palo Alto argues most enterprises will run a mix of open and closed models and that no large provider currently delivers open models securely; Applied Compute CEO Yash Patil becomes co-chief AI officer at close, expected in Palo Alto's fiscal third quarter of 2027 (company release). The deal came with fiscal Q1 results: revenue of $3.44 billion, up 39%, adjusted EPS of $1.05 against $0.97 expected, and a raised 2027 outlook. The shares rose 9.9% in regular trading, and CrowdStrike rose about 8% (CNBC, Investing.com).

Agents keep spreading into everyday software: code that improves itself, workflow builders and infrastructure ops

Xinhua · AP · Yahoo Finance · Stock Analysis · CNBC · Nov 18

A proposed Medicare audit rule sank health insurers and oil dragged energy, while the Nasdaq edged up

Energy (−3.51%) and health care (−2.18%) led six falling S&P sectors; consumer discretionary rose 2.24% (Xinhua). Insurers fell for a second day after CMS proposed its "CRUSH" anti-fraud rule on Monday. The part that hit them would let CMS extrapolate findings from sampled Medicare Advantage record audits to a plan's wider payments, starting with 2018 audits; CMS estimates about $9 billion recovered per payment year and nearly $90 billion in savings over 10 years across programs. CMS Administrator Mehmet Oz framed it as protecting taxpayer money; Oppenheimer analysts expect court challenges because a court threw out a similar framework in 2025, and say insurers would likely pass costs into future bids (Stock Analysis, citing Yahoo Finance). Comments are open until January 19, 2027. Oil fell after US crude and fuel inventories rose more than expected, and Exxon and Chevron each lost about 4% (CNBC). Gold and bitcoin both fell more than 3% as bets on Fed hikes built (FXStreet).

Country risk dips below 850 but the gap between official and financial dollars hits its widest in a year

Wholesale $1,559.50 (+0.5%)Blue $1,630 (record)CCL $1,718 · gap 10.2%

JP Morgan's country-risk index fell 8 points to 848 as dollar bonds gained about 0.2%. The MSCI Merval rose 0.2% in pesos to 82,367, but most ADRs fell: YPF −3.6% with oil, Vista −3.8%, and Grupo Galicia −2.6% to $27.78, its lowest since September 2025 (Infobae). The central bank bought $11 million; gross reserves fell $190 million to $49.51 billion on gold's drop. The Brazilian real weakened 1.8% to 5.87 per dollar, its weakest since April 2025. Analysts quoted by Infobae point to more than $20 billion in debt maturities through 2027, with a presidential election in between, as the main question for bonds.

Iran fires at a US base in Kuwait after a US strike on Qeshm, its first attack on Kuwait since September

The EU proposes a €45 billion loan to cover most of Ukraine's 2027 funding gap, as strikes hit Odesa and Crimea

The Commission's plan covers about two-thirds of an estimated €70 billion gap for 2027: up to €25 billion for military aid and €20 billion in budget support tied to reforms. Kyiv urged Trump and Xi to press Putin in Shenzhen (The Independent).
