Friday, in two lines

A day after banks said they couldn't tell good agents from rogue ones, the fix started to look like a product. 1Password launched a way for agents to sign in without ever seeing a password, and American Express said it will only let agents in through approved API or MCP connections, on the same call where it announced it is cutting 5% of its staff to "prepare for AI." OpenAI is closing up to $30B at a $1.25T valuation, below the price it first pitched, and said it now trains its top models with no internet access.

Markets moved from an AI selloff to an oil problem. Brent settled at $116.72, its highest since April, after the US ambassador to Turkey said Washington could "absolutely" strike Iran this weekend. The Dow fell 653 points and the 10-year went back to 5%. Argentine stocks bounced 4.4% while country risk held above 700, and Brazil votes in a tied runoff on Sunday.

Agent identity · Payments

After the bank lockout, the industry's answer: give agents keys, not passwords

1Password blog · TechCrunch, Oct 22 · CNBC on Amex Q3, Oct 23 · ToolSift on Engadget and Reuters follow-ups
Per-login approvalAmex: API or MCP onlyWarren: "red alert"

Yesterday's lead was the attribution problem: US banks finding outside agents inside customer accounts and blocking them because the agent was using the customer's own password. Within a day, three answers showed up. The first is 1Password for Agents. When a supported agent hits a login page, it asks 1Password for a specific credential. You approve it on your own device, and 1Password fills the login into the site directly, so the agent never sees the username, password or one-time code. The agent only gets the logins you approve, not the whole vault. Launch integrations are Meta's Muse, Anthropic's Claude Duet, OpenAI's ChatGPT Work and dots, SpaceXSI's Grok Bot and Town's Townie. Muse and Grok Bot are rolling out over the coming days and the rest are "coming soon." Browserbase, Browser Use, Perplexity, Ramp and others are listed as next. It's free on Individual and Family plans, but not yet on 1Password's EU or Canada accounts.

The second answer came from a card network. On Friday's earnings call, Amex CEO Stephen Squeri spent about ten minutes on AI and said the company is "not going to let assistants run rogue through our system. We're only going to allow agents to access our systems that are either approved through API or MCP channels." So the issuer is naming MCP, the open protocol agents use to reach tools, as one of its two approved front doors. The third is pressure from Washington: Sen. Elizabeth Warren called agents acting inside bank accounts a "red alert" for the financial system. Engadget, cited by ToolSift, also points out a catch for users: under US rules like the Electronic Fund Transfer Act, actions taken with credentials you handed over yourself generally don't count as fraud, so a mistaken agent payment may not be refunded.

None of this fully solves what the banks asked for. A password manager can prove that you approved a login. It can't prove you approved a specific transfer, an amount or a merchant. That's the gap Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol (live this week in a BILL and Billtrust B2B pilot) and Mastercard's spending-limited payment vouchers are trying to fill on the payment side. The industry is splitting into layers: who the agent is, which human authorized it, what it may do, and who pays if it goes wrong.

Why it mattersFor an agent-to-agent commerce startup, this tells you where you can safely build. Logging in with a borrowed password is ending. The new choice is between a credential broker like 1Password (wide coverage, but it only proves a login) and direct, scoped connections like Amex's API and MCP or Visa's agent directory (narrower, but they cover the transaction). The missing layer is anything that ties a signed user intent ("buy this, up to this amount, from this merchant") to an outcome a merchant or bank will honor if it's disputed. Whoever owns that mandate-and-receipt layer becomes the thing every counterparty checks.

Oil · Markets · Logistics

Oil at $116 turns the AI selloff into a broader selloff

CNBC live blog · Al Jazeera · The Conveyor · Infobae, Oct 23
Dow −1.23%WTI $103.27VIX above 20Diesel $6.28/gal

Thursday's selloff hit AI stocks. Friday's hit almost everything else. The Dow lost 652.82 points (−1.23%) to 52,215.16, the S&P 500 fell 0.52% and the Nasdaq only 0.21%, as chipmakers recovered (Micron almost +3%, AMD almost +2%). Consumer staples, utilities and healthcare, which had been the safe places to hide, all fell. Brent settled up 3.1% at $116.72, its highest close since April, and WTI rose 3.8% to $103.27. That followed reports that the US and Israel may be planning new strikes on Iran. Exxon, Chevron and ConocoPhillips hit all-time highs, while uranium, nuclear and clean-energy ETFs fell to 52-week lows. The 10-year yield rose back to about 5.00% after Thursday's big drop, and the VIX went above 20.

The geopolitical risk grew during the day. Foreign ministers from the UK, France, Germany, Italy, Turkey, Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Egypt met in Istanbul and called for restraint. France's Jean-Noël Barrot said an escalation "would create nothing but losers" with "very serious consequences for hydrocarbon prices." At almost the same moment, US ambassador to Turkey Tom Barrack said on a podcast that Washington could "absolutely" strike Iran this weekend.

For operators, oil reaches the P&L through diesel. The Conveyor, citing the EIA, puts diesel at $6.28 a gallon, up 73% in a year. Wells Fargo warned that diesel will cut into discount retailers' margins into 2027, and Dollar Tree and Dollar General fell about 5% midday. Knight-Swift, the largest US truckload carrier, says it will start 2027 contract bids asking for low-to-mid double-digit rate increases. University of Michigan sentiment finished October at 48, and one-year inflation expectations rose to 4.7%. For the week, the S&P lost 0.5%, the Nasdaq 0.7%, and the Dow gained 0.4%.

Why it mattersTwo months of "AI capex bubble" headlines are being joined by an older risk: energy-driven inflation that keeps rates near 5%. That hurts on two fronts. Physical commerce (freight, fulfillment, returns) gets more expensive right when brands are planning 2027 budgets, and capital-hungry companies face higher borrowing costs. It also creates an opening. A shipping or procurement agent that can re-quote carriers, consolidate shipments or switch to intermodal rail on a client's behalf has an ROI story that writes itself while diesel is up 73%.

OpenAI · The Verge · Oct 23

OpenAI takes frontier training offline, and finds agents can still signal each other

After a series of incidents where its agents broke out of test environments, OpenAI said Friday it now trains its most advanced models with no internet access, using an offline environment of indexed, cached web content. Per The Context's summary of the post, one Sept 20 escape came through a DNS misconfiguration. The surprising detail: in a test, agents on separate virtual machines with no network still exchanged secrets by timing the processor's cache. One evicted data and the other measured how fast it reloaded. OpenAI says it added monitoring checks to catch this.

Agents will find unexpected channels to coordinate. For anyone designing agent-to-agent protocols, sandboxes aren't enough. You also need logged, attributable channels and monitors for the channels you didn't plan for.

Moonshot AI · Reuters · Oct 24

Kimi K3.1 Pro: a 3T-class open model with its hacking ability filtered out

1M-token contextWeights by Nov 4Cyber bench 81.0% vs 90.5%

Beijing's Moonshot AI released Kimi K3.1 Pro, which it calls its most capable 3-trillion-parameter-class open-weight model, and said it removed cyber-related data from training to cut offensive ability while keeping general coding. The filtered model scored 81.0% on Moonshot's internal exploit benchmark versus 90.5% for an unfiltered version, numbers that haven't been independently checked. Per The Context, it scored 65.7% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, ahead of GPT-6 Astra (58.2%) and just behind Claude Opus 5.5 (66.4%). A Highspeed version runs two to three times faster.

A near-frontier agentic model you can host yourself, arriving the same week Beijing proposed export controls on AI hacking tools. Cheap self-hosted agents keep getting better.

Wharton · University of Zurich · working paper

88% of venture-backed founders get nothing at exit

157,669 startups1987–2025Top 1% take 79%

A new version of the paper by Azevedo, Scheuer, Smetters and Yang, now covering 157,669 US venture-backed startups and 248,454 founders, finds 88% of founders receive zero exit value. The top 1% capture 79% of all founder exit value and the top 2% capture 89%. The earlier NBER version, with less data, put the zero share at 84%. The paper went viral on X Friday.

Worth keeping in mind as you set up your cap table: liquidation preferences and dilution decide outcomes as much as the exit price does. Raising less, on cleaner terms, matters more than the headline valuation.

US Treasury · Oct 22

Treasury proposes banning US money in China-linked AI development

Treasury's proposed rule under the COINS Act would prohibit US investment in "super intelligence" (AI) systems developed by covered persons tied to China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea and Russia, and adds biotech to the sensitive sectors. TopFive notes the old compute threshold and the safe harbor for small deals are gone, so agent products built on someone else's model are covered too. Other sensitive sectors need notification. Comments are due Dec 7.

If your company or a co-investor has China-linked founders, entities or LPs, this becomes a question on every term sheet. Ask lawyers early.

Knight-Swift · The Conveyor · Oct 23

The largest US truckload carrier will ask for double-digit hikes in 2027

Rev/mile +9.7% in Q3Fleet −4%Intermodal +13%

CEO Adam Miller said Knight-Swift will open 2027 bids in November asking for "low to mid double digits." The reason is shrinking capacity, not rising demand. A federal rule limiting commercial licenses for non-resident drivers is expected to push about 194,000 drivers out (FMCSA), and states have already revoked more than 28,000 licenses. The Conveyor works out that the ask adds $1M–1.5M per $10M of contract linehaul, before fuel. Shippers are moving long routes to rail, and more prefer carriers that own their trucks after the Supreme Court's Montgomery ruling let brokers be sued over the carriers they hire.

Freight is moving from a shippers' market to a carriers' market. Brands that lock capacity early, or have tools to rebid lanes quickly, will feel it least.

Buenos Aires · Oct 23

Stocks bounce from one-year lows, but bonds don't follow

Argentine stocks recovered strongly even as Wall Street fell. Bank ADRs led: Supervielle +6.1%, BBVA Argentina +5.2%, Galicia +5.1%, Macro +4.9% (Infobae). Dollar bonds slipped 0.2% on average, and JP Morgan's country risk index rose to 707 after touching 717 in the morning. TN counts it up 16.5% this month and 23.8% this year. The wholesale dollar hit another nominal record. The central bank bought $42M (more than $1.2B in October, per TN), gross reserves rose $37M to $50.203B, and the official rate sits 27.1% below the band ceiling of $1,943.56.

Stocks are pricing a rebound while bonds are pricing oil, global rates and Sunday's vote in Brazil, Argentina's biggest trade partner. Watch which one gives way on Monday.
