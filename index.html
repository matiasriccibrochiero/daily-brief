The AI selloff becomes a rout: S&P 500 and Dow in correction, Nasdaq negative for the year

CNBC · Reuters · Portfolio Terminal · SAAM Capital · Becerra Bursátil · Stock Analysis · Nov 20–21
VIX 35.89NVDA −14.0%Chip index −17.1% this week10Y 4.92%

The session. The S&P 500 fell 265.44 points (3.70%) to 6,910.39. It was the index's seventh straight loss and its lowest close since April 13, and left it 11.6% below its October 6 record (Portfolio Terminal). The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% to 22,617.73, which puts it down 2.7% for the year. The Dow lost 949.73 points (1.93%) to 48,147.33, and the Russell 2000 fell 2.76%. CNBC reports that the Nasdaq-100 dropped 5.6% and was briefly in bear-market territory during the day, meaning 20% below its high. The VIX volatility index closed at 35.89, up 26% and close to 40 at its intraday peak. Consumer staples were the only sector to rise: Walmart, Costco, Procter & Gamble and Johnson & Johnson each gained about 1% (CNBC, SAAM).

What hit chips. Three things came together. First, Reuters, citing two people briefed on the matter, reported that SEC enforcement staff are examining possible accounting issues at Nvidia. The SEC declined to comment, and Nvidia did not respond. Nvidia closed down 14.01% (Becerra Bursátil), its seventh straight weekly decline and its longest losing streak since 2008. Second, analyst Ming-Chi Kuo said Google had cut its 2027 orders for Broadcom-designed TPU 8i chips by about 1 million units, roughly 20%. Neither company commented, and Broadcom fell about 12%. Third, AMD and Micron each fell about 13–14%. Becerra Bursátil reports the semiconductor index fell 10.7% on the day and 17.1% for the week, its worst week since 2001.

Anthropic's second day. ANT opened at $89.86, below Thursday's low. It fell as far as $83.05, just five cents above its $83 IPO price, and closed at $88.91, down 8.5%. Volume was about 400 million shares (Yahoo Finance, MarketBeat). CNBC notes that its 17% first-day gain was smaller than the debuts of Circle, Figma, Cerebras and SpaceX. Seaport Research started coverage at neutral, saying the stock asks for "an incredible leap of faith."

Policy noise on top. At a White House press conference, President Trump said US companies building data centers abroad would face a "tremendous tax," and added that he had not yet told his staff (see AI). Fed Governor Lisa Cook warned that waiting longer to raise rates "risks getting behind the curve." Markets still expect a 25 bp hike at the Dec 8–9 meeting. Treasuries rallied anyway: the 10-year yield fell about 12–13 bp to 4.92%. The University of Michigan's final November survey showed year-ahead inflation expectations dropping a full point to 3.7%.

Why it matters (analysis)Thursday's drop could be read as profit-taking after the Anthropic IPO. Friday's moves were bigger and more specific. The losses centered on the companies that supply AI hardware: Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD and Micron. They followed a regulatory report on the sector's largest company and a reported cut in orders from one of its biggest buyers. Views on what comes next differ. BTIG's Jonathan Krinsky says durable market bottoms usually come after much weaker breadth and a deeper fall in Apple, which is only about 3% off its high, so he thinks more downside is likely. Capital Economics expects the S&P 500 at 6,500 by end-2027. Others note that Treasuries did their job as a hedge and inflation expectations eased. Next week is shortened by Thanksgiving, which usually means thin trading. None of Friday's reports had been confirmed by the companies involved.

Geopolitics · Europe

Russian missiles land in Poland during Europe's first Ukraine-force drill, as US envoys push a peace track

France 24 · Reuters · AFP · Axios · Ukrainska Pravda · BBC · Nov 20–21
2 missiles + 1 droneNo casualtiesRutte in Warsaw Sat

What happened. Early Friday, while Russia carried out a large strike on Ukraine, two Russian missiles and a suspected jet-powered drone came down in southeastern Poland. One Kh-101 cruise missile was confirmed in the village of Nowy Lipowiec. Nobody was hurt, and there was little or no damage (France 24, Polish prosecutors). Several thousand troops from the European "Coalition of the Willing" were nearby at Nowa Dęba, holding the first exercise of a planned multinational force for postwar Ukraine. Russian strikes killed at least eight people in Ukraine that day (Reuters).

How it was read. Prime Minister Donald Tusk said "this does not look like a coincidence" and called it "an intentional provocation" tied to the drills. He also said he does not always agree with the US interpretation of such incidents, and that US personnel have access to the debris. A day earlier, Trump had told Fox News that Putin "has been landing drones in Poland, and a couple of little missiles, by mistake." The UK, Germany, France, the EU and NATO's Mark Rutte condemned the incursion. Rutte is due in Warsaw on Saturday. As of France 24's last update, the Kremlin had not commented.

The diplomatic track. Calling in to Thursday's Berlin meeting, Trump told European leaders that "Putin wants to end the war" and urged Germany's Merz to phone Putin directly (Axios). Putin said for the first time that Europeans could take part in peace talks and that he would present an updated European security proposal. Moscow has also revived its 2021 demand that NATO pull forces back from Eastern Europe, Poland included. After Trump called Putin a friend, Zelensky said: "For us, he is an enemy and a killer" (Ukrainska Pravda, BBC). Another round with the Russians could take place next week, and a European official told Axios the group doubts Russia will actually allow Europe in.

Why it matters (analysis)The incident tested the main assumption behind Washington's current approach: that Moscow is ready to deal. To Warsaw and most European capitals, missiles landing near a drill for a postwar Ukraine force look like pressure, not goodwill. The White House had called similar earlier incidents mistakes. Tusk is using the episode to argue that Poland, one of five European countries picked for future US-led talks, should be at the table. What the US says after examining the debris, and whether Russia lets Europe into the next round, will show how far apart the two readings are.

Reuters: the SEC's Nvidia probe is looking at its accounting

SEC declined comment10-Q delayedReceivables 64 days

On Tuesday the Wall Street Journal reported that the SEC was investigating Nvidia, including whether its disclosures misled investors. Reuters now reports, citing two people briefed on the matter, that enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues. Neither outlet has said which issues. Several possible ones are already public. Nvidia said its detailed quarterly filing (10-Q) would come "in the coming weeks" rather than with earnings as usual, and its release mentioned an independent board committee investigation. Customers are taking 64 days to pay, the longest since 2022. In 2025 it stopped reporting Singapore revenue separately, around the time it blocked sales to Singapore-based Megaspeed, a customer US officials suspect of rerouting chips to China (Yahoo Finance, Fortune). Newsquawk notes that an inquiry of this kind is short of a formal order of investigation and well short of an enforcement action. Separately, Trump said Nvidia should let ousted CEO Jensen Huang return after "a great apology." The board has already ruled that out.

Why it matters: Nvidia sits at the center of AI spending. It has fallen about 46% over seven weeks, erasing roughly $2.6 trillion in market value (Becerra Bursátil). The 10-Q is now the key document to watch.

CNBC · AP · Reuters · Nov 20

Trump says US companies that build data centers abroad will face a "tremendous tax"

At a White House press conference, Trump said US companies would be stopped from building data centers in other countries and that "they can, but we're going to put a tremendous tax on them." He gave no mechanism, rate or timeline, and said: "I haven't even said that to my people. But I'm telling them now." Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta and Oracle already run data centers abroad, often so they can serve customers locally or meet data-residency rules. None had commented when CNBC published. CNBC also notes its own survey finding that 64% of registered voters oppose a data center within 10 miles of their home, and only 13% support one.

Why it matters: Any measure like this would cut against the main growth path for cloud providers. It is a statement for now, not a policy, but it added to Friday's pressure on AI infrastructure stocks.

Ming-Chi Kuo (analyst survey) · Stock Analysis · 24/7 Wall St. · Nov 20

Analyst: Google trims 2027 Broadcom TPU orders by about 20% and shifts volume to MediaTek

TF International's Ming-Chi Kuo said his latest industry survey shows Google cutting 2027 shipments of the Broadcom-designed TPU 8i by about 1 million units, roughly 20%. Most of the cut falls on the pricier 8i Max ("Whalefish"). Google would ship more of the MediaTek-designed TPU 8t instead, though Kuo said the details are not final. He connects the shift to Google prioritizing compute for frontier-model training while supply chains are tight and data-center power is limited. Neither company has commented. In September, Broadcom CEO Hock Tan had said "our engagement with Google has never been stronger." Broadcom reports earnings on December 9.

Why it matters: custom AI chips have been Broadcom's main growth story. This shows how a change in one hyperscaler's mix can move a company worth hundreds of billions of dollars. 24/7 Wall St. estimates Broadcom lost $191 billion in market value on the day.

LA–Long Beach watchmen's contract expires with no deal; no strike reported yet

~250 watchmenExpired 05:01 ART Sat85% of Long Beach cargo110+ trade groups urge a deal

Follow-up from yesterday. ILWU Local 26's extension ran out at 12:01 a.m. Pacific on Saturday after a final Friday session between the union and the Pacific Maritime Association produced no agreement (Splash247 via Maritime News). As of the latest reports this brief could find, no strike had formally begun and port operations had not been suspended. The union has a strike authorization and had said it would not extend again. More than 110 US trade associations issued a joint statement Friday urging both sides to keep bargaining, warning that any backlog could take weeks or months to clear. The two sides still disagree on the basic facts. The PMA says its offer raises average full-time pay from more than $170,000 to $198,000. Local 26 president Luisa Gratz calls those figures inaccurate and says the employers ignored the union's counterproposal (Seatrade).

Why it matters: the 250 watchmen work at six terminals that handle about 85% of Long Beach's cargo and 33% of Los Angeles's. If longshore workers honor a picket line, as the PMA expects, those terminals stop during the last weeks of holiday imports. Whether pickets go up is the thing to watch this weekend.

Transportation Insight · FreightWaves · Nov 18–20

Peak freight: rates firm before Thanksgiving, and all three parcel carriers set 2027 increases near 6%

UPS / FedEx +5.9%Amazon +6.1%Rejections ~12.7%

Truckload tender rejections, the share of contracted loads carriers turn down, are holding near 12.7%, more than double the level at this point in 2023 (Transportation Insight). Van costs are rising even as fuel eases, which points to demand absorbing the fuel savings. The week after Thanksgiving has historically brought the sharpest rate pressure of peak season. UPS and FedEx have each set 5.9% general rate increases for 2027, and Amazon 6.1%, with packages of 1–5 lb rising closer to 6.5%. Both UPS and FedEx also adjusted their fuel-surcharge tables. Nearly all LTL (less-than-truckload) carriers close on Thanksgiving, and shippers are advised to tender freight by Tuesday, November 24. UPS's guaranteed Christmas ground cutoff is around December 17.

Why it matters: a 6% increase that is steeper for light packages falls hardest on small-parcel e-commerce shippers, and comes just as capacity tightens for the holidays.

Super-hot rock geothermal gets its first hyperscaler deal: Google to pay over $500M for 50 MW from Quaise

Quaise Energy says Google will pay more than $500 million for 50 megawatts from a planned project in central Oregon that taps rock about three miles down. At those depths water becomes supercritical, and one well could produce 15 MW or more. Below about 2.5 miles, Quaise plans to replace conventional drill bits with a gyrotron that vaporizes rock using millimeter-wave radiation. Rival Mazama Energy (Khosla, Gates) instead cools conventional bits with supercritical CO₂. Its CEO says its second well was drilled 80% faster than its first, and the cooling system cut drilling time by another 20%. Quaise has raised more than $270 million and Mazama $171 million (PitchBook via TechCrunch).

Why it matters: power is the hard limit on new data centers. Google's premium price is a bet on an energy source that hasn't been proven commercially. The technology risk is real.

Becerra Bursátil · Infobae · Perfil · Nov 20

Country risk tops 1,000 for the first time in 13 months as the dollar-bond curve inverts

Hard-dollar bonds fell another 3.8–4.6%: AL30 −4.56% to US$47.50 and GD30 −4.18% to US$49.27. Becerra Bursátil reports that short-dated bonds now yield 3.8–4.8 points more than long ones. When the curve inverts like this, investors are pricing bonds on what they would recover in a default, and doubts center on the 2027 repayment window, about US$24.9 billion in maturities under the official financing program. Country risk closed at 1,071 (Infobae: 1,070, +135), up from 402 on July 10. The central bank (BCRA) bought nothing in the FX market, and gross reserves fell US$81 million to US$49,362 million (Infobae). The pressure came despite strong trade data: October exports hit a record US$10,061 million (+26.4% y/y), with a US$3,394 million trade surplus (INDEC via Becerra).

The two readings. The government blames the oil shock and election uncertainty. Earlier in the week Economy Minister Caputo said: "No hay riesgo Milei. El riesgo país sube por el temor a que no esté Milei." Milei called talk of devaluation "una tontería" (Infobae). Consultancy 1816 notes that Milei's approval has barely moved since July while country risk has doubled, which supports the election-fear explanation. Market analysts quoted by Infobae point to local weaknesses as well: how the BCRA will build reserves, how 2027 will be financed, and economic activity down 1.7% y/y. Infobae adds that some officials see two new problems: a Supreme Court ruling striking down the prepaid-health articles of DNU 70/23, and pressure in Congress for more spending.

UN News · ICJ · AP · Nov 19

The world court finds a state responsible for genocide for the first time: Myanmar, against the Rohingya

In The Gambia v. Myanmar, the International Court of Justice ruled that Myanmar's security forces committed genocide against the Rohingya during the 2017 "clearance operations" in northern Rakhine, and that Myanmar failed to prevent and punish it. The court cited an estimated 10,000 killed, some 700,000 who fled to Bangladesh, and nearly 400 villages partly or fully destroyed. It ordered Myanmar to pass genocide legislation, investigate and prosecute suspects, guarantee it will not happen again, allow safe and voluntary return, and compensate victims. Myanmar must report on restitution within two years. The ruling is final and cannot be appealed. UN Secretary-General Guterres called it "a powerful recognition of the Rohingya victims." The court has no enforcement power of its own, and compliance by Myanmar's military government is the open question.

GOV.UK joint statement · Consilium · Nov 20

EU and UK close deals on food checks, carbon-market linking and a youth mobility scheme

At their second summit, in Brussels, the EU and UK concluded negotiations on three deals. A common sanitary and phytosanitary (SPS) area would remove most checks and certificates on food, animal and plant shipments from March 1, 2028. Their emissions-trading systems would be linked from January 1, 2028, so allowances can be used in either. A Youth Experience Scheme would let 100,000 people aged 18–31 from each side live, work or study in the other for up to three years, with first travel expected by January 2030. They also agreed to coordinate on supply chains such as steel and EVs. All three deals still need formal approval and ratification.
