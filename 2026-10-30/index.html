Markets · Big Tech

Alphabet's worst day ever, then Amazon and Intel: the AI-spending sell-off widens

CNBC · Reuters · Amazon · Intel · Investing.com · Oct 29–30
GOOGL −12.64%NOW −26.95%AMZN capex $77.3BINTC ~−25% pre

Alphabet closed down 12.64% at $283.82 on Thursday, the largest one-day drop since its 2004 listing, after Wednesday's earnings miss and a negative $9.07 billion free cash flow. Investing.com puts the loss at more than half a trillion dollars of market value. ServiceNow had its worst day ever, falling about 27%: revenue beat, but adjusted EPS of $1.00 missed by two cents and its subscription-backlog guidance disappointed, even as it reported production agentic-AI usage up 13-fold (CNBC, Investing.com). Caterpillar fell 9.25%, its worst day since March 2020, despite a record $78.1 billion backlog. The Nasdaq lost 1.38%; the S&P 500 fell 0.3% and snapped a three-day run.

Amazon reported after the close. AWS revenue grew 39% to $46.0 billion, its fastest in nearly five years, and the company posted its highest-ever operating margin, 13.9%. But quarterly capex of $77.3 billion was roughly $19–20 billion above estimates (FactSet, LSEG), trailing-12-month free cash flow swung to a $42.4 billion outflow, and both revenue ($201.9 billion) and EPS ($1.89) missed. CEO Andy Jassy said 2027 capex will be "meaningfully more" than this year's $220 billion and that AWS growth should exceed 40% in 2027 and 2028. Shares fell about 7% after hours.

Intel guided fourth-quarter revenue to $15–16 billion, below the $17.07 billion consensus, and said PC weakness, driven by an AI-led memory shortage that has pushed up PC costs, could last through the first half of 2027. It also said the deadly October explosion at its Kiryat Gat plant in Israel will cost about $600 million of revenue over two quarters, and that it will not generate positive cash flow this year. Third-quarter revenue of $16.51 billion was in line and EPS beat. Chip stocks including Nvidia, AMD and Micron fell 1–6% after hours (Reuters).

The two readings. Seaport's Jay Goldberg told Reuters the market entered earnings season pessimistic: "No result is good enough, and bad results receive extra punishment." Jassy argued the opposite case for Amazon: "Our strategy is working," and AWS "could reach a trillion dollars" in revenue in as little as five years. Microsoft's $25 billion bond sale means all the US hyperscalers have now tapped debt markets for the build-out (Investing.com), and Treasuries rallied as money left tech.

Why it matters (analysis)This is the first earnings season in which every large AI spender was judged primarily on cash flow, and three of the four that reported this week fell sharply on it. The pressure now runs in two directions: down the supply chain, where Intel's warning shows the memory crunch the AI build-out created is hurting PCs, and across software, where ServiceNow's fall reflects a fear that AI agents erode seat-based businesses. If the spenders respond by leaning more on debt, as Microsoft just did, the cost and pace of the build-out become more exposed to bond markets.

Crypto · Security

Binance says hackers stole $6.3 billion, the largest crypto theft on record

Binance · BBC · CNBC · Al Jazeera · Investing.com · Oct 29–30
$6.3B affected~$3B frozenWithdrawals pausedBTC ~$78K

Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange, suspended all withdrawals after what it says was the theft of about $6.3 billion in digital assets. Stablecoin issuers Tether and Circle froze tokens tied to the attack, and roughly half of the stolen value is now frozen on-chain (CNBC, Investing.com). The BBC reports Binance has found multiple "points of breach." The company says it will cover all losses from its SAFU insurance fund and company money, that "user funds are safe," and that withdrawals should resume within about 48 hours, by Sunday.

Analysts cited by Investing.com suggested the North Korea-linked group known as TraderTraitor may be responsible; that attribution is unconfirmed. Bitcoin fell about 2.6% to around $78,000 and briefly dropped below $77,000 for the first time in over a month.

Why it matters (analysis)The theft is more than four times larger than the biggest previous exchange hacks, and it hit the venue that handles the most crypto trading volume. Two things will shape the fallout: whether Binance reopens withdrawals on schedule and covers losses in full, and whether stablecoin issuers' ability to freeze funds, which limited the damage here, becomes a bigger part of how regulators think about crypto security.

Anthropic launches Claude Duet, a proactive personal agent, against Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots

Research previewUS Pro and Max, waitlistClaude Opus 5.5

Claude Duet watches the services a user connects, sends morning briefings and works on tasks in the background, and asks for approval before sensitive actions such as sending messages, making payments, signing documents or deleting things. It runs on Claude Opus 5.5, comes as a standalone iOS and Android app plus claude.ai, and draws on the same usage limits as a user's plan (Anthropic). The Verge frames it as Anthropic's answer to Meta's Muse; CNBC previewed it last week alongside OpenAI's Dots.

All three big consumer AI labs now have an always-on personal agent. The approval gate on payments and messages is the main design difference to watch, since it decides how much these agents can do unattended.

Agent startups keep raising: Serval at $3B, Casap, Broccoli, Rocker, and a Replika founder's Wabi at $1.25B

Serval raised a $150 million Series C led by Sequoia at a $3 billion valuation; staff describe a workflow in plain language and its agents write the automation, starting with IT requests. Private money is still flowing into agent software on the same day ServiceNow, the incumbent in IT service management, had its worst trading day ever. Serval competes directly in that category.

DoorDash · Instacart · MLex · Invested Alpha · Oct 29

DoorDash confirms it will buy Instacart for $17 billion, with a $1.25B break fee if antitrust blocks it

$65 per share26% premiumClose end-2027 to 1H 2028

DoorDash agreed to acquire Instacart (Maplebear) for $65 a share, about $17 billion in equity value and a 26% premium to Instacart's price before the first reports. At least 75% will be paid in cash, funded partly by a JPMorgan bridge loan. The companies pitch it as giving local grocers and retailers "the kinds of innovative technology that the biggest online retailers" build: Instacart users would gain access to more than 50,000 additional stores, and DashPass and Instacart+ would merge into one membership. Reviews are expected in the US, Canada and Israel; DoorDash owes $1.25 billion if antitrust stops the deal, and Instacart owes $650 million if it walks for a better offer (MLex). Holders of about 19% of Instacart have agreed to vote yes.

The long timeline signals the companies expect a deep antitrust review. DoorDash rose 5.6%; Instacart fell 2.2% to $57.11, about 12% below the offer, a gap that reflects the market's doubts about approval and the wait.

Amazon's shipping bill rises 11% on fuel, while online store sales grow just 2%

Shipping $28.3B1M+ robotsOnline stores +2%

Amazon's worldwide shipping costs rose 11.5% to $28.3 billion, against 12% unit growth; excluding fuel and linehaul inflation they rose 8%. Jassy said fuel and linehaul pressure will be "somewhat higher" in the fourth quarter and that the FBA fuel surcharge introduced this year does not fully offset it. The company still expects to cut cost-to-serve per unit for a fourth straight year, helped by more than 1 million robots and a doubled fleet of robotic arms (FreightWaves). Online store sales grew only about 2%, and ads 11%, partly because Prime Day moved into the second quarter this year (Investing.com).

Fuel-driven shipping inflation is hitting the largest, most automated e-commerce network too. Separately, Delta Cargo and SmartKargo launched DeliverDirect, a 1–3-day e-commerce parcel service using passenger-plane belly space.

ECB · CNBC · Bank of Japan · Nikkei Asia · Oct 29–30

ECB holds and Lagarde announces her exit; the Bank of Japan declares its inflation target met

ECB deposit 2.50%Euro inflation 3.8%BoJ 1.25%, 8–1

The ECB kept its deposit rate at 2.50% after September's hike. Lagarde said the Governing Council "debated whether a hike was appropriate" but unanimously chose to wait, as energy pushes euro-area inflation to 3.8% and long-term borrowing costs climb; money markets price about a 67% chance of a December hike (CNBC, LSEG). Earlier in the day the ECB announced that Lagarde will step down on Feb. 7, 2027, before her term ends; EU leaders will choose a successor. On Friday the Bank of Japan held at 1.25%, the highest since 1995, with board member Hajime Takata voting for 1.5%. For the first time since adopting the target in 2013, it said underlying inflation is "generally consistent" with 2%, and it raised its fiscal-2027 inflation forecast to 2.6% (CNBC, Nikkei Asia). It also sped up sales of its ETF holdings.

Capital Economics read the BoJ statement as declaring victory over deflation, which opens the door to further hikes. The ECB succession adds uncertainty at a moment when it is deciding whether to keep tightening.

Ukraine: Russian drone kills 15 at Dnipro's main station while the Oleshky truce holds

A Russian jet-powered drone hit Dnipro's main railway station on Thursday, killing at least 15 people and wounding at least 14, including an 11-year-old (Ukrainska Pravda, BBC). Other strikes killed at least six people elsewhere. In the south, the local ceasefire around occupied Oleshky held, and the NYT reports the city of Kherson across the river has also gone quiet; evacuation corridors for the town's roughly 2,000 residents were still being organised. The WSJ reports Ukraine paused strikes on Russian refineries this month after US pressure, possibly until after the midterms.

The truce shows a narrow humanitarian deal can hold, while the Dnipro strike shows how little it changes the wider war.

Central Elections Committee · Foreign Exchanges · Haaretz · Oct 29

Israel: preliminary count confirms 53–48–19, and Netanyahu presses fraud claims about Arab parties' vote

The Central Elections Committee's preliminary tabulation kept the blocs at 53 for Netanyahu's coalition, 48 for Gadi Eisenkot's opposition and 19 for the two Arab-led parties, with 61 needed. Netanyahu argues the Arab parties' vote was fraudulent; police and election officials have said there is no evidence of fraud. The Shin Bet is reportedly examining claims that a foreign country funded one Arab list, but says it is not seeking to invalidate their vote. Much of Eisenkot's bloc rejects relying on Arab parties, so analysts see a deadlock, with Netanyahu staying on as caretaker and a snap election possible.

Official results are due Nov. 4. Elsewhere: Serbia's winning protest movement is demanding audits and resignations, Venezuela's María Corina Machado says Washington backs elections next year (unconfirmed by the US), and Haiti set its election for July 18, 2027.
